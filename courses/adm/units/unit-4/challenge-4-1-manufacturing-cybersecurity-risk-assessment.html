ADM Unit 4 | Mission Brief 4.1

Manufacturing Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

Identify cyber risks in a connected manufacturing cell and recommend layered safeguards.

Intermediate1-2 class periodsPractice & Review
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Production Specifications

Aegis Aerospace

Department: Operational Technology Security
Goal: Reduce cyber risk in a connected manufacturing workcell.

Background

Engineering Context

A connected aerospace factory experienced unusual robot commands after a maintenance laptop was attached to the network. Production was stopped before damage occurred. Your team must assess the workcell using confidentiality, integrity, and availability principles.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Apply the CIA triad to manufacturing systems
  • Identify technical and human vulnerabilities
  • Prioritize risk using likelihood and impact
  • Recommend layered controls
Equipment

What You Need

  • Workcell/network diagram
  • Risk matrix template
  • Scenario cards
  • Engineering notebook
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Review the provided workcell diagram and identify all devices, connections, and users.
  2. List at least ten assets or data types that require protection.
  3. Identify at least eight vulnerabilities, including one human-factor risk.
  4. For each risk, score likelihood and impact from 1 to 5.
  5. Plot risks on a matrix and select the four highest priorities.
  6. Recommend preventive, detective, and recovery controls for each priority.
  7. Create an incident response sequence for an unexpected robot command.
  8. Present the top risks and explain how each relates to confidentiality, integrity, or availability.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • Identify at least eight distinct vulnerabilities
  • Include risks to safety and production availability
  • Recommend at least two controls for each top risk
  • Include an isolation and recovery procedure
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Do not test real malware or bypass security controls
  • Use only provided diagrams and scenarios
  • Recommendations must preserve authorized maintenance access
  • Distinguish safety stop from cybersecurity response
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Annotated system diagram
  • Risk register and matrix
  • Top-four mitigation plan
  • Incident response flowchart
  • Reflection
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which risk had the highest safety impact?
  2. How can data integrity affect physical quality?
  3. Why are backups not sufficient prevention?
  4. What training would reduce human risk?
Download PDF
Engineering Tip

Use layered defense: one control may fail, so combine access control, monitoring, segmentation, and recovery.

Common Mistake

Cybersecurity is not only confidentiality; altered coordinates can create a physical hazard.

Stretch Goal

Design a network-segmentation diagram separating office, maintenance, and production systems.

Career Connection

Operational technology cybersecurity specialists protect robots, PLCs, sensors, and industrial networks.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
