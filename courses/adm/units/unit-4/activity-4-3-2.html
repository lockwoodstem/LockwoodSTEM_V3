ADM Activity 4.3.2

Aerospace Component Technical Drawing

Create an inspection-ready technical drawing with datums, critical dimensions, tolerances, material, and revision information.

Back to Unit 4Learning StepsStudent TaskWhat You Submit
Focus Question

How does a technical drawing communicate what must be manufactured and inspected?

Design. Verify. Manufacture. Inspect.

Your lesson guide

1. Goal & materials2. Activity3. Check your work4. Report

Objective

Work toward the lesson goal: Aerospace Component Technical Drawing. Use the directions and success criteria in the activity below.

Materials

For the online work: a browser and your engineering notebook. Follow the activity’s materials list for any physical build, and get teacher approval before using equipment.

Plan your time

Suggested work block: 45–60 minutes. Physical builds and projects may span several classes; follow your teacher’s schedule.

Learning targets

Today You Will Learn To

  • Create clear orthographic and isometric views.
  • Dimension the component from functional datums.
  • Identify critical tolerances and inspection methods.
Manufacturing context

Why This Matters

The drawing is the controlled communication document for manufacturing and inspection. It must match the approved CAD revision and clearly identify the features that determine fit and function.

Learning sequence

Learn Before You Build

Complete these steps in order. Record the requested notes or evidence before moving to the graded activity.

10 min

1. Review drawing purpose

Explain how a drawing communicates manufacturing and inspection requirements that may not be obvious from the 3D model.

10–15 min

2. Watch the Fusion drawing overview

Record the purpose of base views, projected views, center marks, hole information, revision tables, and title-block data.

20 min

3. Follow a dimensioning demonstration

Observe how dimensions are organized from functional datums instead of duplicated or chained unnecessarily.

15 min

4. Practice inspection planning

Choose an available measuring tool for each critical dimension before creating the final drawing.

Detailed activity procedure

Create an Inspection-Ready Aerospace Component Drawing

  1. 1
    Open the approved CAD revision and create a new drawing from the design using the assigned standard, sheet size, units, and scale.
  2. 2
    Place the base view in the orientation that best defines the primary machined face.
  3. 3
    Add only the projected, section, detail, or auxiliary views required to define the geometry clearly.
  4. 4
    Add an isometric view for orientation without using it as the primary dimensioning view.
  5. 5
    Add center marks and centerlines for holes, arcs, and symmetric features.
  6. 6
    Identify the primary and secondary datum references used to locate the interfaces.
  7. 7
    Dimension overall size, thickness, hole diameters, hole locations, pocket dimensions, depths, radii, and clearances from the appropriate datums.
  8. 8
    Apply the teacher-provided general tolerance and specific tolerances to critical interface dimensions.
  9. 9
    Add material, finish or deburring note, units, scale, part name, author/team, date, drawing number, and revision.
  10. 10
    Create an inspection-planning table matching each critical dimension with the measurement tool and method.
  11. 11
    Perform a duplicate-dimension, missing-dimension, and manufacturability review.
  12. 12
    Exchange drawings with another team. The reviewer must determine whether the part can be manufactured and inspected without opening the CAD model.
  13. 13
    Resolve review comments, update the revision, and export the approved PDF using the required file name.
Required checkpoints

Stop and Verify Before Moving On

Checkpoint A — Views complete

The selected views fully define the component without unnecessary duplication.

Checkpoint B — Dimensions and tolerances

Interfaces are dimensioned from datums and critical tolerances are identified.

Checkpoint C — Drawing release

Peer review, title block, inspection plan, and PDF export are complete.

Safety and process control

Required Operating Habits

  • Use only teacher-approved files, tools, stock, speeds, feeds, and procedures.
  • Preserve the last verified working file or code version.
  • Stop immediately for unexpected motion, unusual sound, vibration, loose workholding, tool damage, or an unclear condition.
  • Do not operate the CNC mill without the required certification, permission, and supervision.
  • Record failed trials, defects, and interventions honestly.
Quality check

Before You Submit

  • The drawing does not contain missing, duplicate, or conflicting dimensions.
  • Critical features can be measured with available tools.
  • The drawing revision matches the CAD model.
Student deliverables

What You Submit

  • Approved technical drawing PDF or file
  • Critical-dimension and inspection table
  • Peer-review checklist
  • Revision record
