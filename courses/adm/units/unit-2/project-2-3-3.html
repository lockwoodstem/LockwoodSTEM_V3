Optimization Impact Study

Evaluate whether a productivity improvement is truly beneficial when equipment wear, maintenance, downtime, throughput, revenue, and reliability are considered together.

Unit 2 Progression

Sense → Decide → Move → Communicate → Monitor → Optimize → Integrate

Unit 2 applies the programming foundation from Unit 1 to increasingly connected manufacturing systems.

Learning Targets

By the end of this lesson

  • Evaluate a preventive-maintenance schedule using operational data.
  • Compare system behavior before and after a productivity optimization.
  • Calculate maintenance cost, downtime cost, and production value.
  • Create data visualizations and defend an engineering recommendation.
Equipment & Systems

What you will use

  • Optimization Impact Study data set
  • Spreadsheet software
  • Data-visualization tools
  • Maintenance schedule and cost information
Student Workflow

What You Will Do

  1. Analyze the baseline crystal-puller data and identify normal ranges, maintenance events, and warning patterns.
  2. Evaluate whether the preventive-maintenance schedule is appropriate.
  3. Analyze the post-optimization data and compare wear, yield, throughput, and failure indicators.
  4. Calculate relevant maintenance, downtime, and production values.
  5. Create at least one useful visualization and develop a predictive-maintenance recommendation.
  6. Decide whether the optimization should be adopted and defend the decision with evidence.
Programming Focus

How coding supports the lesson

This project is primarily a spreadsheet and data-analysis task. Use AI tools only as directed by your instructor; calculations, visualizations, and recommendations must be supported by the data.

What You Will Submit

What You Submit

  • Completed before / after data analysis
  • Cost, downtime, throughput, and yield calculations
  • At least one decision-support visualization
  • Predictive-maintenance recommendation
  • Final adopt / do-not-adopt engineering recommendation
