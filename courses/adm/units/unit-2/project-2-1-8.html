ADM Unit 2 • Project 2.1.8

AI-Powered Sorting Machine

Engineer a custom automated sorting machine that recognizes parts with AI vision and physically routes them into the correct collection bins.

LEGO or hardware • Custom fabrication • Actual AI inference and automation

Teams of 2–3 • 7 class periods • 60 points

Engineering design brief

Your Mission

A manufacturer needs a compact sorter for mixed small parts. Build a custom feeder, camera station, diverter and collection system that uses a trainable image-classification model to identify each part. The program must automatically actuate the mechanism to deliver parts to different bins. Use teacher-approved cameras, low-voltage electronics, motors and classroom fabrication tools. Do not simply repurpose a complete VEX CTE Workcell sorting arrangement.

Choose a product familyClass A + Class B + reject/unknownWorking physical sorter

Option A — LEGO Sorting

Choose two visibly different LEGO part types, such as same-colored 2×2 and 2×4 bricks, or bricks and plates. Identify their shape, type, or size, not just their color.

Account for changing orientation, lighting, shadows, and object feeding.

Option B — Nuts & Bolts

Choose two distinct hardware categories such as nuts versus bolts, or different bolt sizes. Use only approved clean, blunt hardware without sharp points.

Account for reflective surfaces, glare, object overlap, and proper part handling.

AI hardware requirement: The normal VEX EXP AI Vision Sensor has preset recognition models for selected classroom/competition objects, not arbitrary LEGO bricks or nuts and bolts. Use a teacher-approved trainable camera/model system (for example, a webcam and a trained computer-based classifier) and demonstrate how its output reaches a real physical actuator through an approved interface. Confirm the entire camera → classifier → controller → motor route before finishing your CAD. Color thresholds alone, a fixed timer, or manually choosing an output do not count as working AI classification.
Non-negotiable requirements

Design Specifications & Testing

Mechanical / control

  • Custom-built stable frame, object presentation point, camera mount and feed path.
  • Mechanically sort one item at a time to prevent ambiguous detections.
  • Three destinations: Class A, Class B, and reject/unknown.
  • Physical mechanism actuated automatically, e.g. servo diverter, rotary chute, gate or pusher.
  • Enclose pinch points, secure moving parts, keep wiring neat and add a safe stop/recovery procedure.

Real machine learning

  • Collect at least 20 labeled training photos per target class, varying part rotation and lighting.
  • Reserve at least 10 previously unseen validation images per class, excluded from training.
  • Document class labels, prediction/confidence rules, mistaken predictions and a saved/reproducible trained model.
  • Code the model decision to automatically trigger the physical gate/diverter.
  • Unknown or uncertain object → reject bin or a controlled safe stop.
Final performance benchmark: Run at least 30 physical sorting trials using a mixed test batch. Aim for 80% or better correct routing (24 or more correct out of 30), and attempt at least three unknown/ambiguous parts to prove safe rejection. Record real results even if the machine misses the target.
Performance measureRequired evidence
Sorting accuracyCorrect destination / all physical trials × 100%
Classification accuracyCorrect model predictions on held-out image examples
ThroughputNumber sorted / elapsed minutes
Unknown handlingThree unknown/uncertain part trials and their actual outcome
ImprovementOne specific mechanical, lighting, data, or code change and before/after evidence

Safety: Wear safety glasses for fabrication. Use teacher-approved low-voltage power, controllers and actuators. Do not use unapproved mains wiring, sharpened metal, compressed air, or unguarded moving mechanisms. Instructors must approve the hardware/interface plan and powered testing.

7 × 45-minute lessons

Project Timeline

Day 1 • Wed, Nov 11 — Design

Choose parts/classes, define the training method, review available camera/controller equipment, draw two mechanisms, and select an approach.

Checkpoint: sketches, labeled classes, roles, and approved hardware concept.

Day 2 • Thu, Nov 12 — CAD & plan

Develop the base, feeder, camera mount, actuator and bins. Prepare a part list and fabrication files.

Checkpoint: assembly CAD or dimensioned drawings and production list.

Day 3 • Fri, Nov 13 — Train AI

Capture labeled images, train and validate the classifier, and document errors. Save the trained model or reproducible instructions.

Checkpoint: actual AI classification and validation evidence.

Day 4 • Mon, Nov 16 — Build

Fabricate or assemble the frame, feeder, lighting, camera mount, diverter, and bins. Safely confirm actuator movement.

Checkpoint: physical mechanism and controlled actuator test.

Day 5 • Tue, Nov 17 — Integrate

Connect AI output to the controller and sorter. Verify Class A, Class B and reject commands trigger the correct motion.

Checkpoint: first integrated AI-driven routing trial.

Day 6 • Wed, Nov 18 — Optimize

Run 30 physical trials; record destination accuracy, unknown behavior and throughput; identify the largest failure mode and improve one variable.

Checkpoint: trial table, calculations and retest results.

Day 7 • Thu, Nov 19 — Demonstrate

Run a supervised final test with mixed objects. Present results, engineering choices, code, and limitations; turn in the complete technical package.

Due: final project submission and performance review.

Complete project package

What to Submit

  1. Two initial sketches, chosen classes, team roles, and selected-concept justification.
  2. Custom machine CAD/drawings, fabrication files, component list, and photographs of the build.
  3. Training/validation dataset counts, class definitions, saved model or repeatable training procedure, and held-out results.
  4. Commented software showing AI inference, actuator control, three outputs, and unknown handling.
  5. At least 30 recorded physical trial outcomes, accuracy and throughput calculations, three reject tests, and evidence of one improvement.
  6. Final supervised demonstration and short technical reflection (biggest limitation and next change).

Submit one organized folder or PDF with direct links to CAD, program and model files. All members must explain their own contributions. Due Thursday, November 19, 2026.
