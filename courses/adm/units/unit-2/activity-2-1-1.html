ADM Activity 2.1.1

Anatomy of an Automated Workcell

Analyze how the controller, sensors, robotic arm, conveyors, pneumatic actuators, safety devices, and operator work together as one automated manufacturing system.

Back to Unit 2 System Model Student Workflow What You Submit
Essential Question

How do the individual components of an automated workcell operate together as one manufacturing system?

2 class periods · 10 points

Learning Targets

By the end of this lesson

  • Identify the major mechanical, electrical, sensing, control, material-handling, and safety subsystems of the VEX CTE Workcell.
  • Explain the role of the EXP Brain as the controller for the workcell.
  • Classify workcell components as inputs, processing/control, or outputs.
  • Trace both material flow and information flow through an automated production process.
  • Explain how sensor feedback can change system behavior.
  • Analyze how a subsystem failure can affect the overall manufacturing process.
Lesson Purpose

Think in systems

Unit 1 focused on programming and robotic movement. Unit 2 begins by stepping back and examining how individual devices become a complete automated system.

The goal is not to learn another movement program. Your goal is to understand what each subsystem does, what information it uses, and how its behavior affects the rest of the workcell.

Core Model

Input → Control → Output → Feedback

Automated systems follow a repeatable information pattern. A sensor or operator provides an input. The controller processes that information using the program. The controller commands an output such as a motor, robotic arm, signal tower, or pneumatic actuator. Additional sensing can then provide feedback to verify what actually happened.

Example: Product Detection

Object Sensor → EXP Brain → Conveyor Motor → Product Moves → Sensor verifies the next condition

Example: Product Sorting

Optical Sensor → EXP Brain → program decision → Diverter / conveyor action → product reaches the selected route

Material Flow

Where the product moves

Material flow describes the physical path followed by the product. Your system map should show where a part enters the workcell, what equipment moves or manipulates it, and where it leaves or reaches its final destination.

Information Flow

Where the data moves

Information flow describes how sensor readings, program decisions, controller commands, and operator feedback move through the system. A product can remain stationary while information about that product moves through the controller.

Student Workflow

Two-Day Activity

Day 1

Understand the Workcell

  1. Complete the bellwork: What makes a group of machines a system rather than just separate machines?
  2. Participate in the workcell tour and identify the major mechanical, sensing, control, material-handling, safety, and human-interface components.
  3. Classify each major component as an input, controller/processing element, output/actuator, material-handling device, or safety/human interface.
  4. Begin your labeled Workcell System Map.
  5. Before leaving, identify one complete Input → Control → Output example from the workcell.
Day 2

Analyze the System

  1. Review the Input → Control → Output → Feedback model.
  2. Complete the Workcell System Map and show both material flow and information flow.
  3. Complete the Input–Control–Output–Feedback table for at least four workcell operations.
  4. Analyze three possible component or subsystem failures.
  5. Review your work for technical accuracy and submit the completed systems analysis.

Programming Focus

No new programming structure is introduced

Variables, loops, conditionals, functions, and robotic movement from Unit 1 are prerequisite skills. In this lesson, programming is discussed only as the logic used by the controller to connect inputs to outputs. You are not required to submit a new program for Activity 2.1.1.

Primary Deliverable

Automated Workcell Systems Analysis

  • Workcell System Map: label the major components and classify controller, inputs, outputs/actuators, material-handling equipment, and safety/operator zones.
  • Input–Control–Output–Feedback Table: analyze at least four workcell operations.
  • Material & Information Flow: trace one product through the workcell and show both physical movement and information movement.
  • Failure Analysis: analyze three component or subsystem failures, their effects, detection methods, and appropriate responses.
