Slide 11

MINI CHALLENGE

What Evidence Supports Improvement?

  • Read the claim. Select the two strongest supports.
  • Claim:
  • “The launch pad is more stable after the team revised the base.”
  • Vote for the two strongest supports.
  • A
  • Before-and-after stability observations
  • B
  • A statement that it looks more balanced
  • C
  • A photo or sketch showing the wider base
  • D
  • A teammate saying it is probably fixed
  • E
  • A list of colors used
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the best evidence pair.
Reveal answer / debrief

EVIDENCE REVEAL

  • What Evidence Supports Improvement?
  • The best support connects directly to the claim.
  • Best support: A + C
  • A
  • Before-and-after stability observations
  • Shows whether stability changed after revision.
  • C
  • A photo or sketch showing the wider base
  • Shows what physical feature was improved.
  • Useful, but not enough alone: opinions or appearance do not prove the improvement worked.
Slide 13

SCENARIO RESPONSE

What Should the Team Do Next?

  • Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.
  • The prototype holds the rocket but wobbles during the stability test.
  • A
  • Keep it because it did not fall.
  • B
  • Add details to make it look more realistic.
  • C
  • Record the wobble, identify the weak area, revise the base or supports, and retest.
  • D
  • Skip retesting to save time.
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest next step.
Reveal answer / debrief

SCENARIO REVEAL

  • What Should the Team Do Next?
  • The best next step keeps the process evidence-based.
  • Best answer: C
  • Record the wobble, identify the weak area, revise the base or supports, and retest.
  • Record

  • Engineering move: a test result is useful only when it leads to a justified next step.
