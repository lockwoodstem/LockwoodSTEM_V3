Slide 3

OPENING PROMPT

Why document engineering work?

  • Use this as a quick class discussion while attendance is taken.
  • Think first. Why would an engineer need organized notes, sketches, files, and test data?
  • Quick class share
  • Raise your hand and share one reason documentation matters in engineering.
  • Student answers might include: remember decisions, prove results, share ideas, or avoid mistakes.
  • We will reveal a working definition of engineering documentation on the next slide.
Reveal answer / debrief

OPENING DEBRIEF

  • Engineering Documentation
  • A working definition for the year.
  • Engineering documentation is an organized record of decisions, evidence, changes, and results.
  • Trace
  • Show when and why design decisions were made.
  • Organize
  • Keep pages, files, and artifacts in a consistent order.
  • Prove
  • Use sketches, data, photos, and notes to support claims.
  • Share
  • Make work understandable to teammates, teachers, and reviewers.
  • Today's mission: build the notebook and resource habits that protect your engineering evidence.
Slide 5

INTERACTIVE VOTE

Notebook Habits: Which One Fits?

  • Hold up fingers 1–4 or vote silently. Then justify your choice.
  • Prompt: Which habit best supports professional engineering work?
  • 1
  • Adding pages wherever they fit
  • Random
  • 2
  • Keeping a chronological record with a table of contents
  • Traceable
  • 3
  • Saving files with names only you understand
  • Confusing
  • 4
  • Waiting until the end to write everything down
  • Risky
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest choice.
Reveal answer / debrief

VOTE REVEAL

  • Notebook Habits: Best Answer
  • A good notebook makes design thinking easy to inspect later.
  • Best answer: 2
  • Keeping a chronological record with a table of contents
  • Why it fits
  • It preserves the order of work and helps others find evidence.
  • Why the others miss
  • The other choices make decisions harder to trace or verify.
  • Carry-forward idea
  • In IED, organization is part of the engineering evidence, not extra paperwork.
Slide 7

SYSTEM SETUP

Notebook + Course Resource System

  • You will use physical and digital systems together.
  • Notebook system
  • 1.5 inch binder with dividers
  • Printed table of contents first
  • Number pages after the TOC
  • Add work in chronological order
  • Store evidence for reviews
  • Digital resource system
  • Google Classroom submissions
  • Downloads and templates
  • Shared files when directed
  • Clear file naming habits

Slide 9

ORGANIZATION SHIFT

Loose Work vs. Engineering Notebook

  • The goal is not just to keep papers; the goal is to preserve evidence.
  • Loose work
  • Hard to inspect
  • Pages can get lost
  • Order may be unclear
  • Labels are often missing
  • Files may be hard to find
  • Engineering notebook
  • Evidence record
  • Pages stay in order
  • TOC helps locate work
  • Labels explain artifacts
  • Files connect to submissions
Slide 10

KEY IDEA

Traceable Engineering Work

  • Your work should make sense later, even after the project moves on.
  • Record
  • What you created or observed
  • Label
  • What the evidence shows
  • Locate
  • Where the work can be found
  • Evidence can include…
  • Notebook pages
  • TOC
  • File names
  • Templates
  • Submissions
  • Teacher feedback
Slide 11

MINI CHALLENGE

What Would Help an Absent Teammate?

  • Read the situation. Select the two strongest supports.
  • Claim:
  • “A teammate missed class and needs to understand what the team completed today.”
  • Vote for the two strongest supports.
  • A
  • A dated notebook page with labels and notes
  • B
  • A screenshot named Image1_final_final
  • C
  • The posted lesson page and assignment directions
  • D
  • A classmate saying, “We did stuff”
  • E
  • A loose paper with no name or date
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the best evidence pair.
Reveal answer / debrief

EVIDENCE REVEAL

  • What Would Help an Absent Teammate?
  • The best support must be clear, traceable, and findable.
  • Best support: A + C
  • A
  • A dated notebook page with labels and notes
  • Shows what happened and when it happened.
  • C
  • The posted lesson page and assignment directions
  • Shows what resource or submission was expected.
  • Useful, but not enough alone: screenshots or classmate comments help only when they are clearly named and connected to the task.
Slide 13

SCENARIO RESPONSE

What Should the Student Do Next?

  • Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.
  • A student finishes a worksheet but cannot remember whether it belongs before or after the current notebook page.
  • A
  • Put it anywhere because it is finished.
  • B
  • Leave it loose until the end of the unit.
  • C
  • Check the date, update the table of contents, number it correctly, and file it in order.
  • D
  • Throw it away and ask for another copy later.
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest next step.
Reveal answer / debrief

SCENARIO REVEAL

  • What Should the Student Do Next?
  • The best next step protects the evidence record.
  • Best answer: C
  • Check the date, update the table of contents, number it correctly, and file it in order.
  • Check
  • Use the date and assignment title.
  • Update
  • Add the item to the table of contents.
  • File
  • Place it in the correct chronological order.
  • Engineering move: make the work traceable before it gets lost or disconnected from the project.

QUICK CHECK REVEAL

  • Strong engineering organization makes evidence easy to find and inspect.
  • A

  • Takeaway: traceable work is easier to grade, revise, and defend.
