OPENING PROMPT

What makes notes useful later?

  • Use this as a quick class discussion while attendance is taken.
  • Think first. Why does engineering documentation matter in engineering work?
  • Quick class share
  • Raise your hand and share one way engineering documentation could improve a project.
  • Student answers might include: clearer decisions, better tests, stronger evidence, or better communication.
  • We will reveal a working definition on the next slide.
Reveal answer / debrief

OPENING DEBRIEF

  • Engineering Documentation
  • A working definition for this lesson.
  • Engineering Documentation helps engineers make work clear, testable, and defendable.
  • Clarify
  • Make the goal and evidence easier to understand.
  • Plan
  • Decide what should happen before building.
  • Check
  • Use criteria, data, or feedback to judge the work.
  • Explain
  • Communicate the decision with evidence.
  • Today's mission: practice engineering documentation so the next design step is stronger.

Slide 7

LESSON FOCUS

Engineering Documentation

  • Use today's tools to prepare for the next design step.
  • Strong documentation
  • Dated and labeled pages
  • Clear sketches or photos
  • Measurements and observations
  • Changes explained with evidence
  • Another person can follow it
  • Weak documentation
  • Vague phrases like “it worked”
  • Missing labels or dates
  • No measurements or sketches
  • Unclear design changes
  • Hard for others to understand

Slide 9

MINDSET SHIFT

Weak vs. Strong Documentation

  • The goal is to make engineering thinking visible and useful.
  • Weak notes
  • Weak approach
  • Unclear purpose
  • Little evidence
  • Hard to inspect
  • Difficult to revise
  • Strong documentation
  • Strong approach
  • Clear purpose
  • Evidence is recorded
  • Easy to inspect
  • Supports revision
Slide 10

KEY IDEA

Engineering Documentation

  • Strong engineering work connects actions, evidence, and decisions.
  • Clear
  • Another person can follow it
  • Dated
  • Work is traceable over time
  • Evidence
  • Claims connect to proof
  • Evidence can include…
  • Dates
  • Labels
  • Sketches
  • Data
  • Photos
  • Revision notes

MINI CHALLENGE

What Evidence Would Improve the Notes?

  • Read the claim. Select the two strongest supports.
  • Claim:
  • “Our paper airplane revision was based on test evidence.”
  • Vote for the two strongest supports.
  • A
  • Version 1 and Version 2 distances
  • B
  • A vague note saying it worked
  • C
  • A labeled sketch showing the changed fold
  • D
  • A statement that the plane looked better
  • E
  • A page with no date
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the best evidence pair.
Reveal answer / debrief

EVIDENCE REVEAL

  • What Evidence Would Improve the Notes?
  • The best support connects directly to the claim.
  • Best support: A + C
  • A
  • Version 1 and Version 2 distances
  • Shows measurable performance change.
  • C
  • A labeled sketch showing the changed fold
  • Shows exactly what was revised.
  • Useful, but not enough alone: appearance or vague statements do not prove why a revision helped.

SCENARIO RESPONSE

What Should the Student Do Next?

  • Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.
  • A student writes “we fixed it” after a test but gives no details about what changed.
  • A
  • Leave it because the work is finished.
  • B
  • Add more decoration to the page.
  • C
  • Add the test result, label the change, and explain why the revision was made.
  • D
  • Erase the entry and pretend the first version worked.
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest next step.
