Engineering Notebook & Documentation Basics

Focus Question: What makes engineering documentation useful?

Opening Prompt

Could another person understand your work?

Good documentation is written for the future reader—not only for the person who completed the activity.

Quick class share: What information would be missing from the note “It worked”?

Reveal the documentation standard

Strong documentation makes engineering thinking visible.

It is clear, dated, labeled, evidence-based, and detailed enough to support a review or repeat the work.

Compare

Weak vs. Strong Documentation

More words do not automatically create better evidence.

Weak Evidence
  • Undated or unlabeled notes
  • Missing sketches or measurements
  • Vague claims such as “it worked”
  • No explanation of changes
  • Hard for another person to follow
Strong Evidence
  • Dated, labeled, and organized
  • Sketches show important features
  • Data and observations are specific
  • Decisions are explained with evidence
  • Another person can understand the work
Documentation Standard

Use five checks throughout the course

These checks apply to notebook pages, photos, screenshots, data tables, and design-review evidence.

Clear

Another person can understand what happened.

Dated

The work can be traced over time.

Labeled

Sketches, tables, and images have meaning.

Evidence-Based

Claims connect to data or observations.

Review-Ready

There is enough detail to explain a decision.

Revision Workshop

Improve the Lesson 0.4 artifact

Use the documentation standard to strengthen the paper-airplane worksheet.

Review the evidence

  1. Check the date and page number.
  2. Verify that sketches and versions are labeled.
  3. Confirm that both distances are recorded.
  4. Replace vague statements with specific observations.
  5. Explain why the revision was chosen.

Upgrade a weak note

Weak: “We fixed the wing and it worked better.”

Stronger: “Version 1 curved left. We corrected the uneven left-wing fold. Version 2 traveled 12 ft farther and followed a straighter path.”

Quick Check

Which note is review-ready?

Vote before opening the answer.

  1. 1“Changed it because it was bad.”
  2. 2“After Version 1 curved left, we leveled the wing tips. Version 2 traveled straighter.”
Reveal the stronger note

Best answer: 2

It identifies the observation, the change, and the result. The reader can follow the reasoning.
