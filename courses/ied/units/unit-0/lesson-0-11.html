IED Unit 0 • Lesson 0.11

Final Documentation & Design Review

Focus Question: How do engineers communicate and defend a final design?

View Lesson Plan What You Submit Back to Unit 0
Mission Objective

Defend the final design

Organize process evidence and explain how testing, revisions, criteria, and constraints shaped the finished prototype.

ShowSupportDefend
Mission Briefing

45-Minute Flight Plan

Prepare a concise design review that shows the process—not only the finished object.

Learning Targets

  • Prepare final documentation for the launch-pad prototype.
  • Explain how the design changed from concept to final version.
  • Justify how the final prototype meets criteria and constraints.

Success Looks Like…

Every important design claim is supported by a sketch, photo, test result, observation, or documented decision.

Core habit: Use evidence language to explain what changed, why it changed, and how the final design performs.

Opening Prompt

What makes a final design believable?

A finished prototype is not enough; the team must show how evidence shaped it.

Quick class share: What would you need to see before accepting a team’s claim that its design meets the requirements?

Reveal the design-review purpose

A design review explains and defends the final design with evidence.

It shows the path from problem definition to testing, revision, and final performance.

Visual Communication

Use diagrams to make structure understandable

Labels and views help an audience inspect the design claim.

Evidence Set

Show the process and support the claims

Select evidence that helps the audience understand decisions.

Show the Process
  • Problem definition
  • Initial concepts
  • Build plan
  • Prototype construction
  • Testing and improvement
  • Final design
Use Evidence
  • Labeled sketches
  • Materials and dimensions
  • Photos
  • Test results
  • Observations
  • Design explanations
Review Preparation

Organize the story before presenting

The review should be concise, ordered, and evidence-based.

  1. Check that the prototype is finished and stable.
  2. Organize evidence from Lessons 0.7–0.10.
  3. Create or update a final labeled sketch.
  4. Summarize the most important test results.
  5. Explain one major improvement.
  6. Show how the final design meets criteria and constraints.
Evidence Language

Use sentence frames to defend decisions

Specific language helps connect claims to proof.

  • Our launch pad meets the stability criterion because…
  • The provided rocket is supported by…
  • Our most important test result was…
  • After testing, we changed… because…
  • The evidence that supports this decision is…
  • With more time, the next improvement would be…
Scenario Response

The prototype is finished, but the reasoning is missing

Choose the strongest next step before opening the answer.

A team plans to show only the finished prototype because the changes seem obvious.

  1. APresent the prototype without process evidence.
  2. BSay the changes are obvious and skip the explanation.
  3. CAdd the original problem, test results, revisions, and final justification.
  4. DFocus only on decoration and presentation style.
Reveal the strongest review plan

Best answer: C

The audience needs evidence showing how the team reached the final design. A review should make the reasoning inspectable.
