Slide 3

OPENING PROMPT

What do engineers do?

  • Use this as a quick class discussion while attendance is taken.
  • Think first. What actions, habits, or decisions make someone an engineer?
  • Quick class share
  • Raise your hand and share one short answer. Try to use an action word.
  • Examples from students might sound like: design, build, test, fix, invent, solve, measure, or improve.
  • We will reveal a working definition on the next slide.
Reveal answer / debrief

OPENING DEBRIEF

  • What Engineers Do
  • A working definition for the year.
  • Engineers use knowledge, tools, and creativity to solve problems under real constraints.
  • Define
  • Clarify the need, user, criteria, and constraints.
  • Generate
  • Create multiple possible solutions before choosing one.
  • Test
  • Collect evidence from models, prototypes, data, and feedback.
  • Communicate
  • Document decisions so others can understand and improve the work.
  • Today's mission: practice thinking about claims, evidence, and decisions like an engineer.
Slide 5

INTERACTIVE VOTE

Engineer Actions: Which One Fits?

  • Hold up fingers 1–4 or vote silently. Then justify your choice.
  • Prompt: Which action sounds most like engineering work?
  • 1
  • Copying exact instructions to make the same model as everyone else
  • Completion
  • 2
  • Choosing the first idea because it seems easiest to build
  • Shortcut
  • 3
  • Testing two versions and comparing data before deciding
  • Engineering
  • 4
  • Making a design look impressive without checking if it works
  • Appearance
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest engineering choice.
Reveal answer / debrief

VOTE REVEAL

  • Engineer Actions: Best Answer
  • Engineering is using evidence to make and defend design decisions.
  • Best answer: 3
  • Testing two versions and comparing data before deciding
  • Why it fits
  • It uses comparison, data, and a decision based on evidence.
  • Why the others miss
  • They focus on copying, ease, or appearance rather than proof.
  • Carry-forward idea
  • In IED, your answer is stronger when it connects to evidence.

Slide 8

TURN + TALK

Aerospace Connection Map

  • Connect the course to real aerospace systems before projects begin.
  • Choose one system
  • Rocket
  • Drone
  • Satellite
  • Rover
  • Aircraft
  • Then answer:
  • What design choices would an engineer need to make for that system?
  • Map your answer to engineering habits
  • Sketch
  • What features or shapes might matter?
  • Model
  • What could be represented in CAD or with a prototype?
  • Measure
  • What dimensions, forces, distances, or data might matter?
  • Test
  • What would prove that the design works?
  • Communicate
  • Who needs to understand the design?
  • Share-out target: one system + one design decision + one kind of evidence.
Slide 9

MINDSET SHIFT

Assignment vs. Engineering Problem

  • The goal is not just to finish; the goal is to make a design claim you can defend.
  • Typical assignment
  • Completion mindset
  • Often has one expected answer
  • Can be completed and put away
  • Usually judged by correct/incorrect
  • Mistakes are avoided
  • Engineering problem
  • Evidence mindset
  • Has multiple possible solutions
  • Must satisfy criteria and constraints
  • Uses testing to compare designs
  • Mistakes become evidence for improvement
Slide 10

KEY IDEA

Evidence-Based Engineering

  • Engineers connect what they claim to proof that others can inspect.
  • Claim
  • What you believe about the design
  • Evidence
  • What proves or challenges the claim
  • Decision
  • What you do next because of the evidence
  • Evidence can include…
  • Sketches
  • Measurements
  • Test data
  • Observations
  • Photos
  • User feedback
Slide 11

MINI CHALLENGE

What Evidence Would You Need?

  • Read the claim. Select the evidence that would best support it.
  • Claim:
  • “Our launch pad design supports the rocket safely and is ready for testing.”
  • Vote for the two strongest pieces of evidence.
  • A
  • A labeled sketch showing where the rocket contacts the support
  • B
  • A photo that makes the prototype look finished
  • C
  • A stability test showing the pad does not tip with the rocket placed on it
  • D
  • A teammate saying the design is probably strong enough
  • E
  • A list of the materials used to build the base and support
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the best evidence pair.
Reveal answer / debrief

EVIDENCE REVEAL

  • What Evidence Would You Need?
  • The best evidence must connect directly to the design claim.
  • Best support: A + C
  • A
  • A labeled sketch showing where the rocket contacts the support
  • Shows design intent and support locations.
  • C
  • A stability test showing the pad does not tip with the rocket placed on it
  • Shows performance against a safety/stability criterion.
  • Useful, but not enough alone: photos, material lists, and teammate opinions can support the story, but they do not prove the claim by themselves.
Slide 13

SCENARIO RESPONSE

What Should the Engineer Do Next?

  • Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.
  • A team builds a small rocket support. During the first test, it stands up, but the rocket leans to one side.
  • A
  • Keep it because the prototype did not fall over.
  • B
  • Add decorations so the design looks more complete.
  • C
  • Record the lean, identify where the support is uneven, revise, and retest.
  • D
  • Start over without writing down what happened.
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest next step.
Reveal answer / debrief

SCENARIO REVEAL

  • What Should the Engineer Do Next?
  • The best next step turns a problem into useful evidence.
  • Best answer: C
  • Record the lean, identify where the support is uneven, revise, and retest.
  • Record
  • Write down what happened during the test.
  • Identify
  • Find the likely cause of the lean.
  • Revise + Retest
  • Change the design and collect new evidence.
  • Engineering move: use the problem as evidence, not as a reason to hide or restart the work.
Slide 15

QUICK CHECK

Evidence or Opinion?

  • Vote first. Then explain which statement is strongest and why.
  • A
  • “Our design is the best because it looks strong.”
  • Vote: evidence or opinion?
  • B
  • “Version 2 traveled 12 ft farther than Version 1 in the official test.”
  • Vote: evidence or opinion?
  • C
  • “We changed the wing fold because the first test curved left three times.”
  • Vote: evidence or opinion?
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal which statements are strongest.
Reveal answer / debrief

QUICK CHECK REVEAL

  • Evidence or Opinion?
  • A strong engineering answer connects a claim to specific evidence.
  • A
  • Opinion-heavy
  • Uses a vague judgment: “looks strong.”
  • B
  • Evidence-based
  • Uses a measurable result: 12 ft farther.
  • C
  • Evidence-based reasoning
  • Connects a change to repeated test observations.
  • Takeaway: claim + specific evidence = stronger engineering communication.
