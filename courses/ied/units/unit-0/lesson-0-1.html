Opening Prompt

What do engineers do?

Think about actions, habits, and decisions—not job titles.

Quick class share: Give one short answer using an action word such as design, build, test, fix, invent, solve, measure, or improve.

Reveal the working definition

Engineers use knowledge, tools, and creativity to solve problems under real constraints.

1

Define

Clarify the need, user, criteria, and constraints.

2

Generate

Create multiple possible solutions before choosing one.

3

Test

Collect evidence from models, prototypes, data, and feedback.

4

Communicate

Document decisions so others can understand and improve the work.

Interactive Vote

Engineer Actions: Which One Fits?

Hold up fingers 1–4 or vote silently. Then justify your choice.

Which action sounds most like engineering work?

  1. 1Copying exact instructions to make the same model as everyone else
  2. 2Choosing the first idea because it seems easiest to build
  3. 3Testing two versions and comparing data before deciding
  4. 4Making a design look impressive without checking if it works
Reveal the strongest engineering choice

Best answer: 3

Testing two versions and comparing data before deciding uses comparison, data, and a decision based on evidence. The other choices prioritize copying, ease, or appearance rather than proof.

Carry-forward idea: In IED, an answer becomes stronger when it connects to evidence.

Mindset Shift

Assignment vs. Engineering Problem

The goal is not just to finish; the goal is to make a design claim you can defend.

Completion Mindset

Typical Assignment

  • Often has one expected answer
  • Can be completed and put away
  • Usually judged as correct or incorrect
  • Mistakes are avoided
Evidence Mindset

Engineering Problem

  • Has multiple possible solutions
  • Must satisfy criteria and constraints
  • Uses testing to compare designs
  • Mistakes become evidence for improvement
Key Idea

Evidence-Based Engineering

Engineers connect what they claim to proof that others can inspect.

1

Claim

What you believe about the design

→
2

Evidence

What proves or challenges the claim

→
3

Decision

What you do next because of the evidence

Evidence can include: sketches; measurements; test data; observations; photos; prototypes; user feedback.
Mini Challenge

What Evidence Would You Need?

Select the two pieces of evidence that best support the claim.

Claim: “Our launch pad design supports the rocket safely and is ready for testing.”
  1. AA labeled sketch showing where the rocket contacts the support
  2. BA photo that makes the prototype look finished
  3. CA stability test showing the pad does not tip with the rocket placed on it
  4. DA teammate saying the design is probably strong enough
  5. EA list of the materials used to build the base and support
Reveal the best evidence pair

Best support: A + C

A shows design intent and the support locations. C provides direct performance evidence against a safety and stability criterion.

Photos, material lists, and teammate opinions can support the story, but they do not prove the claim by themselves.

Scenario Response

What Should the Engineer Do Next?

Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.

A team builds a small rocket support. During the first test, it stands up, but the rocket leans to one side.

  1. AKeep it because the prototype did not fall over.
  2. BAdd decorations so the design looks more complete.
  3. CRecord the lean, identify where the support is uneven, revise, and retest.
  4. DStart over without writing down what happened.
Reveal the strongest next step

Best answer: C

Record the lean, identify where the support is uneven, revise, and retest.

Record

Write down what happened during the test.

Identify

Find the likely cause of the lean.

Revise + Retest

Change the design and collect new evidence.

Engineering move: Use the problem as evidence, not as a reason to hide or restart the work.
Quick Check

Evidence or Opinion?

Vote on all three statements before revealing the answers.

A. “Our design is the best because it looks strong.”

B. “Version 2 traveled 12 ft farther than Version 1 in the official test.”

C. “We changed the wing fold because the first test curved left three times.”

Reveal the evidence check
A

Opinion-heavy

Uses a vague judgment: “looks strong.”

B

Evidence-based

Uses a measurable result: 12 ft farther.

C

Evidence-based reasoning

Connects a change to repeated test observations.

Takeaway: Claim + specific evidence = stronger engineering communication.
