Slide 3

OPENING PROMPT

Why is safety part of engineering?

  • Use this as a quick class discussion while attendance is taken.
  • Think first. How can one unsafe choice affect people, tools, materials, or project quality?
  • Quick class share
  • Raise your hand and share one safety risk engineers must manage.
  • Student answers might include: sharp tools, heat, moving parts, dust, cords, or distraction.
  • We will reveal why safety is treated as an engineering requirement on the next slide.
Reveal answer / debrief

OPENING DEBRIEF

  • Safety Is Engineering
  • A working definition for shared workspaces.
  • Engineering safety means controlling risk so people can build, test, and learn without avoidable harm.
  • Protect
  • Keep people, tools, and materials safe.
  • Prepare
  • Use PPE and instructions before starting work.
  • Observe
  • Watch for unsafe behavior or damaged equipment.
  • Stop
  • Pause immediately when something seems unsafe.
  • Today's mission: understand the safety expectations that make hands-on engineering possible.
Slide 5

INTERACTIVE VOTE

Safety Actions: Which One Fits?

  • Hold up fingers 1–4 or vote silently. Then justify your choice.
  • Prompt: Which action sounds most like safe engineering work?
  • 1
  • Using a tool before getting permission
  • Unsafe
  • 2
  • Wearing PPE only after something goes wrong
  • Too late
  • 3
  • Checking directions, PPE, and workspace before starting
  • Ready
  • 4
  • Rushing because the project needs to be finished
  • Risky
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest choice.
Reveal answer / debrief

VOTE REVEAL

  • Safety Actions: Best Answer
  • Safe engineers prepare before tools, materials, or machines are used.
  • Best answer: 3
  • Checking directions, PPE, and workspace before starting
  • Why it fits
  • It prevents risk before work begins.
  • Why the others miss
  • The others wait too long, skip permission, or create new hazards.
  • Carry-forward idea
  • In IED, unsafe work stops the process until the risk is fixed.
Slide 7

SAFETY CULTURE

PPE + FabLab Expectations

  • The FabLab is a shared engineering workspace with higher expectations.
  • PPE expectations
  • Safety glasses when required
  • Closed-toe shoes for fabrication
  • Hair tied back around tools
  • Loose items secured
  • Gloves only when directed
  • Behavior expectations
  • No horseplay or rushing
  • Ask before using tools
  • Keep work areas clear
  • Respect active build zones
  • Report unsafe conditions
Slide 8

WORKSPACE CHECK

Before You Build

  • Connect safe setup to the work you will do later in Unit 0.
  • Check before work
  • PPE
  • Permission
  • Tool condition
  • Clear workspace
  • Safe behavior
  • Then answer:
  • What should happen before a team begins building a launch pad prototype?
  • Safety habits
  • Look
  • Check the work area and materials.
  • Prepare
  • Put on required PPE.
  • Ask
  • Get permission before tools.
  • Use
  • Operate only as directed.
  • Report
  • Speak up about risks or damage.
  • Share-out target: one safety habit + one risk it prevents.
Slide 9

SAFETY SHIFT

Careless Work vs. Safe Engineering

  • The goal is not speed; the goal is controlled, responsible work.
  • Careless work
  • Creates risk
  • Starts without permission
  • Ignores PPE directions
  • Leaves clutter in work areas
  • Hides mistakes or damage
  • Safe engineering
  • Controls risk
  • Prepares before starting
  • Uses required PPE
  • Keeps work area clear
  • Reports problems immediately
Slide 10

KEY IDEA

Safety Protects Quality

  • A safe process creates better evidence, better prototypes, and fewer avoidable problems.
  • People
  • No project is worth an injury
  • Tools
  • Equipment must stay usable
  • Evidence
  • Clean, controlled work improves results
  • Evidence can include…
  • PPE
  • Permission
  • Clear space
  • Tool checks
  • Supervision
  • Reporting
Slide 11

MINI CHALLENGE

What Safety Evidence Matters?

  • Read the claim. Select the two strongest supports.
  • Claim:
  • “Our team is ready to begin a supervised FabLab activity.”
  • Vote for the two strongest supports.
  • A
  • Everyone has the required PPE ready
  • B
  • The team wants to finish quickly
  • C
  • The teacher has given permission to begin
  • D
  • The materials look fun to use
  • E
  • A teammate says they used the tool before
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the best evidence pair.
Reveal answer / debrief

EVIDENCE REVEAL

  • What Safety Evidence Matters?
  • The best support confirms readiness and permission.
  • Best support: A + C
  • A
  • Everyone has the required PPE ready
  • Shows the team is prepared for the activity.
  • C
  • The teacher has given permission to begin
  • Confirms supervision and readiness for tool use.
  • Useful, but not enough alone: prior experience and motivation do not replace permission, PPE, and safe setup.
Slide 13

SCENARIO RESPONSE

What Should the Student Do Next?

  • Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.
  • A student notices a damaged tool on the table before the activity begins.
  • A
  • Use it carefully and hope it works.
  • B
  • Hide it so the team does not lose time.
  • C
  • Stop, set it aside, and report it to the teacher immediately.
  • D
  • Let someone else discover the problem later.
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal the strongest next step.
Reveal answer / debrief

SCENARIO REVEAL

  • What Should the Student Do Next?
  • The best next step removes the hazard from the workflow.
  • Best answer: C
  • Stop, set it aside, and report it to the teacher immediately.
  • Stop
  • Do not continue with a known hazard.
  • Separate
  • Keep the damaged tool from being used.
  • Report
  • Tell the teacher so the risk can be fixed.
  • Engineering move: unsafe conditions are not ignored; they are identified and controlled.

QUICK CHECK

  • “I put on required PPE and waited for permission.”
  • Vote: safe or unsafe?
  • C
  • “I stopped when something seemed unsafe and reported it.”
  • Vote: safe or unsafe?
  • Vote first. Then we will reveal which statements are strongest.
Reveal answer / debrief

QUICK CHECK REVEAL

  • Safe or Unsafe?
  • Safe choices reduce risk before it becomes a problem.
  • A
  • Unsafe
  • Watching does not replace permission or instruction.
  • B
  • Safe
  • PPE and permission are readiness steps.
  • C
  • Safe
  • Stopping and reporting protects the workspace.
  • Takeaway: safe habits are part of professional engineering behavior.
