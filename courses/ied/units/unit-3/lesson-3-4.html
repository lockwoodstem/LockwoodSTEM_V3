Joints & Assembly Relationships

Focus question: How do assembly relationships control where components belong and how they are allowed to move?

Assignment

Control the Rocket Assembly with Joints

Apply appropriate Fusion joints to the major rocket interfaces. Most static rocket connections will use rigid relationships, but the instructor may demonstrate other joint types for comparison.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Identify the first interface: Select two components that should remain fixed relative to one another in the final assembly.
  2. Create the joint: Select meaningful mating faces, edges, centers, or joint origins and apply the required relationship.
  3. Check orientation: Flip or rotate the joint when necessary so the parts meet correctly.
  4. Repeat for the remaining major interfaces: Add joints until the assigned assembly components are controlled.
  5. Test the assembly: Attempt to drag components. Parts that should be rigidly connected should no longer move independently.
  6. Inspect the Browser: Confirm the expected joints appear and are not duplicated unnecessarily.
  7. Repair failed joints: Fix any relationship that references the wrong geometry or creates an obviously incorrect orientation.

Before You Submit

  • Major components are controlled by joints.
  • Rigid components do not move independently.
  • Joint orientation matches the intended assembly.
  • No unnecessary duplicate joints are present.
  • The grounded reference component remains correct.
