Rover Assembly: As-Built Joints

Focus question: How can we make an already-positioned rover function using rigid and revolute As-Built Joints?

Go to AssignmentWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 3
Assembly Motion • Autodesk Fusion

Your six-wheel rover is already positioned correctly—including its frame, motors, suspension links, payload box, and gripper—but has no functioning assembly joints.

Engineering challenge: Define what is fixed and what can rotate without repositioning or remodeling the supplied parts.

Unit 3 Learning Studio • Lesson 3.4

Understand, visualize, and apply the concept

Visual ExplanationVideo TutorialFusion / Lab GuidePracticeAssignmentWhat to Submit
Concept

As-Built Joint

Applies a mechanical relationship to components that are already in their correct locations. It defines motion without moving the parts into alignment.

Decision

Rigid or revolute?

Use Rigid when two parts must move as one. Use Revolute when one part must rotate around a specified axle or pivot axis.

Proof

A functional rover

  • Stationary chassis or frame grounded.
  • Fixed brackets and supports attached correctly.
  • All intended wheels rotate around the correct axis; nothing else drifts or detaches.
  • Joint limits stop suspension and arm mechanisms before they move through parts or beyond their mechanical range.

02 / Fusion command reference

As-Built Joint — when parts are already in position

Official Autodesk walkthrough

Use Design → Solid → Assemble → As-Built Joint. Select two existing components. For wheels, choose Revolute, identify the hub center and orient the rotation axis down the axle. For fixed mounts, choose Rigid.

Autodesk: Create an As-Built Joint ↗

Use Preview Motion before accepting and Animate Joint from the Joints folder for verification. For joints with physical stops, also use Edit Motion Limits to define allowable minimum and maximum positions.

Joint types and motion

Rigid: zero movement between parts. Revolute: rotation about one chosen axis. A rigid motor housing can support a revolute wheel without becoming a moving body itself.

Autodesk: Joint Relationships Tutorial ↗

Do not substitute Move, Align, standard Joint or a rigid group for the required As-Built Joint demonstrations.

03 / Component-by-component procedure

Build a functioning six-wheel rover

Required: chassis, payload, wheel drives

  1. 1. Open and save. Open Rover Assembly Practice.f3d. Use Save As to create LastName_FirstName_3_4_Rover. Leave all placed parts where they are.
  2. 2. Inspect. Expand the Browser, identify Base Body, six occurrences of Wheel, their motor housings, legs/shafts and Payload Box.
  3. 3. Ground. Ground the stationary main frame (Base Body if it is the chassis occurrence). Do not ground the wheels or moving suspension links.
  4. 4. Fix frame-mounted parts. Add Rigid As-Built Joints for the Payload Box to its fixed support and for each Motor housing to the leg/bracket that carries it.
  5. 5. Connect wheel hubs. At each of six visible wheels, add a Revolute As-Built Joint between the wheel (or its rotating wheel-and-shaft subassembly) and its fixed motor/axle support. Select the actual hub axis as the joint rotation axis.
  6. 6. Connect the leg structures. Examine Rear Legs, Rear Legs 2, Leg Shaft and the blue frame links. Use Rigid where two parts are bolted together; use Revolute only where a suspension pivot can physically turn. Avoid locking both ends of a true pivot.
  7. 7. Apply physical joint limits. For any suspension pivot with a real travel stop, right-click the joint and choose Edit Motion Limits. Set Minimum and Maximum angles that match the available mechanical range; check both endpoints. Do not arbitrarily limit wheels designed for continuous rotation.
  8. 8. Verify all six wheels. Animate each wheel joint, checking left/right and front/middle/rear positions. Wheels should rotate on their own hubs without translating away or carrying the chassis with them.

Quality checks and optional arm extension

Core must work before starting the arm:

  • Exactly six visible wheels have independently testable revolute motion.
  • Motor housings and payload box remain attached to their stationary or articulated support.
  • The chassis does not move when a wheel joint is animated.
  • Any leg suspension pivots that are meant to move have appropriate axes and physically reasonable minimum/maximum limits where applicable; fixed links do not drift.

Advanced extension: articulated arm

  1. Locate Arm Base, Arm and Gripper Wrist in the Browser.
  2. Use Rigid As-Built Joints between fixed motor/servo housings and their supports.
  3. Add a Revolute As-Built Joint at each physically articulated shoulder, elbow or wrist pivot. Do not guess a pivot that the geometry does not support.
  4. For GripperMechanism and its small parts, constrain fixed retainers rigidly. Only attempt detailed gripper-finger kinematics if the separate components and pivot centers are clearly modeled.
  5. Set minimum and maximum joint limits on each arm or gripper pivot that has a physical stop so it cannot rotate through the rover or exceed the mechanism’s range.
  6. Animate the arm to both ends of each permitted travel range; verify the chassis and wheels do not unexpectedly move.

Troubleshooting: if a wheel will not move, check for a rigid joint to the frame or a second conflicting joint. If a wheel wobbles, edit the revolute joint origin and rotation axis. If parts become loose, identify which joint chain is missing.

04 / Make movement physically possible

Joint limits: prevent impossible motion

Engineering rule: A revolute joint defines how a part rotates; joint limits define how far it is permitted to rotate. Real suspension arms, steering linkages, robot arms and grippers cannot usually rotate through the chassis, fold through neighboring parts or move past their mechanical stops. Use limits to prevent that impossible motion.

How to set and test limits in Autodesk Fusion

  1. Find the moving As-Built Joint in the Browser’s Joints folder.
  2. Right-click it and choose Edit Motion Limits (sometimes shown as Edit Joint Limits).
  3. For its Rotate motion, enable Minimum and Maximum; enter angles in degrees measured relative to the joint’s reference position.
  4. Choose limits based on the model’s actual physical clearances or the instructor’s specified range. Do not invent or copy universal angle values.
  5. Preview or drive the joint to both endpoints. Confirm it stops before colliding with or passing through another solid and still has its intended range of movement.
  6. Repeat for other joints that have genuine mechanical endpoints. If available, use Animate Joint Relationships to check effects on connected components.

Official Autodesk: Joint Motion Limits ↗

Which rover joints need limits?

JointLimit decision
Six drive wheelsNormally no angular stops — they need continuous 360° rotation.
Suspension / leg pivotsYes, if the physical structure has a bounded travel range. Prevent legs or links folding through the chassis or extending past their stops.
Arm / wrist / gripper pivots (extension)Yes, when the design has stops. Prevent over-rotation and interference with neighboring parts.
Rigid fixed hardwareNo motion to limit; a Rigid joint already prevents relative movement.

Important: Joint limits restrict the specified joint’s angles; they are not automatic collision detection. Check the model at the minimum, maximum and intermediate positions to confirm that the movement is physically possible.

Before you leave

Why use As-Built instead of a regular Joint here?

The rover components are already in the correct locations. An As-Built Joint adds the motion relationship without repositioning them.

What needs to be grounded?

The stationary chassis or frame that serves as the assembly reference—not the freely rotating wheels.

How do you know the assembly works?

Every intended rotating part turns about its correct axis; fixed hardware remains connected; the grounded frame does not drift; bounded joints stop at realistic endpoints.

Should wheel joints have minimum and maximum rotation limits?

Not when the wheels must rotate continuously. Limits belong on mechanisms with physical end stops, such as some suspension and arm pivots.

Do joint limits automatically prevent every collision?

No. A limit controls the range of one joint. Check real part clearance throughout the permitted motion.

3.4 — As-Built Joint Rover Assembly

Starting file: Rover Assembly Practice.f3d, provided by your instructor. All model parts are already in place. You will not draw, extrude, reposition or redesign the rover. Your job is to define the mechanical relationships.

Required work checklist

  • Save your own copy as LastName_FirstName_3_4_Rover.
  • Identify and ground the stationary main chassis, not the wheels or articulated frame pieces.
  • Create rigid As-Built Joints for fixed equipment and motor mounts, including the frame-mounted payload.
  • Create six revolute As-Built wheel joints, with each axis centered in its true hub.
  • Connect any additional suspension links/leg supports according to their physical motion. Do not accidentally constrain intended pivots rigidly.
  • Apply Joint Motion Limits wherever a real part has a limited range of travel. For a suspension pivot with a mechanical stop, enter reasonable minimum/maximum angles based on the model. Test both stops and make sure parts cannot pass through each other. Do not limit free-spinning wheels. If a bounded joint is not present in the required core, explain why no core joint needs angular limits.
  • Animate all six wheels. The chassis must stay fixed, each wheel must rotate without wobble, and no parts may detach. Animate any limited joint to both minimum and maximum endpoints.
  • Capture the specified joint evidence and a short video that proves correct independent wheel motion.

Optional +5 point extension

Constrain Arm Base, Arm, Gripper Wrist and their clearly modeled pivots with correct As-Built Joints. Set motion limits on bounded arm/gripper joints and demonstrate both travel endpoints for at least two motions. Detailed internal gripper-finger mechanism constraints are optional and should be attempted only if the supplied parts support them.

20-point rubric (core activity)

  • 4 pts: starting model preserved, chassis grounded, original parts identifiable.
  • 4 pts: rigid As-Built connections correctly secure mounted motors, payload and fixed support components.
  • 6 pts: six revolute wheel joints, with correct origins and independent rotation (one point per wheel).
  • 2 pts: working suspension/support relationships and motion checks, with no loose parts or unintended movement.
  • 2 pts: correct minimum/maximum motion limits for bounded joints, with endpoint tests; or a justified explanation if no core joints have physical end stops.
  • 2 pts: clearly labeled evidence and a concise explanation of As-Built versus ordinary joints.

Completion standard: Do not add joints just to increase the total. Correct independent motion matters more than joint count beyond the six wheel interfaces. The exact suspension pivot configuration should match the physical geometry visible in Fusion.
