Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Plan an assembly with multiple joint types.
  • Ground the frame and connect components in a logical sequence.
  • Combine revolute and slider joints to create coordinated motion.
  • Troubleshoot joint orientation and over-constrained components.

Unit 3 Progression

This challenge combines the previously practiced joint types in a single functioning assembly.

Assignment

Crank-Slider Mechanism Assembly Challenge

Use the instructor-provided parts: frame, crank, connecting rod, slider/carriage, and required pins or retainers. Build from the individual files; do not start with an already jointed assembly.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Create a new design named LastName_FirstName_3_4_CrankSlider and insert the supplied parts.
  2. Ground the frame and organize component names in the Browser.
  3. Use revolute joints at the crank pivot and the connecting-rod pivots.
  4. Use a slider joint to constrain the carriage to the linear guide.
  5. Use rigid joints only for pins or retainers that do not move relative to their parent component.
  6. Drive the crank through a complete available cycle and verify the carriage translates without detaching.
  7. Diagnose and fix joint errors or over-constraints before saving the assembly.

Before You Submit

  • The frame is grounded; no loose or unconstrained parts remain.
  • The connecting rod links the crank and carriage.
  • Crank rotation produces the intended linear motion of the carriage.
Submission

What to Submit

Upload the following specific items to the Lesson 3.4 assignment in Google Classroom:

  • Fusion file (.f3d): submit LastName_FirstName_3_4_CrankSlider.f3d with named parts and complete joints.
  • Motion recording (.mp4, 15–30 seconds): drive the crank through at least one full cycle while showing the carriage moving along its guide.
  • One-sentence explanation: in the submission comment, name the joint types that control rotation and translation.

Submission check: Assessment focuses on a working jointed mechanism, not on remodeling the supplied geometry.
