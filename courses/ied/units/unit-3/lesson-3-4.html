Rover Assembly: As-Built Joints

Focus question: How can we make an already-positioned rover function using rigid and revolute As-Built Joints?

Go to AssignmentWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 3
Assembly Motion • Autodesk Fusion

Your rover is already assembled in the correct location—but none of its components have joint relationships.

Engineering challenge: Define what is fixed and what can rotate without repositioning or remodeling the supplied parts.

Concept

As-Built Joint

Applies a mechanical relationship to components that are already in their correct locations. It defines motion without moving the parts into alignment.

Decision

Rigid or revolute?

Use Rigid when two parts must move as one. Use Revolute when one part must rotate around a specified axle or pivot axis.

Proof

A functional rover

  • Stationary chassis or frame grounded.
  • Fixed brackets and supports attached correctly.
  • All intended wheels rotate around the correct axis; nothing else drifts or detaches.

03 / Practical walkthrough

Make the positioned rover move correctly

Guided Fusion workflow

  1. 1. Open the teacher-supplied rover Fusion design. Save a personal copy named LastName_FirstName_3_4_Rover. Do not alter original component positions.
  2. 2. Expand the Browser. Identify the chassis, fixed mounting pieces, wheels, axles and any additional moving parts. Verify that each independently moving part is a component, not merely a body.
  3. 3. Ground the stationary chassis or main frame to establish a fixed reference.
  4. 4. Apply Rigid As-Built Joints to pieces intended to stay firmly attached, such as fixed mounting brackets, housings or retainers.
  5. 5. Apply Revolute As-Built Joints at the actual wheel or axle rotation axes. If a wheel is rigidly connected to a rotating axle, make those parts rigid to each other and place the revolute joint between the rotating assembly and its stationary support.
  6. 6. Repeat for every intended moving component without adding extra joints that remove necessary motion.
  7. 7. Use Animate Joint or Drive Joint on each wheel joint; verify smooth rotation and a stationary chassis.
  8. 8. Save the completed design and collect the required evidence.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Everything spins together: a wheel was made rigid to the chassis. Choose a revolute relationship for its intended bearing.
  • Wheel wobbles or rotates off-center: edit the revolute origin/axis so it coincides with the hub or axle center.
  • Chassis moves when a wheel is driven: ensure the stationary chassis is grounded and the wheel is connected to that reference through a valid joint chain.
  • Part flies away: the wrong components or joint type may have been selected; check the Browser and edit the relationship.
  • No joint can be created: verify that both selections are actual components. Ask the instructor if the supplied geometry contains bodies that need preparation.

Important: Do not use Move, Align, new modeling features, or a standard Joint to rebuild the supplied rover. Its parts are already correctly positioned; the job is to define relationships with As-Built Joints.

Before you leave

Why use As-Built instead of a regular Joint here?

The rover components are already in the correct locations. An As-Built Joint adds the motion relationship without repositioning them.

What needs to be grounded?

The stationary chassis or frame that serves as the assembly reference—not the freely rotating wheels.

How do you know the assembly works?

Every intended rotating part turns about its correct axis; fixed hardware remains connected; the grounded frame does not drift.

Individual Assignment

Rover Assembly: Add As-Built Joints

Starting file: Open the instructor-provided Fusion rover model. All components are in their correct assembly locations, but no joints have been created. Your task is to define the correct connections and demonstrate working motion—not to redesign, insert or reposition parts.

Required Tasks

  1. Save your own copy as LastName_FirstName_3_4_Rover. Keep the supplied geometry and positioning intact.
  2. Identify the stationary chassis and all component groups. Ground only the stationary reference component, not each wheel.
  3. Use Assemble → As-Built Joint → Rigid for fixed connections that should have zero relative movement.
  4. Use Assemble → As-Built Joint → Revolute for each intended rotating wheel/axle interface. Position the joint origin precisely on its axle centerline.
  5. Determine whether a wheel and axle should rotate together or independently. Use the correct rigid/revolute relationship for the configuration shown in the supplied model.
  6. Complete all required mechanical connections. Do not leave unintended loose components or constrain the rover so tightly that intended parts cannot rotate.
  7. Test the completed assembly: animate at least two distinct wheel positions and verify that fixed parts stay attached. Repeat for every wheel or other intentionally moving feature.
  8. Capture evidence and save the finished assembly.

Decision rule: There is no single predetermined joint count in this lesson page. The final number and exact pairs depend on the instructor's rover model. Every intended component must be properly constrained while preserving its intended movement.

Before You Submit

  • All parts remain in their original intended locations.
  • The stationary chassis is grounded.
  • Fixed hardware moves with its parent and does not separate.
  • Each intended wheel/axle rotates about the correct physical axis, without wobble or drifting.
  • Joint types and relationships can be identified in the Fusion Browser.
  • No unrelated geometry or modeling features have been added.

Grading (20 points)

  • 4 pts: Correct starting file, grounded frame and organized components.
  • 5 pts: Appropriate rigid connections for fixed hardware.
  • 6 pts: Correct revolute joints, placement and intended wheel motion.
  • 3 pts: Verification and labeled evidence.
  • 2 pts: Brief explanation of why As-Built Joints were used.
Submission

What to Submit

  • Joint evidence (PDF): LastName_FirstName_3_4_JointEvidence.pdf, with three labeled images: (1) full rover with Fusion Browser/Joints folder visible, (2) a rigid As-Built Joint selected or shown in its settings, and (3) a revolute As-Built Joint selected with its rotation axis visible.
  • Motion evidence (15–30 second .mp4): Record at least one wheel rotating using Animate/Drive Joint while the chassis and fixed parts remain stationary. Show a second wheel joint if the rover contains independently rotating wheels.
  • Two-sentence explanation: In your submission comment, explain why an As-Built Joint is appropriate for this already-positioned model, and how you decided which parts require rigid versus revolute motion.

Assessment focuses on: correct joint behavior and clear evidence, not creating new geometry. Your instructor may identify exact component pairs once the supplied rover file is reviewed.
