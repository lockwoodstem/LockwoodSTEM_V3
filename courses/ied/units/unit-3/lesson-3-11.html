IED Unit 3 • Lesson 3.11

Engineering Change & Final Revision

Focus question: How should an engineer revise a rocket when the physical assembly does not exactly match the CAD prediction?

Go to AssignmentProject RequirementsWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 3
Revision

Turn a real prototype observation into a controlled engineering change before flight.

Expected result: A final revised rocket design whose change is documented and reverified.

Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Write a specific problem statement from physical evidence.
  • Create an Engineering Change Record before editing final CAD.
  • Revise only the affected component or interface.
  • Re-manufacture and re-test changed parts before flight readiness.

Unit 3 Progression

The physical prototype is not the end of the design process; it provides evidence for the final controlled revision.

Compressed-Air Rocket Challenge

Project Requirements

These requirements apply throughout Lessons 3.6–3.14.

Launcher Interface

3/4 in PVC Reference Tube

  • The launcher tube outside diameter is 1.050 in (26.67 mm).
  • Model the launch tube as a separate grounded Fusion reference component.
  • You choose the rocket's internal launch clearance, but the finished rocket must slide freely over the actual PVC tube without binding.
  • The launcher itself is teacher equipment and is not modified as part of this project.
Printed Structure

Modular 3D-Printed Rocket

  • The structural rocket body is 3D printed in classroom-approved filament.
  • Use at least 3 major printed modules: a lower launch/fin module, a main body module, and a nose/upper module.
  • The rocket must have at least 2 removable printed-to-printed interfaces.
  • Major printed modules must be disassembled without damaging the parts; permanent adhesive is not allowed between major modules.
Student Choice

Connection Method

  • You choose how major modules connect.
  • Examples include keyed/slip couplers with retention, twist-lock/bayonet features, screws/inserts, snap-fit features, or another approved method.
  • The connection must keep the rocket aligned and secure during handling and flight-readiness checks.
  • If a connection uses hardware or a tool, it must still be serviceable without damaging the printed parts.
Fins

Laser-Cut Fin Assembly

  • Use 3 or 4 identical fins, equally spaced at 120° or 90°.
  • Fins are laser-cut from 1/8 in acrylic or plywood.
  • Use 3.175 mm as the nominal CAD thickness, then measure the actual stock and use that measurement for the final fin interface.
  • Fins must use a repeatable locating feature such as slots, tabs, keyed pockets, or another approved feature. Surface gluing alone is not an acceptable locating method.
  • Adhesive may be used to retain the fins after the fit/alignment check is approved.
Material Efficiency

Lightweight by Design

  • Primary body walls should generally be 1.2–2.0 mm thick; 1.6 mm is the recommended target.
  • Geometry thicker than 2.5 mm should be limited to local interfaces, couplers, fin mounts, fastener features, or other justified reinforcement.
  • Total predicted 3D-printed material should be 100 g or less.
  • The finished rocket should have a total mass of 125 g or less before the flight-readiness review.
Manufacturing & Safety

Build for the Classroom Process

  • Every printed component must fit the classroom printer build volume and be designed to print without support material.
  • No support material may be required inside the launch bore or any mating interface.
  • The nose must be rounded/blunt, and exposed fin corners/edges must be made safe before flight.
  • Only the instructor operates, pressurizes, or changes settings on the compressed-air launcher.
Assignment

Document, Revise, and Re-Test the Rocket

Use the strongest issue from Lesson 3.10. Even a rocket that assembles successfully should identify one evidence-based improvement or confirm that no dimensional change is required.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Select the issue or improvement: Choose a launcher-fit, module-interface, fin-fit, alignment, mass, or manufacturability finding supported by physical evidence.
  2. Document the current condition: Identify the affected component(s), measured/observed condition, and why it matters.
  3. Propose the engineering change: State the exact dimensional, geometric, interface, or material-use change before editing CAD.
  4. Complete the Engineering Change Record: Record the reason, affected part, proposed change, and expected result.
  5. Revise the correct component: Update the final assembly version rather than an outdated copy.
  6. Repeat digital checks: Re-run the relevant interference, section, measurement, symmetry, mass, or support-free review.
  7. Re-manufacture only what changed: Export and fabricate the revised component(s) when needed.
  8. Repeat the physical fit test: Verify the revision improved the intended condition without creating a new problem.
  9. Freeze the final flight version: Assign the final revision and stop making unrecorded changes.

Before You Submit

  • The change is based on physical evidence.
  • The ECR was completed before final CAD editing.
  • Only the intended component/interface changed.
  • The revised design was digitally rechecked.
  • Changed parts were physically re-tested when required.
  • The final flight version is clearly identified.
Submission

What You Will Submit

  • Engineering Change Record: final ECR/revision documentation.
  • Final revised Fusion archive: LastName_FirstName_3_11_AirRocket_Final.f3d.
  • Before/after evidence: photos or screenshots showing the original issue and revised result.
