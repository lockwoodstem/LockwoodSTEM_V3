IED Unit 3 • Lesson 3.13

Flight Readiness Review & Final Design Package

Focus question: What evidence must a rocket show before it is safe and ready for a controlled flight test?

Go to AssignmentProject RequirementsWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 3
Flight Readiness

Prove that the physical rocket and final documentation represent the same approved design and are ready for teacher-controlled launch.

Expected result: An instructor-approved flight-ready rocket and complete final design package.

Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Perform a structured preflight inspection.
  • Verify the rocket still matches the final Fusion revision.
  • Confirm launcher fit, mass, fin security, module retention, and safe external geometry.
  • Compile the final engineering evidence before launch.

Unit 3 Progression

No rocket advances to Lesson 3.14 until it passes this readiness gate.

Compressed-Air Rocket Challenge

Project Requirements

These requirements apply throughout Lessons 3.6–3.14.

Launcher Interface

3/4 in PVC Reference Tube

  • The launcher tube outside diameter is 1.050 in (26.67 mm).
  • Model the launch tube as a separate grounded Fusion reference component.
  • You choose the rocket's internal launch clearance, but the finished rocket must slide freely over the actual PVC tube without binding.
  • The launcher itself is teacher equipment and is not modified as part of this project.
Printed Structure

Modular 3D-Printed Rocket

  • The structural rocket body is 3D printed in classroom-approved filament.
  • Use at least 3 major printed modules: a lower launch/fin module, a main body module, and a nose/upper module.
  • The rocket must have at least 2 removable printed-to-printed interfaces.
  • Major printed modules must be disassembled without damaging the parts; permanent adhesive is not allowed between major modules.
Student Choice

Connection Method

  • You choose how major modules connect.
  • Examples include keyed/slip couplers with retention, twist-lock/bayonet features, screws/inserts, snap-fit features, or another approved method.
  • The connection must keep the rocket aligned and secure during handling and flight-readiness checks.
  • If a connection uses hardware or a tool, it must still be serviceable without damaging the printed parts.
Fins

Laser-Cut Fin Assembly

  • Use 3 or 4 identical fins, equally spaced at 120° or 90°.
  • Fins are laser-cut from 1/8 in acrylic or plywood.
  • Use 3.175 mm as the nominal CAD thickness, then measure the actual stock and use that measurement for the final fin interface.
  • Fins must use a repeatable locating feature such as slots, tabs, keyed pockets, or another approved feature. Surface gluing alone is not an acceptable locating method.
  • Adhesive may be used to retain the fins after the fit/alignment check is approved.
Material Efficiency

Lightweight by Design

  • Primary body walls should generally be 1.2–2.0 mm thick; 1.6 mm is the recommended target.
  • Geometry thicker than 2.5 mm should be limited to local interfaces, couplers, fin mounts, fastener features, or other justified reinforcement.
  • Total predicted 3D-printed material should be 100 g or less.
  • The finished rocket should have a total mass of 125 g or less before the flight-readiness review.
Manufacturing & Safety

Build for the Classroom Process

  • Every printed component must fit the classroom printer build volume and be designed to print without support material.
  • No support material may be required inside the launch bore or any mating interface.
  • The nose must be rounded/blunt, and exposed fin corners/edges must be made safe before flight.
  • Only the instructor operates, pressurizes, or changes settings on the compressed-air launcher.
Flight Approval Gate

No approval = no launch.

The instructor controls the launcher and determines whether a rocket is safe and ready to test. A failed readiness item is a revision opportunity, not permission to bypass the requirement.

Assignment

Complete the Flight Readiness Inspection

Inspect the final physical rocket against the approved CAD and project requirements. Correct any failed item before the flight test.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Identify the final revision: Confirm the physical parts match the final Lesson 3.11 CAD revision and the Lesson 3.12 drawing/BOM.
  2. Check finished mass: Verify the complete rocket is 125 g or less.
  3. Check launcher fit: Under instructor supervision and with the launcher unpressurized, verify smooth fit on the 1.050 in / 26.67 mm OD PVC tube.
  4. Check module retention: Verify both removable printed interfaces are fully engaged and cannot separate during normal handling.
  5. Check fin security: Confirm all fins are identical, equally spaced, aligned, and securely retained.
  6. Check external safety: Confirm the nose is blunt/rounded, sharp fin corners have been made safe, and there are no exposed hazards.
  7. Check structural condition: Look for cracks, delamination, loose hardware, damaged couplers, or other defects.
  8. Check serviceability: Demonstrate that the major printed modules can still be disassembled without damage after the inspection.
  9. Review documentation: Confirm the BOM, exploded view, ECR, manufacturing files, prototype evidence, and final CAD all describe the same rocket.
  10. Receive instructor approval: Only instructor-approved rockets proceed to the pressurized flight test.

Before You Submit

  • Rocket matches the final CAD revision.
  • Finished mass is within the project limit.
  • Launcher fit passes without binding.
  • Major modules are secure but still serviceable.
  • Fins are secure and aligned.
  • No cracks, sharp hazards, or loose hardware remain.
  • The final design package is complete.
  • Instructor flight approval has been received.
Submission

What You Will Submit

  • Flight-readiness checklist: completed and instructor-approved.
  • Final design package: final Fusion assembly, assembly drawing/BOM, ECR, prototype evidence, and manufacturing files.
  • Flight-ready physical rocket: presented for instructor inspection.
