Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Translate the challenge constraints into an assembly architecture.
  • Plan at least three major printed modules and two removable interfaces.
  • Select a connection strategy without being given one required solution.
  • Plan the 21.34 mm launcher interface and laser-cut fin system as real assembly interfaces.

Unit 3 Progression

Lessons 3.1–3.5 provided controlled assembly practice. The rest of Unit 3 applies those skills to a student-designed compressed-air rocket that will be physically flight-tested.

Compressed-Air Rocket Challenge

Project Requirements

These requirements apply throughout Lessons 3.6–3.14.

Launcher Interface

1/2 in PVC Reference Tube

  • The launcher tube outside diameter is 0.840 in (21.34 mm).
  • Model the launch tube as a separate grounded Fusion reference component.
  • You choose the rocket's internal launch clearance, but the finished rocket must slide freely over the actual PVC tube without binding.
  • Before choosing the rocket's internal bore dimension, measure the actual unpressurized classroom launch tube. The 0.840 in (21.34 mm) diameter is the nominal outside-diameter reference, not the target bore size.
  • The launcher itself is teacher equipment and is not modified as part of this project.
Printed Structure

Modular 3D-Printed Rocket

  • The structural rocket body is 3D printed in classroom-approved filament.
  • Use at least 3 major printed modules: a lower launch/fin module, a main body module, and a nose/upper module.
  • The rocket must have at least 2 removable printed-to-printed interfaces.
  • Major printed modules must be disassembled without damaging the parts; permanent adhesive is not allowed between major modules.
Student Choice

Connection Method

  • You choose how major modules connect.
  • Examples include keyed/slip couplers with retention, twist-lock/bayonet features, screws/inserts, snap-fit features, or another approved method.
  • The connection must keep the rocket aligned and secure during handling and flight-readiness checks.
  • If a connection uses hardware or a tool, it must still be serviceable without damaging the printed parts.
Fins

Laser-Cut Fin Assembly

  • Use 3 or 4 identical fins, equally spaced at 120° or 90°.
  • Fins are laser-cut from 1/8 in acrylic or plywood.
  • Use 3.175 mm as the nominal CAD thickness, then measure the actual stock and use that measurement for the final fin interface.
  • Fins must use a repeatable locating feature such as slots, tabs, keyed pockets, or another approved feature. Surface gluing alone is not an acceptable locating method.
  • Adhesive may be used to retain the fins after the fit/alignment check is approved.
Material Efficiency

Lightweight by Design

  • Primary body walls should generally be 1.2–2.0 mm thick; 1.6 mm is the recommended target.
  • Geometry thicker than 2.5 mm should be limited to local interfaces, couplers, fin mounts, fastener features, or other justified reinforcement.
  • Total predicted 3D-printed material should be 100 g or less.
  • The finished rocket should have a total mass of 125 g or less before the flight-readiness review.
Manufacturing & Safety

Build for the Classroom Process

  • Every printed component must fit the classroom printer build volume and be designed to print without support material.
  • No support material may be required inside the launch bore or any mating interface.
  • The nose must be rounded/blunt, and exposed fin corners/edges must be made safe before flight.
  • Only the instructor operates, pressurizes, or changes settings on the compressed-air launcher.
Assignment

Plan the Modular Rocket Assembly

Create the architecture for a rocket that is fully 3D printed except for laser-cut fins and that can be disassembled after manufacturing.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Define the major modules: Plan at least three printed modules: a lower launch/fin module, a main body module, and a nose/upper module. You may use additional printed couplers or retainers.
  2. Plan two removable interfaces: Decide how the nose/upper module connects to the body and how the body connects to the lower module. Major printed sections may not be permanently glued together.
  3. Select a connection concept: Choose an approved method such as a keyed coupler, twist-lock/bayonet, fasteners/inserts, snap-fit feature, or another serviceable connection.
  4. Plan the launch interface: Add the 1/2 in PVC launcher as a 21.34 mm OD reference and identify the rocket region that will slide over it.
  5. Choose 3 or 4 fins: Plan identical fins at 120° or 90° spacing. Identify whether you will use 1/8 in acrylic or plywood.
  6. Design the fin locating strategy: Show how slots, tabs, pockets, or another repeatable feature will control fin position and angle.
  7. Plan lightweight construction: Use approximately 1.6 mm body walls and identify only the locations that need local reinforcement.
  8. Estimate manufacturability: Confirm each printed module can fit the classroom printer and can be printed without support material.
  9. Sketch the assembly: Create an annotated concept showing the modules, connections, fins, and launcher interface.
  10. Get approval: Do not begin final CAD until the architecture has been approved.

Before You Submit

  • At least three major printed modules are shown.
  • At least two removable interfaces are planned.
  • The 21.34 mm launch tube interface is identified.
  • The fin count, material, spacing, and locating feature are defined.
  • Wall thickness and reinforcement are reasonable.
  • The concept can be manufactured without support material.
Submission

What to Submit

Submit only the following assignment-specific deliverables in Google Classroom:

  • Annotated rocket concept (one PDF): LastName_FirstName_3_6_Concept.pdf. Show front and isometric sketches labeling the nose/upper module, body, lower/fin module, both removable interfaces, 3 or 4 fins, and launch-tube opening.
  • Connection and manufacturing plan (included in same PDF): identify the connection method for both detachable interfaces; name the fin material (1/8 in acrylic or plywood), printed module list, and a brief plan for locating/retaining fins.
  • Design-approval checkpoint: include a clearly labeled space for instructor feedback and approval; no final Fusion model is due for Lesson 3.6.
