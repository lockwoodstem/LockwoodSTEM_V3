Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

A final presentation should defend design decisions with evidence.

Key Idea 2

The prototype, CAD assembly, and drawings should tell one coherent story.

Key Idea 3

Strong presentations explain what changed and why it matters.

Your Task

Present the Final Rocket Assembly

Communicate the final design, physical prototype, documentation, testing evidence, and revisions in a concise engineering presentation.

  1. Introduce the assembly goal and major design choices.
  2. Show the CAD assembly, exploded view, and technical drawing evidence.
  3. Demonstrate the physical prototype and explain fit results.
  4. State one revision, limitation, and recommended next step.

Quality check: The presentation uses visible evidence for each major claim and every team member understands the final design.

Lesson Interactive

Build the Final Engineering Story

Arrange the presentation sections so the audience can follow the team’s decisions from need through final evidence.

Presentation Evidence

Use a logical engineering story rather than listing files in the order they were created.

Audience goal: Understand the need, the design response, the physical result, and how testing improved the final assembly.

Put the Sections in Order

  1. Physical prototype and CAD assembly
  2. Final design package and conclusion
  3. Design need and success criteria
  4. Revisions made from evidence
  5. Key design decisions and exploded view
  6. Fit, alignment, and tolerance test results
Start with the design need and finish with the evidence-supported final assembly.
