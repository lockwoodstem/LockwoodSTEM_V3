IED Unit 3 • Lesson 3.10

Prototype Assembly & Fit Test

Focus question: What does a physical prototype reveal that cannot be confirmed from CAD alone?

Go to AssignmentWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 3
Prototype

Assemble the printed rocket prototype and collect specific physical fit evidence.

Expected result: A physically assembled prototype with documented fit, alignment, and assembly observations.

Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Compare manufactured parts with the digital assembly.
  • Evaluate physical fit at critical interfaces.
  • Identify assembly problems caused by geometry, manufacturing, or process.
  • Document evidence before making design changes.

Unit 3 Progression

The verified digital assembly is now tested against real manufactured parts.

ComponentsAssembleVerifyPrototypeReviseDocument
Assignment

Build and Inspect the Physical Rocket Prototype

Use the printed components and assigned classroom parts to assemble the rocket prototype. Do not modify CAD yet; first collect evidence.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Inspect the printed parts: Check for warping, incomplete features, damaged edges, or other manufacturing defects before assembly.
  2. Match parts to the CAD: Confirm each printed component corresponds to the current assembly version.
  3. Assemble in the planned order: Follow the assembly plan from Lesson 3.6 and avoid forcing parts together.
  4. Evaluate each interface: Record whether connections are correct, too tight, too loose, misaligned, or blocked by interference.
  5. Check overall alignment: Inspect the rocket from multiple directions and verify major components share the intended centerline.
  6. Check repeated parts: Inspect fin spacing/orientation and any other repeated components.
  7. Photograph the prototype: Capture an assembled view and at least one image showing a meaningful fit observation.
  8. Separate design issues from print issues: Identify whether each problem should be fixed in CAD or through manufacturing/process correction.

Before You Submit

  • Printed parts were inspected before assembly.
  • No failed interface was forced together.
  • Critical fits and alignment were documented.
  • Design problems are distinguished from manufacturing defects.
  • Prototype photos clearly show the evidence.
Submission

What You Will Submit

  • Prototype evidence: assembled prototype photo plus close-up fit/alignment evidence.
  • Fit-test record: classify each critical interface and note any problem that requires action.
