IED Unit 3 • Lesson 3.7

Rocket Component Design & CAD Assembly

Focus question: How can component geometry and assembly relationships be designed together so the physical rocket will actually fit and function?

Go to AssignmentProject RequirementsWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 3
CAD Design

Build the complete rocket in Fusion as a controlled assembly rather than as one continuous solid.

Expected result: A component-based rocket assembly with modeled launcher reference, removable body interfaces, and accurately positioned fin components.

Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Create each major rocket module as an identifiable Fusion component.
  • Design removable printed-to-printed interfaces around intentional geometry.
  • Model laser-cut fins at measured stock thickness.
  • Use joints and controlled references to assemble the rocket.

Unit 3 Progression

Compressed-Air Rocket Challenge

Project Requirements

These requirements apply throughout Lessons 3.6–3.14.

Launcher Interface

3/4 in PVC Reference Tube

  • The launcher tube outside diameter is 1.050 in (26.67 mm).
  • Model the launch tube as a separate grounded Fusion reference component.
  • You choose the rocket's internal launch clearance, but the finished rocket must slide freely over the actual PVC tube without binding.
  • The launcher itself is teacher equipment and is not modified as part of this project.
Printed Structure

Modular 3D-Printed Rocket

  • The structural rocket body is 3D printed in classroom-approved filament.
  • Use at least 3 major printed modules: a lower launch/fin module, a main body module, and a nose/upper module.
  • The rocket must have at least 2 removable printed-to-printed interfaces.
  • Major printed modules must be disassembled without damaging the parts; permanent adhesive is not allowed between major modules.
Student Choice

Connection Method

  • You choose how major modules connect.
  • Examples include keyed/slip couplers with retention, twist-lock/bayonet features, screws/inserts, snap-fit features, or another approved method.
  • The connection must keep the rocket aligned and secure during handling and flight-readiness checks.
  • If a connection uses hardware or a tool, it must still be serviceable without damaging the printed parts.
Fins

Laser-Cut Fin Assembly

  • Use 3 or 4 identical fins, equally spaced at 120° or 90°.
  • Fins are laser-cut from 1/8 in acrylic or plywood.
  • Use 3.175 mm as the nominal CAD thickness, then measure the actual stock and use that measurement for the final fin interface.
  • Fins must use a repeatable locating feature such as slots, tabs, keyed pockets, or another approved feature. Surface gluing alone is not an acceptable locating method.
  • Adhesive may be used to retain the fins after the fit/alignment check is approved.
Material Efficiency

Lightweight by Design

  • Primary body walls should generally be 1.2–2.0 mm thick; 1.6 mm is the recommended target.
  • Geometry thicker than 2.5 mm should be limited to local interfaces, couplers, fin mounts, fastener features, or other justified reinforcement.
  • Total predicted 3D-printed material should be 100 g or less.
  • The finished rocket should have a total mass of 125 g or less before the flight-readiness review.
Manufacturing & Safety

Build for the Classroom Process

  • Every printed component must fit the classroom printer build volume and be designed to print without support material.
  • No support material may be required inside the launch bore or any mating interface.
  • The nose must be rounded/blunt, and exposed fin corners/edges must be made safe before flight.
  • Only the instructor operates, pressurizes, or changes settings on the compressed-air launcher.
Assignment

Build the Modular Rocket in Fusion

Create the complete rocket assembly while preserving the separation between manufactured components.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Create the launcher reference: Model a simple 26.67 mm diameter cylinder as a separate component and ground it. Use the teacher-designated insertion length or reference geometry.
  2. Design the lower module: Create the launch bore/sleeve and the fin-locating geometry in the lower rocket component.
  3. Design the main body module: Use a hollow lightweight shell with primary walls generally between 1.2 and 2.0 mm.
  4. Design the nose/upper module: Use a rounded/blunt nose profile and include the selected removable connection to the body.
  5. Create the second removable interface: Design the connection between the main body and lower module.
  6. Model the fins: Create one fin component using the actual measured acrylic/plywood thickness, then place 3 or 4 identical fin components at equal spacing.
  7. Use joints: Apply appropriate rigid relationships or other required assembly constraints so the rocket components stay in their intended positions.
  8. Check serviceability: Confirm the major modules can conceptually separate without trapped geometry or impossible assembly order.
  9. Check material use: Remove unnecessary solid material and reserve thicker geometry for interfaces and local reinforcement.
  10. Save an assembled checkpoint: Finish with the rocket fully assembled around the grounded launch-tube reference.

Before You Submit

  • The design is a true component assembly.
  • At least three major printed modules are separate components.
  • Two removable printed interfaces are modeled.
  • The launcher reference is 26.67 mm OD and is a separate grounded component.
  • Fins are separate components at the correct measured stock thickness.
  • Body walls are lightweight and local reinforcement is intentional.
Submission
