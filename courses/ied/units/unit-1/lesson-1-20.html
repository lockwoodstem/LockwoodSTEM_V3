Rocket Assembly Notes

Focus question: How do engineers write reassembly instructions that another team can follow?

Mission Focus

Write and test step-by-step instructions another team can follow correctly.

SequenceTestRevise

Mission Briefing

45-Minute Flight Plan

Use this page with the lesson presentation and your engineering notebook.

Learning Targets

  • Write ordered assembly steps using clear action verbs and consistent item names.
  • Add orientation cues, cautions, and completion checks where needed.
  • Use peer-test evidence to revise unclear instructions.

Success Looks Like

A different team can follow the sequence and verify each completed step.

Opening Prompt

What makes an assembly instruction more useful than “put the parts together”?

Think first, then be ready to share a short response that connects the question to engineering communication.

Reveal: why this matters

A useful instruction names the part, action, direction, connection, and completion check. It removes the need to guess what happens next.

Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

Assembly notes communicate sequence, orientation, connection, and cautions.

Key Idea 2

One clear action per numbered step reduces skipped or combined operations.

Key Idea 3

Testing reveals where readers need additional detail or verification cues.

Interactive Check

Which instruction is strongest?

APut it together correctly
BSlide Item 4 into Item 2 until the holes align, then verify the edges are flush
CDo the next part
DUse common sense
Reveal Answer

B — Slide Item 4 into Item 2 until the holes align, then verify the edges are flush.

The instruction identifies the parts, action, direction, and completion condition.

Student Task

Write Reassembly Instructions Another Team Can Follow

Transform the assembly record into a clear reassembly sequence and test it with another team.

  1. List the required tools, materials, and components before Step 1.
  2. Write numbered steps using one action per step and the established item names or numbers.
  3. Add orientation cues, cautions, and completion checks where mistakes are likely.
  4. Have another team perform a dry run, then revise the unclear steps.

Quality check: The instructions must be executable in order and include checks before any irreversible action.

Student Deliverables

Tested Rocket Reassembly Instructions

Submit one revised instruction set and one testing note.

  • One final tools list and numbered reassembly sequence with orientation and quality checks.
  • One peer-test note naming the confusion observed and the revision made to correct it.

Submission standard: Avoid vague verbs; name the part, action, direction, connection, and completion condition.
