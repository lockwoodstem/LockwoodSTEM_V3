Assembly Instructions & Technical Communication

Focus question: How can engineers write assembly instructions that another person can follow correctly without needing verbal help?

Class Flow

Work Through the Lesson

Today — Write, Test, Revise
  • Identify the major stages of reassembly before writing individual steps.
  • Write concise action-based instructions using consistent part names/item numbers.
  • Add diagrams or callouts where words alone may be unclear.
  • Have another student review or test a portion of the instructions.
  • Revise steps that caused hesitation, questions, or mistakes.
Core Ideas

What to Pay Attention To

Action first

Start steps with a clear action such as insert, align, attach, tighten, position, or verify.

Orientation

Use specific references—front/back, left/right, tab/slot, item number—instead of “like this.”

Verification

Include checkpoints that tell the assembler how to know a step was completed correctly.

Engineering Check

Stop and verify before moving on

If the instructions only make sense while you are standing next to the reader explaining them, they are not finished.

Your Task

Write Reassembly Instructions Another Student Can Use

Use your existing technical documentation as the source of truth. Your instructions should make the reassembly process repeatable.

  1. Outline the major assembly stages.
  2. Write numbered action steps.
  3. Reference item numbers or exact component names.
  4. Add orientation and verification details.
  5. Peer-test the instructions and revise unclear language.

Use evidence: Keep sketches, measurements, observations, and revisions visible. Unit 1 is building one connected engineering documentation package.

What You Will Submit

Rocket Assembly Instructions

  • Numbered assembly sequence.
  • Consistent component names/item numbers.
  • Necessary orientation, fastener, and verification notes.
  • Evidence of at least one revision after peer review.

Before submitting: Check that your work is labeled, readable, complete, and consistent with the technical conventions used in this lesson.
