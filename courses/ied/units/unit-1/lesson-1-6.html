IED Unit 1 • Lesson 1.6

Isometric Circles, Arcs, and Shading

Focus question: How can engineers show curved features and surface depth in an isometric sketch?

Download PresentationFlight Plan3D ModelsInteractiveWhat You SubmitBack to Unit 1
Mission Focus

Represent curved forms as ellipses and arcs, then use consistent shading to show depth.

ConstructCurveShade

Curved features align with their surfaces and the shading follows one clear light direction.

Mission Briefing

45-Minute Flight Plan

Use this page with the lesson presentation and your engineering notebook.

Learning Targets

  • Represent isometric circles as ellipses on the correct face.
  • Connect arcs and straight edges with smooth, believable transitions.
  • Use light, medium, and dark values consistently to communicate surface direction.

Success Looks Like

The curved geometry looks attached to the part and the shading strengthens rather than hides the form.

Opening Prompt

Why does a circular feature usually look like an ellipse in an isometric sketch?

Think first, then be ready to share a short response that connects the question to engineering communication.

Reveal: why this matters

The circular feature is viewed on an angled face, so its apparent shape is compressed into an ellipse while still representing a true circle on the part.

Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

Circles usually appear as ellipses in isometric view.

Key Idea 2

Curved features must be placed on the correct isometric face.

Key Idea 3

Consistent values help the viewer distinguish surface direction and depth.

Student Task

Sketch Curves and Show Surface Depth

Choose one of the two Lesson 1.6 models and create an isometric study of its curved and flat surfaces.

  1. Rotate both models and select the one with the most useful curved-feature challenge.
  2. Block in the overall form and construct circles as ellipses on the correct faces.
  3. Refine the transitions where curved surfaces meet straight edges.
  4. Choose one light direction and apply light, medium, and dark values consistently.

Quality check: Ellipses must align with their surfaces, and the shading must follow the stated light direction.

Student Deliverables

Curved Isometric and Shading Study

Submit one completed model study and one concise explanation.

  • One refined isometric sketch showing the curved construction and three-value shading.
  • A light-direction arrow and one sentence explaining how a circle appears in isometric view.

Submission standard: Keep object edges readable and use shading to support the geometry rather than cover it.
