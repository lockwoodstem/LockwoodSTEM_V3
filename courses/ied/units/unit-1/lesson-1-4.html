Interactive Check

Which statement best describes line conventions?

AThey decorate a drawing
BThey tell the viewer what kind of feature each line represents
CThey are optional once a sketch is neat
DThey only matter in CAD
Reveal Answer

B — they tell the viewer what kind of feature each line represents.

Line conventions reduce guessing by showing visible edges, hidden features, center locations, and symmetry consistently.

Student Task

Locate Centers and Axes of Symmetry

Use the Lesson 1.4 model and drawings to identify the center of the circular feature and any axes of symmetry. Then correct or complete the views using standard centerline conventions.

  1. Identify the view in which the circular feature is visible as a circle and mark its exact center.
  2. Add crossing centerlines using the long-short line pattern and extend them slightly beyond the circular feature.
  3. Examine the other views and determine where the same axis should appear as a centerline through hidden geometry.
  4. Mark any overall line of symmetry and explain whether the entire part or only one feature is symmetrical.

Quality check: Centerlines must locate an actual center or axis and must not be confused with hidden lines.

Student Deliverables

Centerline and Symmetry Study

Your evidence should show both correct drafting technique and an understanding of what is symmetrical.

  • One completed view with a center mark and correctly patterned centerlines
  • A second view showing the same feature axis from another direction
  • A written statement identifying whether the part, a feature, or both are symmetrical
  • One annotation explaining how the centerline would help with dimensioning, machining, or inspection

Submission standard: Centerlines should cross at the true feature center and extend only far enough to remain easy to read.
