Adhesives & Fasteners

Focus question: How do engineers decide the best way to connect parts together?

Mission Briefing

Today’s Plan

Use this page with the lesson presentation and your engineering notebook.

Learning Targets

  • Distinguish permanent, removable, and adjustable connection methods.
  • Compare adhesives and fasteners using relevant design criteria.
  • Predict a possible connection failure and propose a prevention step.

Success Looks Like

The selected method fits the materials, access, loading, and future service needs of the joint.

LaunchLearnApplyCheckSubmit
Opening Prompt

Why might the strongest adhesive still be the wrong choice for a connection?

Think first, then be ready to share a short response that connects the question to engineering communication.

Reveal: why this matters

The joint may need to be inspected, repaired, or disassembled. Material compatibility, access, cure time, mass, and serviceability can matter as much as maximum strength.

Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

Connection methods may be permanent, removable, adjustable, or temporary.

Key Idea 2

Adhesives and fasteners perform differently depending on materials and joint conditions.

Key Idea 3

A good selection balances strength with access, assembly, inspection, and repair needs.

Your Task

Select a Connection Method for the Rocket

Evaluate one connection between two rocket components and recommend an appropriate adhesive or fastener.

  1. Identify the materials, expected direction of force, available access, and whether the joint must be removable.
  2. Compare at least three joining options in a simple criteria table.
  3. Sketch the selected joint and show the fastener location or adhesive area.
  4. Identify one likely failure mode and a step that would reduce the risk.

Quality check: The recommendation must connect each important joint requirement to evidence from the comparison.

What You Will Submit

Adhesive and Fastener Decision

Submit one comparison package and one recommendation.

  • One criteria table paired with a labeled sketch of the selected joint.
  • One paragraph justifying the choice and explaining one failure-prevention step.

Submission standard: Do not choose a permanent joint without considering future inspection or disassembly needs.
