IED Unit 1 • Lesson 1.7 • 1–2 class periods

Joining Methods & Assembly Systems

Focus question: How do engineers choose joining methods that make an assembly strong, serviceable, manufacturable, and appropriate for its purpose?

Start the LessonDecision FactorsWhat You SubmitBack to Unit 1
Mission Focus

Inspect real connections, classify how parts are joined, and justify why one joining method may be better than another for a specific interface.

IdentifyCompareJustify

Success: You can explain not only what joins two parts, but why that joining method makes sense for the job.

Mission Briefing

What You Are Learning

Joining methods are engineering decisions. The best connection depends on what the joint must do, how the product will be manufactured, and whether it must ever come apart.

Learning Targets

  • Identify common mechanical fasteners, adhesives, friction/press fits, tabs, slots, and snap-fit connections.
  • Distinguish removable, semi-permanent, and permanent joints.
  • Explain how strength, alignment, serviceability, cost, access, and manufacturing affect joint selection.
  • Identify the components on each side of an interface and the function of the connection.
  • Recommend an appropriate joining method and support the choice with engineering reasoning.

Success Looks Like

Your analysis connects the physical joint to its engineering purpose. You can compare alternatives and explain what would improve or worsen if the joining method changed.

Core Ideas

A Joint Is More Than the Fastener You Can See

Mechanical Fasteners

Designed for assembly and service

Screws, bolts, nuts, washers, rivets, pins, and similar hardware can provide alignment, clamping, retention, or load transfer. Some are removable; others are effectively permanent.

Bonded & Formed Joints

Fewer separate pieces

Adhesives, press fits, snap fits, tabs, and slots can reduce hardware and simplify assembly, but may change serviceability, strength, or manufacturing requirements.

Interfaces

Where parts interact

An interface is the region where components meet, locate, transfer force, allow motion, seal, or otherwise interact. The joining method must support that function.

Engineering Selection

What Should Drive the Joining Decision?

Load

What force, torque, vibration, impact, or repeated use must the joint survive?

Serviceability

Does the product need to be opened, repaired, adjusted, or have components replaced?

Manufacturing

Can the joint be assembled consistently with the available tools, materials, access, and production process?

Alignment

Does the connection need to hold parts in a precise location or orientation?

Cost & Complexity

Does the chosen method add unnecessary hardware, labor, steps, or specialized processes?

Permanence

Should the joint come apart easily, only with tools, or not at all during normal use?

Your Task

Connection Decision Lab

Inspect the instructor-provided assemblies, sample joints, or rocket interfaces. For each assigned connection, document what is being joined and evaluate whether the joining method is appropriate.

  1. Identify the two components or features that meet at the interface.
  2. Identify the joining method and any hardware used.
  3. Classify the connection as removable, semi-permanent, or permanent.
  4. Describe what the connection must accomplish: hold, align, transfer load, allow motion, seal, or another function.
  5. Identify the most important selection factors for that joint.
  6. Compare the existing joining method with at least one reasonable alternative.
  7. Recommend which joining method you would use and justify the decision with evidence.

Engineering rule: “It is strong” is not enough. A good justification connects the joining method to the actual requirements of the interface.

Connection to the Unit 1 Challenge

Document the Interface, Not Just the Hardware

In the final Unit 1 challenge, you will need to communicate how parts relate to one another. Record the joint type, the components it connects, and the reason the connection works so that another engineer can understand the assembly without relying on verbal explanation.
