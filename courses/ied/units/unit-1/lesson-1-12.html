Why Documentation Matters

Opening Prompt

How can an engineer know whether documentation is actually clear?

Think first, then be ready to share a short response that connects the question to engineering communication.

Reveal: why this matters

The document must be tested by someone who did not create it. Their errors, pauses, and questions reveal missing or ambiguous information.

Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

Documentation preserves what a design is and how work was completed.

Key Idea 2

A usable document must make sense to someone who was not present.

Key Idea 3

Testing and revision improve clarity more reliably than assuming the instructions are clear.

Lesson Interactive

Documentation Stress Test

Choose the instruction that another team could execute without asking a follow-up question, then identify the main ambiguity in the weaker document.

Document A

“Put the small connector back into the body and tighten it until it looks right.”

Document B

“Insert Item 4 into the aft end of Item 2 with the keyed tab facing the marked reference line. Turn the retaining ring clockwise until hand-tight, then confirm the tab remains aligned.”

Strong documentation names the part, reference direction, action, and verification condition.
Your Task

Test Whether Documentation Can Stand Alone

Create a short drawing-and-instruction set for a simple assembly or arrangement, then test it with a partner.

  1. Prepare an initial document without asking the partner for help.
  2. Remain silent while the partner attempts to follow it.
  3. Record each hesitation, incorrect action, or request for information.
  4. Revise the document to resolve the observed problems.

Quality check: The revision must directly address the ambiguity revealed by the test.

What You Will Submit

Documentation Test and Revision

Submit one before-and-after package and one feedback summary.

  • The original and revised versions of the drawing or instruction set, kept together for comparison.
  • A short tester-feedback summary naming the most important revision and why it was needed.

Submission standard: Do not erase the original version; it is evidence of the improvement process.
