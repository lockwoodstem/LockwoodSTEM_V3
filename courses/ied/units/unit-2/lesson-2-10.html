Puzzle Cube Concept Design & Sketching

Focus question: How can an engineer communicate a three-dimensional puzzle design clearly enough to build it accurately in CAD?

Go to AssignmentWhat to SubmitBack to Unit 2
20 Points • 1 Class Period

Design a five-piece puzzle cube on paper before opening Fusion.

Expected result: A complete color-coded plan showing how all 27 unit cubes are divided among five connected puzzle pieces.

Objective

Translate a 3D idea into a clear color-coded isometric sketch. Represent each puzzle piece separately while preserving its 3D geometry. Check a concept against measurable design constraints before CAD modeling.

Materials

For the online work: a browser and your engineering notebook. Follow the activity’s materials list for any physical build, and get teacher approval before using equipment.

Puzzle Cube Challenge

Project Requirements

These requirements apply throughout Lessons 2.10–2.13.

Finished Cube

3 × 3 × 3 Unit Cube

  • 27 total unit cubes.
  • Each unit cube is exactly 20 mm × 20 mm × 20 mm.
  • The assembled puzzle must be 60 mm × 60 mm × 60 mm.
  • The final puzzle contains exactly 5 pieces.
Piece Geometry

Five Connected Pieces

  • Each piece contains at least 3 unit cubes.
  • Every piece must be one continuous solid with cubes connected face-to-face.
  • At least 1 of the 5 pieces must span at least 2 unit cubes in X, Y, and Z.
  • Pieces may repeat the same shape.
Color & Printing

Plan for Manufacturing

  • Use five different colors consistently in the sketch, Fusion model, and final printed puzzle.
  • Each piece must have a practical flat-face orientation that can print without support material.
  • No additional clearance is required; use the exact 20 mm unit geometry.
Example

Notice: The colors make the five pieces easy to distinguish while the completed form still reads as one 3 × 3 × 3 cube. Your design should use the same idea with your own five-piece arrangement.

Assignment

Design Your Five-Piece Puzzle Cube

Create a paper design for a 3 × 3 × 3 puzzle cube made from five separate interlocking-style pieces. You will use this drawing as the blueprint for the Fusion model in Lesson 2.11.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Draw the full cube: Create a 3 × 3 × 3 isometric cube grid. The grid represents all 27 unit cubes in the finished puzzle.
  2. Divide the cube into five pieces: Decide which unit cubes belong to Piece A, B, C, D, and E. Every one of the 27 unit cubes must belong to exactly one piece.
  3. Choose five colors: Assign one distinct color to each piece. Use those same five colors throughout the entire Puzzle Cube Challenge.
  4. Color the assembled isometric: Clearly color each visible unit cube so the boundaries between the five pieces can be interpreted.
  5. Check the piece sizes: Count the unit cubes in each piece. Every piece must contain at least 3 unit cubes.
  6. Check connectivity: Confirm each piece is one continuous shape. Every unit cube in a piece must connect face-to-face to another cube in that same piece.
  7. Check the 3D-piece rule: At least one piece must extend at least 2 unit cubes in the X, Y, and Z directions. These pieces should occupy at least a 2 × 2 × 2 bounding region, even though they do not need to fill it.
  8. Sketch each piece separately: Draw a separate isometric sketch for Pieces A–E. Use the same color as the assembled drawing and make the location of every unit cube clear.
  9. Perform a final count: Add the cube counts from all five pieces. The total must equal exactly 27.

Before You Submit

  • Your assembled drawing represents a 3 × 3 × 3 cube.
  • Exactly five colors and five pieces are shown.
  • All five individual piece sketches match the assembled design.
  • Every piece has at least 3 unit cubes and is face-connected.
  • At least one piece satisfies the X/Y/Z span requirement.
  • The piece cube counts total exactly 27.
Submission

What You Will Submit

  • Color-coded assembled isometric: one complete drawing of the 3 × 3 × 3 cube showing all five pieces.
  • Five individual piece sketches: one separate isometric sketch for Piece A, B, C, D, and E.
  • Piece cube counts: clearly label how many unit cubes are used in each of the five pieces.

File requirement: Combine your sketches into one clear PDF or scan named LastName_FirstName_2_10_PuzzleSketches.pdf. Keep the original sketches available because you will use them while modeling in Lesson 2.11.
