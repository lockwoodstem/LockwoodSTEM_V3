IED Unit 2 • Lesson 2.10

Condensation-Draining Coaster Mini-Project

Focus question: How can multiple CAD features work together to create a functional product that solves a simple everyday problem?

Go to AssignmentBack to Unit 2
Lesson Goal

Independently design a two-part coaster with a removable patterned insert and a lower condensation collection tray.

Expected result: A clean, editable, parametric CAD design that meets all project constraints and is ready for formal CAD review in Lesson 2.11.

Lesson Overview

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Plan an efficient modeling strategy before building.
  • Combine Extrude, Cut, Hole, Fillet/Chamfer, Mirror/Pattern, and other appropriate tools in one design.
  • Create two parts that fit together within a controlled design envelope.
  • Use repeated geometry to create a functional drainage pattern.
  • Build the design so critical dimensions and clearances can be revised efficiently.

How to Approach the Work

This is an independent CAD mini-project. Plan the feature sequence first, model deliberately, and verify dimensions before submission. There is more than one valid modeling strategy.

PlanModelVerifyRefineSubmit
Project Constraints

Two-Part Condensation Coaster

Required Limits

  • 80 mm maximum overall width or diameter.
  • 15 mm maximum total assembled thickness.
  • Two separate parts: lower collection tray and removable insert.
  • Patterned insert: repeated openings must allow condensation to drain.

Functional Requirements

  • Tray contains a real recessed collection volume.
  • Insert is supported securely but remains removable.
  • Insert remains structurally connected and can support a cup.
  • Repeated geometry is created efficiently with Mirror or Pattern.
  • Clearance between the two parts is intentionally modeled.
Assignment

Condensation-Draining Coaster Mini-Project

Design a two-part coaster that allows condensation to drain through a patterned insert into a lower collection tray. This is your independent CAD challenge for Lessons 2.1–2.9. You will review and revise the design in Lesson 2.11 before manufacturing.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Plan before modeling: Create a quick concept sketch showing the overall shape, lower tray, removable insert, drainage pattern, and the critical dimensions you intend to control.
  2. Stay inside the design envelope: Keep the complete coaster at or below 80 mm in overall width/diameter and 15 mm total assembled thickness.
  3. Model the collection tray: Create a stable base with a recessed collection area and a ledge, shoulder, or support surface for the insert.
  4. Model the removable insert: Create the insert as a separate body/component or separate design as directed. Include intentional clearance so it can be removed.
  5. Create a functional drainage pattern: Model one opening or pattern unit, then use Mirror, Rectangular Pattern, Circular Pattern, or another efficient repeated-feature method.
  6. Use design intent: Use dimensions, constraints, parameters, and dependent features so the model can be edited without unnecessary rebuilding.
  7. Add appropriate finishing features: Apply fillets or chamfers only where they improve function, safety, fit, or manufacturability.
  8. Verify digitally: Use Inspect/Measure to check the final footprint, assembled thickness, tray depth, insert dimensions, support ledge, and intended clearance between parts.

Before You Submit

  • The two parts meet the required size limits.
  • The tray includes a real collection volume beneath the insert.
  • The insert is removable and structurally connected.
  • Repeated openings use proper Mirror/Pattern features.
  • Critical dimensions and clearances are easy to find and edit.
  • The Timeline is logical and free of unnecessary features.
Submission

What You Will Submit

  • Concept sketch/modeling plan: overall shape, two-part strategy, drainage pattern, and critical dimensions.
  • Editable Fusion design: complete two-part coaster model.
  • Assembled screenshot: isometric view showing both parts in their intended relationship.
  • Insert screenshot: top view showing the repeated drainage pattern clearly.
  • Design verification: record final overall width/diameter, total assembled thickness, and intended insert-to-tray clearance.
  • CAD strategy note: identify one Mirror/Pattern feature and one parameter/dimension that would make the design easy to revise.

Important: Do not submit final manufacturing files yet. Lesson 2.11 is the formal CAD review and revision step before the design moves into additive manufacturing.
