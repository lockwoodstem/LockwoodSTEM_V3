IED Unit 2 · Lesson 2.10

Condensation-Draining Coaster Mini-Project

Essential question: How can multiple CAD features work together to create a functional product that solves a simple everyday problem?

Lesson goalDesign a two-part coaster that supports a drink while allowing condensation to drain into a collection tray below.
DeliverableTwo Fusion parts plus exported manufacturing files for instructor printing.
Design Brief Requirements CAD Workflow What You Submit

Project Overview

You will design a compact two-part coaster that separates the drinking surface from collected condensation.

The upper part is a removable patterned insert. Openings in the insert allow condensation to pass through. The lower part is a shallow tray that captures the water beneath the drink.

Your coaster will be printed by the instructor. You will receive the finished part and evaluate it in Lesson 2.11.

Important: You are responsible for the CAD design. The instructor will manufacture the model exactly as submitted whenever possible, so dimensional and design errors may appear in the physical print.

Design Brief

Design a two-part coaster that keeps a drinking cup above collected condensation. The design must include a removable upper insert and a lower collection tray.

80 mm maximumMaximum overall width or diameter.
15 mm maximumMaximum total assembled thickness.
2 printed partsOne outer tray and one removable insert.
No supports preferredDesign both parts for straightforward FDM printing.

How the Coaster Works

Required Design Features

Outer Condensation Tray

  • Must fit completely within the 80 mm maximum footprint.
  • Must contain a recessed area that can collect condensation.
  • Must include an internal ledge, shoulder, or other support surface for the insert.
  • Must have a stable flat bottom.
  • Must allow the insert to be removed without destroying either part.

Patterned Insert

  • Must support a cup or glass across the top surface.
  • Must include repeated openings that allow water to drain into the tray.
  • The pattern must be created using Mirror, Rectangular Pattern, Circular Pattern, or another appropriate repeated-feature method.
  • The mesh must remain connected and strong enough to print as one part.
  • The insert must fit inside or on top of the outer tray while remaining removable.

Optional Personalization

You may add a simple logo, initials, name, or short text in the center of the patterned insert. The personalized area may be raised or recessed, but it may not eliminate the drainage function or weaken the surrounding mesh.

Design Inspiration

These examples show different ways to solve the same design problem. Your coaster does not need to match any of them.

Do not copy an example feature-for-feature. Use the images to understand the functional requirements, then create your own pattern, geometry, and personalization.

Fusion Skills You May Need

Sketch Constraints Dimensions Extrude Extrude Cut Fillet Chamfer Mirror Rectangular Pattern Circular Pattern Text Inspect / Measure

Instructor Demonstration

  1. Set the overall design envelope and explain the 80 mm × 15 mm limits.
  2. Create a simple tray cross-section and identify the collection area and insert-support ledge.
  3. Create one example opening in an insert and repeat it with a pattern tool.
  4. Show how to leave a central solid region for optional text or a logo.
  5. Demonstrate how to check the assembled height and the insert-to-tray relationship with Inspect/Measure.
The instructor will not model a complete coaster. The demonstration covers techniques only. Your final geometry and pattern must be your own.

Recommended CAD Workflow

  1. Concept sketch: Decide on the overall shape, insert pattern, tray geometry, and optional personalization.
  2. Plan critical dimensions: Identify the outer size, tray depth, insert thickness, support ledge, and insert clearance.
  3. Model the tray: Create the lower condensation collection part first.
  4. Model the insert: Create the removable upper part as a separate body/component or separate design as directed by the instructor.
  5. Create the pattern: Build one opening or pattern unit and repeat it efficiently.
  6. Add personalization: Add optional text or a simple logo only after the functional geometry works.
  7. Verify: Use Inspect/Measure to confirm the final dimensions and assembled thickness.
  8. Export: Export both parts in the format required by the instructor for printing.

Before You Submit

  • Overall width or diameter is 80 mm or less.
  • Total assembled thickness is 15 mm or less.
  • Tray contains a functional condensation collection area.
  • Insert is removable and supported above the collection area.
  • Insert includes a repeated drainage pattern.
  • Pattern geometry is connected and printable.
  • Any center text/logo does not block most of the drainage area.
  • Critical sketches are fully constrained where appropriate.
  • Critical dimensions have been checked with Inspect/Measure.
  • Both parts are ready for instructor printing.

What You Submit

  1. Concept sketch or design plan.
  2. Fusion model containing both coaster parts.
  3. Isometric screenshot of the assembled design.
  4. Screenshot showing the patterned insert.
  5. Critical dimension check showing the maximum width/diameter and total assembled thickness.
  6. Exported manufacturing file for the outer tray.
  7. Exported manufacturing file for the insert.
  8. Short prediction: What feature of your design is most likely to need revision after the first print?

What Happens Next

The instructor will print your coaster before Lesson 2.11. When you receive the physical part, you will evaluate the fit of the insert, drainage function, print quality, strength, and overall usability. You will then revise the CAD model based on physical evidence.
