IED Unit 5 • Lesson 5.14

Ethics, Safety, Sustainability, and Life-Cycle Analysis

Focus question: How do engineers make responsible design decisions beyond simply making a prototype work?

45-Minute Flight Plan

Learning Targets

  • Identify ethical and safety considerations connected to the design.
  • Consider sustainability, materials, life cycle, and impact.
  • Recommend a responsible improvement or limitation statement.

Opening Prompt

How do engineers make responsible design decisions beyond simply making a prototype work?

Think first, then share one short response that connects today’s skill to a user need, stakeholder, design criterion, prototype decision, or piece of evidence.

Reveal: useful engineering move

Start by naming the evidence you need before deciding the work is ready. In Unit 5, strong capstone work is not just complete; it is user-centered, responsible, tested, revised, and communicated clearly.

Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

A working design is not automatically a responsible design.

Key Idea 2

Engineers consider who benefits, who may be affected, and what happens over the product life cycle.

Key Idea 3

Responsible design decisions should be stated clearly in the portfolio.

Engineering Workflow

Use These Moves

  1. Identify the user need, stakeholder, or responsible design decision.
  2. Use today’s lesson to collect or create meaningful evidence.
  3. Apply the capstone skill carefully: research, define, plan, model, build, test, revise, or present.
  4. Save the note, sketch, CAD view, feedback, test result, photo, or reflection that proves the work.
Student Task

Apply the Skill

Complete a responsibility check that addresses ethics, safety, sustainability, life cycle, and design impact.

Checkpoint: be ready to show evidence that connects the capstone work to the user need, criteria, feedback, testing, revision, responsibility, or final communication.

Interactive Check

Which action best supports today’s engineering goal?

AChoose the first idea because it is easiest to build
BUse today’s evidence to make a user-centered design decision
CMake the prototype look complete before checking the user need
DIgnore stakeholder feedback if it complicates the plan
Reveal Answer

B — Use today’s evidence to make a user-centered design decision.

B — Evaluate the design beyond function and name responsible improvements or limitations.

Evidence Challenge

What evidence would you need?

Claim: “My ethics, safety, sustainability, and life-cycle analysis work is accurate and ready to move forward.”

AA sketch, model, stakeholder note, plan, or prototype view that shows the work
BA statement that the team likes the idea
CRelevant criteria, feedback, test results, measurements, or observations
DA polished page with no evidence or explanation
Reveal Answer

A + C are the strongest evidence pair.

A shows what was created or observed. C shows whether it meets the user need, criterion, or responsible design claim. Vague confidence is not enough.

Scenario Response

What should the engineer do next?

A team working on this lesson notices that its evidence does not fully support the capstone decision it wants to make.

AIgnore the gap and keep the original conclusion
BRecord the gap, identify missing evidence, revise, and check again
CDelete the evidence and restart without notes
DChoose the faster option even if it cannot be justified
Reveal Answer

B — Record the gap, identify missing evidence, revise, and check again.

The strongest next step turns the problem into useful engineering evidence instead of hiding it or guessing.
