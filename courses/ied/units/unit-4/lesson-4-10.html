IED Lesson 4.10 · Circuits and Soldering

Introduction to Soldering

45 minutes · 10 points

Concept Practice check Activity Submission Final check

Today’s Goal

Make and evaluate two supervised solder joints on an unpowered practice board.

Learn and see a demonstration (10 min), work (30 min), then submit and clean up (5 min).

Learn the Concept

A solder joint connects conductors when molten solder wets both metal surfaces, meaning it spreads onto them. A fillet is the small shape the solder forms between the pin and pad. A bridge is an unintended connection between adjacent conductors. Look at how the solder meets each surface. A separate electrical check may still be needed.

Worked Example

Joint A
Solder spreads onto the lead and pad. The gap to the next pad is clear.
Joint B
A strand of solder crosses the gap to the next pad.
What to mark
A is ready to be checked. Flag B as a possible bridge.

Practice check

Make a choice and check your answer before you start the activity.

You see a thin strand of solder joining two neighboring pads that should be separate. What should you do?

What to Do

Safety: Use eye protection, working local fume extraction, and a heat-safe stand under direct instructor supervision. Use an unpowered board, never solder battery cells, let joints cool before handling, and wash hands after cleanup.

  1. Watch the demonstration. Ask your teacher to check your station and confirm you can handle the iron safely.
  2. Under direct supervision, make two joints on the supplied practice board. If the station is unavailable, inspect two sample joints.
  3. Label the two joints on a photo or sketch and record the instructor’s feedback or your inspection findings.

What to Submit

One labeled photo or sketch of two joints. Mark each as ready to be checked or needing rework, give one visible reason per joint, and identify your practice or inspection-only result.

Submit one file and include your name. Turn it in through Google Classroom for Lesson 4.10.

Check Your Work

Did you inspect both pin-to-pad contact and the gap to the next pad? Ask the instructor before any rework.

10-point rubric
  • 4 points · Completion. Include all requested parts of today’s submission.
  • 4 points · Engineering accuracy. Use correct concepts and labels. Support your result or decision with the evidence requested.
  • 2 points · Clarity. Make the evidence readable and include the required name or team names.

Finish the practice check to mark your work ready.
