IED Lesson 4.1 · Materials and Testing

Engineering Materials

45 minutes · 10 points

Concept Practice check Activity Submission Final check

Today’s Goal

Choose a material for a model part by matching one property to the part’s job.

Learn and see a demonstration (10 min), work (30 min), then submit and clean up (5 min).

Learn the Concept

Materials are what parts are made from. A property describes how a material behaves. Stiffness is resistance to changing shape under a load. A part’s shape and size affect how easily it bends. Strength describes how much stress a material can take before yielding or breaking. Mass tells you how much matter a sample contains. Compare samples of similar shape and size before drawing conclusions.

Worked Example

Compare
Same-sized plywood and foam board, pressed gently the same way.
Observe
The plywood bends less.
Choose
Use plywood for the support. Less bending helps hold the axle in place.

Practice check

Make a choice and check your answer before you start the activity.

Two equal-sized strips are pressed gently in the same way. Strip A bends less. What did you learn?

What to Do

Pick a material for a wheel support

The support holds an axle in place while the wheel turns. Use five labeled samples and a notebook or digital document to decide which sample you would use.

Safety: Use provided samples only. Check with the instructor before handling pieces with sharp edges or splinters. Keep the samples intact.

  1. Observe (10 min). Match each sample to its label. Look for features such as visible layers or surface texture. If permitted, gently compare how two similar-sized samples bend. Stop before either sample creases or cracks.
  2. Record (10 min). Make a two-column table labeled Sample and What I noticed. Add one specific observation for each of the five samples.
  3. Decide (10 min). Pick a sample for the wheel support. Use an observation from your table to explain which property helps keep the axle in place.

What to Submit

Submit one typed page or one clear photo of your notebook page through the Lesson 4.1 assignment in Google Classroom. Include your name, your five-row table, and one choice sentence below it.

Example table entry: Plywood | Bent less than the similar-sized foam-board strip.

Sentence starter: I would use ___ for the wheel support because I observed ___, which shows that its ___ would help ___.

Check Your Work

Does your choice point to a specific observation from your table? A gentle bending comparison gives evidence about how stiff the samples are. It does not show how much load they can take before they fail.

10-point rubric
  • 4 points · Completion. Include five named samples with an observation for each, plus your choice sentence.
  • 4 points · Engineering accuracy. Cite a real observation, identify the relevant property, and explain how it helps the support hold the axle in place.
  • 2 points · Clarity. Use readable column labels, include your name, and put everything in one file.

Finish the practice check to mark your work ready.
