Building a Motor Circuit

Build and test a switched low-voltage motor circuit.

Today's goal

Build and test a switched low-voltage motor circuit.

Class plan

10 minutes demonstration · 30 minutes guided work · 5 minutes submission and cleanup (adjusted for safe equipment shutdown).

Learn the concept

From electricity to motion

A DC motor converts electrical energy into rotary motion. A switch controls whether current flows. A geared motor uses a gear reduction to provide slower output rotation and greater torque than the motor's internal shaft. The battery holder voltage and current capability must match the approved motor.

Example: Battery positive connects through a switch to the motor, and the other motor lead returns to battery negative.

Remember: Inspect disconnected wiring before adding batteries. Never solder directly to battery cells.

What to do

  1. Set out the motor, switch and battery holder with power disconnected.
  2. Connect the circuit using the supplied diagram; only certified students solder under supervision.
  3. Have wiring inspected, briefly test the motor and demonstrate the switch.

Safety: Disconnect battery before making or changing any connection. Obtain instructor approval before power-on.
