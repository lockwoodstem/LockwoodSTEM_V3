IED Lesson 4.11 · Circuits and Soldering

Building a Motor Circuit

45 minutes · 10 points

Concept Activity Submission

Today’s Goal

Build and verify a low-voltage motor circuit controlled by a switch.

Learn and see a demonstration (10 min), work (30 min), then submit and clean up (5 min).

Learn the Concept

A DC motor converts electrical power into rotary motion. A geared motor uses a gear reduction to trade output speed for greater available torque, or turning effect. In a single-path circuit the switch goes in series with the motor, so opening it interrupts the motor current.

Worked Example

Follow the path from battery + to the switch, then to the motor, then back to battery −. With the circuit checked and power applied, ON should drive the unloaded shaft. In OFF, the shaft may coast briefly before stopping.

What to Do

Safety: Use approved low-voltage components only. Keep bare conductors apart, secure the motor, and keep clear of the shaft. Disconnect immediately if leads heat up or the motor stalls.

  1. Lay out the approved motor, switch, and matching power source with the source disconnected.
  2. Connect the switch in series using provided leads and have the instructor inspect the circuit.
  3. After approval, test ON and OFF once, note what happens, and disconnect power.

What to Submit

One annotated circuit sketch or photo showing the entire loop, plus one observed result for each switch position. If power was withheld, record the reason and the correction needed.

Submit one file and include your name. Turn it in through Google Classroom for Lesson 4.11.

Check Your Work

Do your results distinguish what actually happened in ON and OFF from what you predicted?

10-point rubric

  • 4 points · Completion. Include all requested parts of today’s submission.
  • 4 points · Engineering accuracy. Use correct concepts and labels. Support your result or decision with the evidence requested.
  • 2 points · Clarity. Make the evidence readable and include the required name or team names.
