IED Lesson 4.9 · Circuits and Soldering

Simple Circuits and Soldering Safety

45 minutes · 10 points

Concept Activity Submission

Today’s Goal

Explain how a switch controls a low-voltage motor circuit and identify its safety checks.

Learn and see a demonstration (10 min), work (30 min), then submit and clean up (5 min).

Learn the Concept

A circuit is a path for electric current. Current flows when the path is complete and the source is connected. A load, such as a motor, converts electrical energy to another form. An open switch breaks the path. Solder joins conductors at a connection by melting a filler metal.

Worked Example

Trace this loop: battery + → closed switch → motor → battery −. The motor can turn when the loop is closed. Open the switch and current through the motor stops. The motor may coast briefly even after it loses power.

What to Do

Safety: Observe soldering from the teacher’s designated area. Keep the power source disconnected during wiring. Hot tools require instructor supervision, eye protection, working local fume extraction, and a station check before use.

  1. Trace and draw the loop from the teacher’s demonstration circuit.
  2. Mark where the path breaks with the switch open and predict what the motor does in each state.
  3. Identify three pre-soldering checks at the teacher’s demo station: supervision, eye protection, and working local fume extraction.

What to Submit

One circuit diagram labeled with source, switch, and motor, with open and closed predictions and the three station checks written below it.

Submit one file and include your name. Turn it in through Google Classroom for Lesson 4.9.

Check Your Work

Can you trace the whole return path, and do you know who must approve the station before soldering starts?

10-point rubric

  • 4 points · Completion. Include all requested parts of today’s submission.
  • 4 points · Engineering accuracy. Use correct concepts and labels. Support your result or decision with the evidence requested.
  • 2 points · Clarity. Make the evidence readable and include the required name or team names.
