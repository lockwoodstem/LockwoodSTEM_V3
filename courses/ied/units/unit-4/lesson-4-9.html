IED Lesson 4.9 · Circuits and Soldering

Simple Circuits and Soldering Safety

45 minutes · 10 points

Concept Practice check Activity Submission

Today’s Goal

Explain how a switch controls a low-voltage motor circuit and identify its safety checks.

Learn and see a demonstration (10 min), work (30 min), then submit and clean up (5 min).

Learn the Concept

A circuit is a path for electric current. Current flows when the path is complete and the source is connected. A load, such as a motor, converts electrical energy to another form. An open switch breaks the path. Solder joins conductors at a connection by melting a filler metal.

Worked Example

Closed switch
Battery + → switch → motor → battery −
Open switch
The break stops current through the motor.
What you see
The motor can turn when the path is closed. It may coast briefly after the path opens.

Practice check

Make a choice and check your answer before you start the activity.

What happens to the motor circuit when you open the switch?

What to Do

Safety: Watch the soldering demonstration from the marked viewing area. Keep the power source disconnected during wiring. Hot tools require instructor supervision, eye protection, working local fume extraction, and a station check before use.

  1. Trace and draw the loop from the demonstration circuit.
  2. Mark where the path breaks with the switch open and predict what the motor does in each state.
  3. Identify three pre-soldering checks at the demonstration station: supervision, eye protection, and working local fume extraction.

What to Submit

One circuit diagram labeled with source, switch, and motor, with open and closed predictions and the three station checks written below it.

Submit one file and include your name. Turn it in through Google Classroom for Lesson 4.9.

10-point rubric
  • 4 points · Completion. Include all requested parts of today’s submission.
  • 4 points · Engineering accuracy. Use correct concepts and labels. Support your result or decision with the evidence requested.
  • 2 points · Clarity. Make the evidence readable and include the required name or team names.
Optional extension · up to +2 points

Diagnose an open circuit on paper

Copy your closed circuit sketch and mark a single broken wire. Predict whether the motor receives current, then trace where the path stops. Keep this extension on paper.

Add your extension to the same submission under Extension.

+1 point: Mark one break on a labeled circuit sketch.
+1 point: Explain how the break affects current through the motor.
