IED Lesson 4.12 · DuckWorks Amusement Park

Amusement Park Design Brief

45 minutes · 10 points

Concept Activity Submission

Today’s Goal

Turn your assigned DuckWorks ride requirements into a labeled concept sketch.

Learn and see a demonstration (10 min), work (30 min), then submit and clean up (5 min).

Learn the Concept

A performance requirement states what the model must do and gives you something to check. A constraint sets a limit, such as the maximum base footprint. A concept sketch shows how the parts could work together before you build them.

Worked Example

For a Ferris Wheel, “four suspended cabins” can be checked by counting. Draw where the four cabins hang and how the wheel will turn. An overhead outline labeled 230 × 240 mm fits the 250 × 250 mm base limit.

What to Do

Safety: This is a tabletop model. Get the instructor’s approval of the mechanism and electrical plan before fabrication.

  1. In your assigned team of 2–3, read the three requirements for your ride in the DuckWorks challenge.
  2. Sketch one idea that fits the base and uses the standard kit’s low-voltage motor and switch.
  3. Label the moving part, motion arrow, supports, motor, switch, power source, and one part your team could design.

Open your ride requirements.

What to Submit

One labeled concept sketch with the planned length and width of the base and a checklist of your three ride-specific requirements on the same page.

Submit one file for your team and include all team members’ names. Turn it in through Google Classroom for Lesson 4.12.

Check Your Work

Does the sketch show a path from the motor to the moving part, and are all three requirements testable?

10-point rubric

  • 4 points · Completion. Include all requested parts of today’s submission.
  • 4 points · Engineering accuracy. Use correct concepts and labels. Support your result or decision with the evidence requested.
  • 2 points · Clarity. Make the evidence readable and include the required name or team names.
