Activity Flow

How the Lesson Moves

The page highlights only the student-facing elements that make sense outside the full slide deck.

1. Start with the mission problem

Students identify what engineering task this topic helps solve: lift, move, rotate, transfer, measure, support, power, or control.

2. Connect to aerospace systems

Students map the topic to a rover, aircraft, rocket, drone, or satellite mechanism and name the evidence that would prove it worked.

3. Use evidence to decide

Students compare mechanism choices and practice choosing evidence that supports a claim rather than relying on appearance or preference.

Interactive Checks

Questions Before Answers

Use the prompts first. Expand the answer only after students vote or respond.

Prompt 1: Which choice sounds most like engineering?

Students choose among easy, copied, impressive-looking, or evidence-based decision-making.

Reveal answer

Best answer: Compare options against criteria and evidence before deciding.

This keeps the decision connected to the problem, measurable expectations, and proof another person can inspect.

Prompt 2: What evidence would you need?

Students select the strongest evidence for a mechanism decision that is ready to use in the Unit 1 challenge.

Reveal answer

Best evidence: a labeled sketch/diagram plus measurement, calculation, or repeated test data.

Photos and preferences can support the story, but they do not prove performance by themselves.

Prompt 3: What should the engineer do next?

A mechanism works once but produces inconsistent output on the next trial.

Reveal answer

Best next step: record the issue, identify the likely cause, revise one thing, and retest.

The engineering move is to use the problem as data rather than hiding or restarting without learning.

Today’s Student Work

Main Deliverable

Motor performance observation notes

Observe how load, speed, torque, and gearing change motor performance.

  • Label the mechanism, input, output, motion, and evidence clearly.
  • Use data, calculations, or observations when available.
  • Connect the work to the VEX Rover Mechanisms Challenge.
