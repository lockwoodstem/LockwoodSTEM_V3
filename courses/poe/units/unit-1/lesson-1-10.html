VEX Crane Stability and Torque Challenge

Engineering focus: How do torque, reach, center of mass, and structural design determine whether a crane can lift a load without tipping?

Engineering Challenge

A crane must do more than lift—the structure must resist the turning effect created by a load acting at a distance.

Done When

The crane lifts 300 g at least 250 mm high while the load acts at least 300 mm from the main support, remains freestanding, and completes three consecutive successful trials.

Design Brief

VEX Crane Stability and Torque Challenge

Design and build a freestanding VEX crane that lifts a 300 g payload at least 250 mm off the ground while the payload acts at a horizontal reach of at least 300 mm from the crane's main support/tower. The crane must remain stable and complete three consecutive successful trials.

Required Features

Your Crane Must Include

Freestanding Base

The crane must remain upright without a student holding or pressing on the structure.

Boom

A structural member must extend the payload away from the main support/tower.

Motor-Powered Lift

A VEX motor must provide the lifting power.

Lifting System

Use a winch, pulley, gear train, sprocket/chain system, or an appropriate combination.

Stability Strategy

Use base geometry, bracing, counterweight, or another engineering method to resist tipping.

Measurable Reach

The payload force must act at least 300 mm horizontally from the main support/tower during the official test.

Physics

Torque and Stability

Load Force

Calculate the payload weight using F = mg.

Load Torque

Calculate the turning effect of the payload using τ = Fd, where d is the horizontal distance from the main support/tipping reference.

Center of Mass

Consider where the combined mass of the crane and counterweight acts relative to the base.

Structural Loading

Watch for bending, twisting, loose joints, and frame deformation as reach increases.
