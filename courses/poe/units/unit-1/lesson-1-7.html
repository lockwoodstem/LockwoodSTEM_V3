POE Unit 1 • Lesson 1.7

Guided VEX Gear & Sprocket Drive Systems

Focus question: How do we correctly build gear and sprocket drive systems so that calculated mechanical relationships work in a physical VEX mechanism?

Start LessonOpen Build GuideUnit 1 Overview
Guided Build Lab

Copy the build first. Test it. Explain it. Then make one small change.

This lesson is intentionally step-by-step so every team gains confidence with VEX shafts, collars, bearings, gears, sprockets, and chain.

Lesson Snapshot

What You Will Be Able to Do

This is a training-wheels-on lesson before later open-ended mechanism challenges.

1

Build a gear reduction

Construct a 12T driver gear meshed with a 36T driven gear and test smooth rotation.

2

Add an idler gear

Use an idler to change direction or transfer motion across more distance while tracking the overall ratio.

3

Build a chain drive

Connect a 12T driver sprocket to a 24T driven sprocket using chain, proper alignment, and good tension.

Learning Target

Main Objective and Evidence

Learning Target

I can build functional VEX gear and sprocket drive systems using correct shaft support, spacing, alignment, collars, spacers, and chain tension.

Deliverable

Guided VEX Gear & Sprocket Drive Systems Build Guide with calculations, stop-and-check boxes, troubleshooting notes, and photos.

Success Looks Like

Your mechanisms spin freely by hand, the gears do not skip, the chain stays on, and your team can explain speed, torque, and direction changes.

Build Checks

Stop Before You Continue

Do not keep building on top of a problem. If a shaft binds, a gear skips, or the chain climbs off the sprocket, stop and fix alignment, spacing, collars, or chain tension before moving to the next step.

Gear drive should...

  • Spin freely by hand
  • Mesh without jamming
  • Show slower output with more ideal torque
  • Reverse direction with one external mesh

Idler gear should...

  • Mesh smoothly with both gears
  • Change the direction pattern
  • Transfer motion across more distance
  • Keep the overall simple-train ratio based on first and last gears

Chain drive should...

  • Have aligned sprockets
  • Use chain that is not too tight or too loose
  • Keep the chain on the teeth
  • Rotate input and output in the same direction
