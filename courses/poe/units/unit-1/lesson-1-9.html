VEX Cargo Lift Engineering Challenge

Challenge: Design and build a VEX cargo lift that raises a 500 g payload at least 400 mm vertically and completes three consecutive successful trials.

Design Brief

Mission Objective

Required Performance

Design and build a VEX cargo lift that can raise a standardized 500 g payload vertically at least 400 mm. The payload must travel on a guided platform, the structure must remain freestanding, and the lift must complete three consecutive successful lifting trials without student assistance.

Requirements

Your Lift Must Include

Structural Frame

A freestanding VEX structure that supports the lift and remains upright without being held.

Payload Platform

The 500 g payload must sit on a platform, carrier, or tray. It may not hang directly from the string.

Guided Vertical Motion

Use rails, channels, rollers, wheels, sliding guides, or another approved method so the platform moves primarily up and down instead of swinging freely.

Motor-Powered Lift

A VEX motor must supply the lifting power. No manual assistance is allowed during an official trial.

Power Transfer

Connect the motor to the lift using a winch/drum, gears, chain and sprockets, pulleys, or an appropriate combination.

Stable Base

The structure must support itself throughout the lift.

Design Freedom

What Your Team Gets to Decide

  • Overall lift geometry
  • Frame width and height
  • Platform design
  • Guide system
  • Motor location
  • Winch/drum design
  • Gear ratio
  • Gears, chain/sprockets, or pulleys
  • Structural bracing
  • Cable/string routing
  • Payload retention method
  • Speed-versus-torque strategy
  • How your design reduces friction
  • How your design prevents binding
Rules

What You May Not Do

  • Hold or stabilize the lift during an official trial.
  • Touch the payload or platform while it is moving.
  • Manually assist the motor, axle, drum, or lifting string.
  • Change the standardized payload mass.
  • Begin with the payload partially raised.
  • Allow the payload to hang freely instead of using a guided platform.
  • Use non-VEX structural components unless specifically approved.

If the mechanism jams, tips, drops the payload, or requires assistance, that trial does not count as successful.

Before You Build

Concept Sketch Required

Your concept sketch must show and label the overall lift structure, payload platform, vertical guide system, motor location, power-transfer system, winch/drum or lifting mechanism, planned gear ratio, and major structural bracing.

Your team should be able to explain how power travels from the motor to the payload before construction begins.

Engineering Concepts

Use the Physics You Already Know

Gear Ratio

How does your ratio change output speed and torque?

Mechanical Advantage

Does your mechanism increase the force available to raise the load?

Work

W = Fd. Use the payload force and vertical lift distance.

Power

P = W/t. Compare how quickly your mechanism performs the lift.

Friction & Alignment

Look for rubbing, binding, uneven winding, and platform tilt.

Structure & Load Path

Identify how the payload force travels through the cable, frame, bracing, and base.

Official Testing

Three Consecutive Trials

Before each trial, place the platform at the LOW position with the full 500 g payload on it. Remove your hands from the mechanism. Timing begins when the motor starts moving and stops when the payload reaches the required height.

Iteration

Required Design Revision

Your first working design is not automatically your final design. Identify at least one problem or area for improvement, make a meaningful engineering revision, and document the change.

Possible issues include platform tilt, motor stalling, slow lifting, frame flex, gear skipping, uneven string winding, binding, base instability, excessive friction, or unnecessary mass.

Submission

What You Will Submit

  1. Initial concept sketch
  2. Completed VEX cargo lift
  3. Gear ratio and/or mechanical-system explanation
  4. Three-trial testing data
  5. Average lift time
  6. Work calculation
  7. Power calculation
  8. Description of one problem observed during testing
  9. Description of one design revision
  10. Final reflection: explain the most important tradeoff your team made between speed, torque, stability, and reliability.
