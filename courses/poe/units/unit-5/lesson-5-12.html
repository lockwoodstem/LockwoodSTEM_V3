Lesson Sequence

From Capstone Question to Evidence

Each step moves the final project from an idea toward a defensible engineering solution.

  1. Opening prompt: How does testing protocol and risk review move the capstone forward?
  2. Capstone connection: Identify the requirement, subsystem, evidence, or decision today’s work supports.
  3. Interactive vote: Choose the strongest capstone action and justify it.
  4. Guided work time: Build the deliverable using clear labels, notes, measurements, sketches, CAD, test plans, or data.
  5. Exit ticket: Identify what changed, what evidence supports it, and what needs to happen next.

POE Connection

Testing Protocol and Risk Review in Context

Capstone work should always connect a design decision to requirements and evidence.

Mission requirement

What must the system accomplish, and how will success be measured?

Subsystem evidence

What sketches, CAD, build notes, code, wiring, test data, or observations show how the system works?

Design defense

What decision can the team justify using the evidence collected today?

Interactive Checks

Questions to Use During the Lesson

Use the reveal boxes after students vote, discuss, or write a quick response.

Which action best supports a strong capstone decision?

Options: choose the easiest idea; use requirements and evidence; focus only on appearance; or save documentation until the end.

Reveal answer

Best answer: use requirements and evidence. A capstone decision should be traceable to criteria, constraints, test results, observations, or documented trade-offs.

What should the team do when the prototype works once but fails twice?

The system succeeds in one trial, but repeated tests show inconsistent results.

Reveal answer

Record all trials, identify the failure mode, revise the weak subsystem or test setup, and retest before making a final claim.
