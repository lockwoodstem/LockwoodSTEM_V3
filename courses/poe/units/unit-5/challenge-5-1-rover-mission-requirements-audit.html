POE Unit 5 | Mission Brief 5.1

Rover Mission Requirements Audit

Turn a broad rover mission statement into clear, testable engineering requirements.

Intermediate1 class periodPractice & Review
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

A client has requested “a reliable rover that can transport a payload across rough terrain.” The statement is not specific enough to design or verify.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Distinguish needs from requirements
  • Write measurable shall-statements
  • Identify verification methods
  • Detect conflicting or missing requirements
Materials

What You Need

  • Mission statement
  • Requirements template
  • Engineering notebook
  • Optional sample rover data
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Underline every user need or desired function in the mission statement.
  2. Convert each need into one or more measurable requirements.
  3. Add units, limits, conditions, and success criteria.
  4. Assign a verification method: inspection, analysis, demonstration, or test.
  5. Identify at least two missing requirements and one possible conflict.
  6. Prioritize requirements as Must, Should, or Could.
  7. Conduct a peer audit and revise unclear statements.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • At least ten measurable requirements
  • Every requirement uses a defined condition and limit
  • Each requirement has a verification method
  • Avoid solution-specific wording unless required by the client
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Do not invent unrealistic performance limits
  • Maintain traceability to the original mission need
  • Clearly mark assumptions
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Need-to-requirement table
  • Prioritized requirement list
  • Verification matrix
  • Conflict/missing-information note
  • Revised final set
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which user need was hardest to make measurable?
  2. What requirement could unintentionally restrict design creativity?
  3. Which requirement has the highest mission risk?
  4. What question should be returned to the client?

Engineering Tip

A strong requirement states what the system must accomplish, not how the designer must accomplish it.

Common Mistake

Words such as reliable, fast, lightweight, and easy are not testable without a number or condition.

Stretch Goal

Add interface requirements for charging, payload attachment, or data transfer.

Career Connection

Systems engineers manage requirements and verification across complex aerospace projects.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
