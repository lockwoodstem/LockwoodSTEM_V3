01 / Prepare

Drivetrain Assembly Briefing

5 min • Safety5 min • Parts25 min • Build5 min • Inspect5 min • Log

Today's learning target

I can install a standard Clawbot drivetrain, inspect its mechanical power path and document alignment, gear engagement and wheel clearance.

Materials: the chassis from 3.3; standard VEX V5 Clawbot drive motors, gears, shafts, bearings, collars and wheels; VEX tools; supplied V5 Speed Build chassis instructions; camera-enabled device.

Build constraints

Preserve the standard Clawbot chassis architecture and drive-motor count. Use an instructor-approved initial wheel and gear arrangement; wheel and gear-ratio comparisons and design changes are studied in Lesson 3.5.

Safety: Keep the V5 battery disconnected while assembling and inspecting the drivetrain. No powered wheel tests are required today. Do not attach the arm or claw.

Engineering checkpoint: A finished drivetrain is mechanically complete and turns without obvious binding. It does not need to drive under power yet. If you cannot complete the build, record the actual progress and inspection results honestly; keep all parts safely stored for the next class.

02 / Engineering Concepts

What Makes a Drivetrain Work?

Torque transmission

The motors supply rotational torque. Gears and shafts transmit that rotation toward the wheels.

Shaft support

Bearings and supports keep shafts aligned to reduce wobble, friction and premature wear.

Gear engagement

Gears must mesh without skipping or excessive friction. Misalignment can jam the drivetrain.

Wheel clearance

Wheels must not rub the chassis. Proper spacing and secure shaft retention allow smooth rotation.
