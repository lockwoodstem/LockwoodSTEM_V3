01 / Concept Lesson

Torque, Speed, Traction & Wheel Size

Use simplified ideal mechanical models; real results depend on friction, traction, loading, efficiency and gear availability.

5 min — Concept review10 min — Gear & wheel math10 min — Inspect options15 min — Engineering analysis5 min — Export

Gear reduction

For a simple external gear pair, reduction ratio = driven gear teeth ÷ driving gear teeth. A 36-tooth driver and 60-tooth driven gear give a reduction of 60 ÷ 36 ≈ 1.67:1. The output rotates more slowly while ideal torque increases proportionally.

Wheel diameter

One wheel revolution travels approximately π × wheel diameter when rolling without slipping. A larger wheel covers more ground per revolution but requires more axle torque to generate the same ground force.

Force versus traction

Ideal pulling force scales with wheel torque divided by wheel radius: F = τ / r. But the ground can transfer only as much force as the tires' traction allows. More gear reduction does not guarantee victory.

Standard Clawbot constraints

Keep the standard chassis and drive motor count. Only select instructor-approved compatible wheel types and gear ratios. Do not install the arm/claw, add ballast, or redesign the frame.

Important: Calculators below assume one simple gear pair, negligible energy loss, a common motor speed, and rolling without slip. These values are comparative predictions, not measured speed or actual tug-of-war force. For compound gear trains, calculate the product of individual stage ratios separately.
