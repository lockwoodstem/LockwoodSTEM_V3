01 / Challenge Brief

The Clawbot Tug-of-War

Use your approved drivetrain configuration from Lesson 3.5 and reliable motor controls from Lesson 3.6.

Allowed modifications

Use the standard VEX V5 Clawbot base chassis and the original drive motor count. Instructor-approved wheel types and drivetrain gear ratios are permitted. No arm, claw, extra ballast or structural modifications intended to add pulling weight.

Match format

Each team records up to three scored pulling rounds against an assigned opponent. A round ends at the instructor's stop signal or specified boundary condition. Record ties and incomplete rounds accurately; the instructor may adjust match schedules for class time.

Data to observe

Record configuration, opponent, result, approximate pull duration, and what occurred: tire slip, steady movement, stalled drivetrain, loss of traction or uneven turning. Tie observations to the predictions from Lesson 3.5.

Engineering focus

Gear reduction can increase ideal wheel torque but reduces output speed. Actual pulling is limited by available traction and other real losses. A robot that loses can earn full credit by documenting and explaining its performance.

Mandatory instructor supervision: Do not tie robots together or begin a pull independently. The instructor must approve and install low, centered tow attachments and suitably rated equipment, establish a guarded pulling area, control starting and stopping, and keep all observers clear of moving robots and tensioned lines. Stop immediately for wheel lift, tipping, unstable motion or equipment damage. If a safe towing setup is unavailable, the instructor may substitute a controlled traction test.

02 / Technical Preparation

Predict Before You Pull

Gear reduction

For one gear pair, reduction ratio = driven teeth ÷ driving teeth. A higher reduction usually increases ideal wheel torque while reducing axle speed.

Force and wheel radius

For the same wheel torque, ideal ground force scales inversely with wheel radius: F = τ/r. This is a mechanical prediction, not a directly measured pull.

Traction limitation

If wheels slip, the tire-floor contact may be limiting the pulling force even when the drivetrain could produce more torque.
