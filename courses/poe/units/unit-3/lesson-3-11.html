01 / Engineering Concepts

How a Robotic Manipulator Works

The VEX V5 Clawbot extends its existing drive chassis with a motorized lifting arm and gripping claw.

5 min • Mission scenario10 min • Joints and torque10 min • Examine arm/claw parts15 min • Interactive analysis5 min • Export

Pivot and rotational motion

The arm rotates about a fixed joint. This provides one principal lifting degree of freedom. The claw jaws add a gripping action, which is different from the rover's wheel movement.

Motor torque and leverage

A sample creates a turning moment about the arm pivot. For a simplified horizontal arm, τ = F × r, where F is the downward load and r is its perpendicular moment arm. Longer reach increases required holding torque.

Gearing and loads

Gearing changes the relationship between motor speed and available output torque. The motor, gear train, arm supports and fasteners must all tolerate the lifting load.

Gripping a sample

The claw must apply enough gripping force to retain a sample without crushing it or stalling the motor. Grip success depends on jaw shape, contact surfaces, sample size and friction.

Motion limits

Before powered testing, inspect potential collisions and the maximum safe travel of the arm and jaw assembly. Avoid forcing gears or driving a mechanism against a hard stop.

Safety: This is an analysis and preparation lesson. Keep the Brain off and motors disconnected during part handling. Never put fingers between claw jaws or near a powered arm pivot. In Lesson 3.12, support moving assemblies and inspect mechanical stops before powering the system.
