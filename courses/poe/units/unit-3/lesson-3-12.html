Clawbot Arm & Claw Assembly

Engineering question: How can we add a reliable lifting and gripping mechanism without compromising the rover's mobility or safety?

01 / Prepare

Arm & Claw Build Briefing

Continue with the existing rover that completed the precision-driving qualification.

5 min • Safety5 min • Parts25 min • Build5 min • Inspect5 min • Record

Today's learning target

I can assemble and attach a VEX arm and claw, inspect the pivot and gripping mechanism, and document clearance, alignment and wiring readiness.

Materials: completed rover from Lessons 3.3–3.10; instructor-approved VEX Clawbot arm and claw parts and assembly diagrams; VEX hand tools; ruler; student device or camera.

Build boundaries

Preserve the existing chassis, drive motors and functional driver-control code. Add the standard instructor-approved arm and claw components. Do not alter the drivetrain during this installation.

Power: Turn the V5 Brain off and disconnect the battery before installing, adjusting or manually inspecting mechanisms. No powered manipulator testing today.

Build reference: Use the instructor-provided arm-and-claw assembly diagrams or the appropriate official Clawbot manipulator instructions. The V5 Speed Build link used in Lessons 3.3 and 3.4 is the chassis reference, not an independently verified arm-and-claw guide. Ask the instructor for the mechanism instructions before assembling.

02 / Mechanical Engineering

What to Inspect as You Build

Arm pivot support

The pivot carries both the arm's weight and the payload's loading. Supports must remain aligned and securely fixed to the chassis.

Gear and linkage engagement

Moving parts should engage smoothly through the intended travel without shaft wobble, skipped teeth or collisions.

Claw jaw alignment

Both gripping surfaces must approach and release a sample cleanly. Inspect jaw clearance and mechanical stops.

Wire management

Route motor cables away from moving gears, jaws and the arm pivot. Leave enough slack through the entire intended motion.
