Bridge Load Testing & Structural Analysis

Engineering question: How can load, deflection, and failure evidence be collected safely and used to evaluate the bridge?

Coming In

What You Already Know

you have an inspected as-built bridge. Now it must prove performance under a controlled test.

Learning Targets

By the End

  • Run the approved load test safely.
  • Measure load and midspan deflection.
  • Determine qualification, maximum load, efficiency, and failure mode.
Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Qualification

At 100 N, the bridge must remain at or below 10 mm midspan deflection and satisfy all other challenge constraints.

Progressive loading

Increase load in controlled steps so trends and warning signs are captured.

Load-deflection graph

Plot load against midspan deflection. Changes in slope can indicate joint motion, damage, or nonlinear behavior.

Ultimate test

Only when directed, continue beyond design load to the defined stop/failure condition.

Failure analysis

Record what failed first, where, how, and whether it matches the prediction from Lesson 2.13.

Efficiency

Use maximum supported load divided by actual as-built mass to calculate N/g.

Suggested test sequence

Record midspan deflection and observations at 25 N, 50 N, 75 N, and the 100 N qualification load. Continue beyond 100 N only under the approved procedure and teacher direction.

Apply It to Your Bridge

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Place the bridge in the standard two-support test fixture.
  2. Verify the reference/zero measurement.
  3. Increase load in approved 25 N increments through the 100 N qualification load, unless your teacher specifies a smaller increment.
  4. Record deflection and observations at every step.
  5. Determine qualification at 100 N.
  6. If directed, continue to the ultimate stop condition.
  7. Calculate efficiency and compare actual failure with prediction.
Lesson Deliverable

Bridge test report

  • Load/deflection data table.
  • Load vs. midspan-deflection graph.
  • Qualification result at 100 N.
  • Maximum supported load and N/g efficiency.
  • Failure/deformation evidence.
  • Comparison of predicted and actual failure.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, photos, CAD, spreadsheet, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. What qualifies the bridge at the design-load checkpoint?

It carries 100 N at the required location while staying at or below 10 mm deflection and meeting all other constraints.

2. Why graph load vs. deflection?

The graph reveals stiffness and changes in behavior before ultimate failure.
