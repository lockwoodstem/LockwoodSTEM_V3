Bridge Fabrication & Inspection

Engineering question: How can a team manufacture the designed bridge accurately while preserving evidence of what was actually built?

Coming In

What You Already Know

your design package is the approved baseline. During fabrication, build accurately and document intentional or unavoidable changes.

Learning Targets

By the End

  • Fabricate safely from an approved design.
  • Inspect dimensions, joints, alignment, and mass.
  • Document deviations so later analysis describes the as-built bridge.
Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

As-designed vs. as-built

The prototype may differ from the plan because of tolerances, fit, breakage, or material availability. Document meaningful changes.

Dry fit first

Check fit, alignment, support interface, and member placement before permanent joining.

Joint quality

Surface preparation, adhesive quantity, clamping, alignment, curing, and gusset placement can determine success.

Inspection

Before loading, verify span, width, mass, support fit, alignment, critical dimensions, and visible defects.

Deviation example

If a planned gusset must be replaced with a thicker plate, record the change and its effect on mass and joint geometry. The final review must discuss the bridge that was actually tested.

Apply It to Your Bridge

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Fabricate using the approved plan and lab safety rules.
  2. Photograph major stages.
  3. Record meaningful deviations.
  4. Measure final dimensions and mass.
  5. Inspect support fit, joints, alignment, cracks, and loose components before approval.
Lesson Deliverable

Prototype inspection record

  • Fabrication and completed-bridge photos.
  • Final measured mass and critical dimensions.
  • Inspection status/checklist.
  • Documented deviations with reasons.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, photos, CAD, spreadsheet, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why is a deviation not automatically a mistake?

Manufacturing often requires changes; what matters is whether the change is controlled, justified, and documented.

2. Why measure mass before testing?

Mass is a qualification constraint and is required for structural-efficiency calculations.

Engineering thinking · Lesson 2.16

Inspect the physical bridge before loading

Measure overall width including protruding bolts, mass including all hardware and adhesives, truss openings, plywood thickness, gusset limits and printed connector limits.

Check your understanding: What would you record if the completed bridge is 66 mm wide?

Bridge challenge connection: Record calculations, design choices, inspection measurements or test evidence in your team's design portfolio. Review the complete material, connection and fabrication requirements.
