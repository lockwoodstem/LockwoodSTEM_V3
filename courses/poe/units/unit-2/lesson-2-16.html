Wing Fabrication & Inspection

Engineering question: How can a team manufacture the designed wing accurately while preserving evidence of what was actually built?

Coming In

What You Already Know

Your design package is the approved baseline. During fabrication, the engineering task is to build accurately and document intentional or unavoidable changes.

Learning Targets

By the End of This Lesson

  • Fabricate safely from an approved design package.
  • Inspect dimensions, joints, alignment, and mass.
  • Document deviations so the final analysis describes the as-built structure.
Fabrication Gate

Do not test an uninspected wing

Complete the required inspection and receive teacher clearance before loading. A loose root connection, uncured joint, cracked member, or incorrect fixture fit can create unpredictable failure.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

These notes are intended to be complete enough to learn the core idea directly from the page.

As-designed vs. as-built

The final prototype may differ from the plan because of tolerances, material availability, breakage, fit, or manufacturing decisions. Those changes must be documented.

Dry fit before permanent joining

Check fit, alignment, root interface, and member placement before adhesive or irreversible assembly.

Joint quality

Adhesive amount, surface preparation, clamping/alignment, curing time, and gusset placement can determine whether a theoretically strong design succeeds.

Inspection before load

Measure span, chord, mass, root fit, major dimensions, alignment, and visible defects before structural testing begins.

Deviation example

If a planned 2 mm gusset is unavailable and the team substitutes a thicker plate, record the change and its effect on mass and joint geometry. The final design review should discuss the prototype that was actually tested, not only the original drawing.

Apply It to Your Wing

Turn the Concept into Engineering Evidence

  1. Fabricate using the approved design and lab safety procedures.
  2. Photograph major stages of construction.
  3. Record every meaningful deviation from the approved design.
  4. Measure final dimensions and mass.
  5. Inspect joints, root fit, alignment, cracks, and loose components before approval.
Lesson Deliverable

Prototype inspection record

Turn in:

  • Photos of fabrication and completed wing.
  • Final measured mass and critical dimensions.
  • Inspection checklist/status.
  • Documented deviations from the approved design with reasons.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, CAD/design file, spreadsheet, photos, or another teacher-approved format as appropriate to the lesson.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why is a documented deviation not automatically a mistake?

Manufacturing often requires changes. The engineering issue is whether the change is controlled, justified, and included in later analysis.

2. Why inspect mass before testing?

Qualification includes a 75 g mass limit, and mass is also needed for the structural-efficiency calculation.
