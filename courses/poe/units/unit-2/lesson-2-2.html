01 / Build understanding

What is a centroid?

Imagine balancing a uniform cardboard shape on the tip of a pencil. Where should the pencil touch?

Centroid

The average position of an area

A centroid is the geometric center of a shape. Think of averaging the position of every tiny piece of its area. It is located using coordinates, not by guessing the middle of a bounding box.

For a thin plate of uniform thickness and density in a uniform gravitational field, its area centroid also gives the horizontal location of its center of gravity.

Engineering significance

Why engineers care

Locating centroids helps us reason about balance, mass distribution, structural cross-sections and where the weight of an object acts. In later lessons, this connects to beam bending and structural stiffness.

Remember: The centroid is a location. It does not by itself tell us how strong or stiff a part is.

Centroid

Based on geometry: area, line or volume.

Center of mass

Based on how mass is distributed.

Center of gravity

Where the resultant gravitational force acts.

Warm-up: Think before calculating

You cut an L-shaped plate from plywood. Will its balance point be at half the total width and half the total height? Sketch your prediction, then revisit it after the worked example.

Check your prediction

Not necessarily. An L-shaped plate is uneven: there is more material near the upright and top flange. Its centroid is an area-weighted location, not necessarily the center of the surrounding rectangle.

02 / Simple geometry

First learn the two building blocks

Always choose an origin O and measure both centroid coordinates from the same origin. The yellow dot is the centroid.

Rectangle

A = bh  ·  x̄ = b/2  ·  ȳ = h/2

For the 120 mm × 60 mm plate: x̄ = 60 mm, ȳ = 30 mm. A uniform rectangle has two lines of symmetry; the centroid is at their intersection.

Right triangle

A = bh/2  ·  x̄ = b/3  ·  ȳ = h/3

For this particular orientation, measured from the right-angle corner: x̄ = 40 mm, ȳ = 20 mm. Measure one-third from each perpendicular side, not from the sloping edge.

Coordinate warning: Rectangle and triangle formulas above assume the origin shown in each drawing. Moving the origin changes the reported coordinates—even though the physical centroid stays in the same place.

04 / Follow a full solution

Worked example: an L-shaped plate

We will solve the exact type of problem you will see in the practice packet. Study the origin, colored regions, area table and final reasonableness check.

Step 1 · Divide the shape

The upright is 20 × 80 mm. The separate upper extension to its right is 40 × 20 mm. They touch, but do not overlap, so their areas can be added.

Step 2 · Build the area table

Use the lower-left corner as origin O. Each component rectangle has its own centroid, measured from that same origin.

RegionA (mm²)xᵢ (mm)yᵢ (mm)Axᵢ (mm³)Ayᵢ (mm³)
Web 11,600104016,00064,000
Top 2800407032,00056,000
Total2,400——48,000120,000
x̄ = Σ(Axᵢ)/ΣA  ·  ȳ = Σ(Ayᵢ)/ΣA

Step 3 · Solve: x̄ = 48,000 / 2,400 = 20 mm. ȳ = 120,000 / 2,400 = 50 mm.

Step 4 · Check: The centroid moves toward the web because the web contributes twice as much area as the upper extension. It is not at the bounding-box center (30, 40).

Why are Axᵢ and Ayᵢ measured in mm³?

Area is measured in mm², and each centroid coordinate is measured in mm. Multiplying gives mm³ (a first moment of area). Dividing by total area in mm² produces a coordinate in mm.

05 / Interactive exploration

What happens when we add more material?

Move the slider to change only the top extension's width. The vertical web stays 20 × 80 mm and the top extension stays 20 mm tall. Watch the yellow centroid shift.

Live centroid calculation

x̄ = 20.0 mm · ȳ = 50.0 mm

Prediction: When you widen the top extension, should the centroid move right, left, up or down?

Each part contributes to the total based on its area. Adding material high on the right pulls both centroid coordinates toward that region.

Explain the motion

As the top extension gets wider, its area becomes a larger fraction of the total. The overall centroid shifts right and upward toward the centroid of the added area.

06 / An important variation

What about a hole or cutout?

Removed material is negative area. Subtract its area and its first moments from the uncut plate.

100 × 80 mm plate with a 40 × 20 mm corner removed

  1. Full rectangle: A₁ = 8,000 mm², centroid (50, 40) mm.
  2. Removed corner: A₂ = −800 mm², centroid (80, 70) mm.
  3. Remaining area: A = 8,000 − 800 = 7,200 mm².
  4. Calculate: x̄ = [8,000(50) − 800(80)] / 7,200 = 46.67 mm.
  5. Calculate: ȳ = [8,000(40) − 800(70)] / 7,200 = 36.67 mm.

Sanity check: Removing material from the upper-right makes the centroid move left and down.

07 / Independent checks

Try it before opening the answers

Draw the shape, label your origin, calculate the coordinates, then expand the solution. Work in your 2.2 practice packet or engineering notebook.

Check A · Fundamentals

1. Simple shapes

A: Find the centroid of a uniform rectangle 90 mm wide and 40 mm tall, measured from its lower-left corner.

B: Find the centroid of a right triangle with base 90 mm and height 60 mm, measured from its right-angle corner.

Show answers and reasoning

A: (45, 20) mm, half of each rectangle dimension.

B: (30, 20) mm, one-third of each perpendicular dimension from the right angle.

Check B · Symmetry

2. T-shaped plate

A vertical web 30 mm wide and 60 mm tall supports a centered horizontal flange 100 mm wide and 20 mm tall. The flange sits immediately above the web, making the overall shape 80 mm high. Origin is the lower-left of the overall 100 mm width.

Can you predict x̄ from symmetry, then calculate ȳ?

Show answer and area table

Web: A₁ = 1,800 mm², center (50, 30). Flange: A₂ = 2,000 mm², center (50, 70). x̄ = 50 mm by symmetry. ȳ = [1,800(30) + 2,000(70)] / 3,800 = 51.05 mm.

Check C · Explain

3. Which way will it move?

You take a uniform rectangular plate and remove a small square from its upper-left corner. Predict the direction the centroid moves.

Show the reasoning

It moves right and down: removing material from the upper-left leaves relatively more area on the right and bottom.

Check D · Diagnose

4. Spot the error

A student splits an L-shaped plate into two overlapping rectangles and adds their areas without correction. What will happen?

Show the reasoning

The overlap is counted twice, so the calculated centroid may be incorrect. Divide the part into nonoverlapping regions, or subtract the overlap exactly once.

Engineering vocabulary

Centroid
Average geometric location of an area.
Composite area
Shape built from two or more simpler regions.
First moment of area
Area multiplied by the distance to a chosen axis.
Origin
The (0, 0) reference for all coordinates.
