Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Use these ideas as your lesson notes. Focus on explaining the behavior, not memorizing vocabulary by itself.

External load vs. internal force

An external load is applied to the structure from outside. Inside the structure, members develop forces and stresses that transfer that load toward the support.

Tension and compression

Tension pulls material apart; compression pushes material together. When a girder or deck beam bends downward, material on one side is stretched while material on the opposite side is compressed.

Shear and bending

Shear tends to slide adjacent sections past one another. Bending occurs when loads create curvature. A simply supported bridge experiences both; maximum bending is often near midspan under a centered load, while shear is highest near the supports.

Torsion

Torsion is twisting. Loads that do not pass through a section's effective center can twist a bridge, which is one reason bridges use trusses, girders, deck systems, and multiple load paths.

Failure modes

Structures may fail by fracture, yielding, buckling, excessive deflection, joint separation, crushing, or local tearing. The weakest location—not the strongest member—often controls the final result.

Reading a loaded simply supported bridge

A centered 100 N load bends the bridge between its two supports. The support reactions carry the load back into the ground. Truss members develop tension or compression, while deck and beam members may experience bending and shear. Poor joint geometry, slender compression members, and weak load-transfer points can control failure.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Where would you expect the largest bending demand in a simply supported bridge?

Near midspan for a centered load, because the bending moment reaches its maximum there.

2. What is the difference between bending and torsion?

Bending curves a member; torsion twists it about its longitudinal axis.

Engineering thinking · Lesson 2.2

Diagnose failure from evidence

Separate what you can see (splitting, lateral bowing, joint separation) from the inferred mechanism (fracture, buckling, shear).

Check your understanding: Could a joint fail even if each individual member is strong enough?
