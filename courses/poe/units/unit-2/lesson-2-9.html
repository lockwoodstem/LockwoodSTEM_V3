What You Already Know

You screened materials qualitatively. Now you will quantify mass and compare performance using a load-to-mass metric.

Learning Targets

By the End of This Lesson

  • Calculate density, mass, or volume when two quantities are known.
  • Calculate structural efficiency using load and structure mass.
  • Use efficiency alongside stiffness and design-load requirements.

Density

ρ = m / V

Structural efficiency

Efficiency = Maximum Load / Structure Mass

For this unit, use N/g.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

These notes are intended to be complete enough to learn the core idea directly from the page.

Density relationship

Density connects mass and volume. For a uniform material, knowing any two of density, mass, and volume lets you calculate the third.

Mass budget

The 75 g limit is a design constraint, not a target. A 74 g wing is not automatically better than a 45 g wing if both meet load and stiffness requirements.

Structural efficiency

A simple challenge metric compares maximum supported load with structural mass. Higher values mean more load carried per unit mass.

Minimum requirements first

Efficiency does not replace the design requirements. A very light wing that fails the 20 N design load or exceeds 20 mm deflection does not qualify.

Worked comparison

WingMassMaximum loadEfficiency
A70 g45 N0.64 N/g
B45 g40 N0.89 N/g

Wing A carries more total load, but Wing B carries more load for each gram of structure. Both would still need to pass the 20 N deflection requirement to qualify.

Apply It to Your Wing

Turn the Concept into Engineering Evidence

  1. Complete density or mass calculations for sample materials/components.
  2. Compare at least three hypothetical wing structures using load-to-mass efficiency.
  3. Identify which structure is strongest, which is lightest, and which is most efficient.
  4. Explain why those three labels may describe different designs.
Lesson Deliverable

Structural efficiency analysis

Turn in:

  • Density/mass calculations.
  • Efficiency calculations for at least three structures.
  • A short evidence-based comparison of strength, mass, and efficiency.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, CAD/design file, spreadsheet, photos, or another teacher-approved format as appropriate to the lesson.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. A 50 g wing carries 35 N before failure. What is its structural efficiency?

35 ÷ 50 = 0.70 N/g.

2. Can the highest-efficiency wing automatically win?

No. It must also meet all qualification criteria such as design load, deflection, dimensions, and material rules.
