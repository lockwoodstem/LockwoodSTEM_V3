Beam Deflection

Engineering question: What determines how far a beam bends under load?

LearnPracticeApplySubmitUnit 2
Foundational instruction

Learn it before the bridge project

Use varied engineering examples to master the mechanics, then apply the technique to your final structural design later in the unit.

Learning Targets

  • Distinguish strength from stiffness
  • Predict the effect of beam span and section depth
  • Interpret controlled deflection tests

Learn the Concept

  • Deflection is displacement under load, not necessarily failure
  • For a simply supported beam with a central point load, δ = PL³/(48EI) under ideal linear-elastic assumptions
  • I for a rectangular section about its horizontal centroidal axis = bh³/12

During instruction: Sketch the forces, geometry, variables or joints discussed and record units for every quantity.

Practice Together

Compare two otherwise identical beams, one 200 mm long and one 400 mm long. Under the ideal central-load model, by what factor does deflection change?

Show: a labeled diagram, a mathematical model when relevant, and a final statement interpreting the result.

Apply the Skill

Design a fair test comparing two wooden ruler orientations with the same span and load. Identify your independent, dependent and controlled variables.

Check your understanding: Why can a tall, narrow beam resist vertical bending better than the same beam laid flat?

Student Evidence

Beam deflection predictions and experimental plan. Use your engineering notebook or teacher-provided guided notes; include dimensions, units and a conclusion.

Looking ahead: These mechanics techniques will be revisited when students analyze the bridge design in Lessons 2.15–2.20.
