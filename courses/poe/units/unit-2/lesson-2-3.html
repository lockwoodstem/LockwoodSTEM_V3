Learning Targets

By the End of This Lesson

  • Isolate a structure from its surroundings.
  • Draw applied forces, distances, and support reactions on a free-body diagram.
  • Explain why a simply supported bridge uses force reactions at two supports.
FBD Checklist

A useful free-body diagram includes

  • The isolated object drawn simply.
  • Every important external force shown with an arrow.
  • Support reactions replacing the physical support.
  • Force magnitudes when known.
  • Distances needed for moment calculations.
  • A coordinate direction/sign convention.
Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Use these ideas as your lesson notes. Focus on explaining the behavior, not memorizing vocabulary by itself.

What a free-body diagram does

A free-body diagram (FBD) removes unnecessary visual detail and replaces physical connections with the forces and moments they exert on the isolated object.

System boundary

Choose what object you are analyzing. For the unit challenge, one useful boundary is the entire bridge structure between the two supports.

Bridge support reactions

For this unit, model the bridge with a pin support on the left and a roller support on the right. The supports provide force reactions but do not provide a fixed-end reaction moment.

Dimensions matter

A force without its location is incomplete. The distance between the applied load and each support determines the support reactions, which you will calculate in Lesson 2.5.

Bridge challenge FBD

For the required design case, show a 20 N downward load located 250 mm (0.250 m) from the root. Replace the root fixture with its reaction forces and a support reactions. If the only applied vertical load is 20 N, equilibrium requires the vertical reaction to oppose that load. The support reactions will be determined from the load and its distance.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why should the physical clamp be replaced by reactions on an FBD?

The FBD shows how the surroundings interact with the isolated bridge. The detailed clamp geometry is replaced by the forces and moment it applies.

2. Why must distance be shown on the FBD?

The turning effect of a force depends on both force magnitude and perpendicular distance from the reference point.
