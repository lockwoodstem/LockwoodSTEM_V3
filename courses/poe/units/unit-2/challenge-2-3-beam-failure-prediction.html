Beam Failure Prediction

Predict where a beam will fail, test it, and explain the difference between prediction and evidence.

Intermediate1 class periodPractice & Review
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

A rover instrument bracket uses a cantilevered support. Before destructive testing, engineers must predict the critical region and expected failure mode.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Draw free-body diagrams
  • Identify bending moment trends
  • Distinguish stress from deflection
  • Compare prediction with test evidence
Materials

What You Need

  • Uniform beam samples
  • Clamp or support fixture
  • Incremental masses
  • Ruler
  • Marker
  • Engineering notebook
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Measure the beam geometry and mark several observation stations.
  2. Draw the beam, support, applied load, and reactions.
  3. Predict the region of maximum bending moment and likely failure location.
  4. Predict whether the beam will bend elastically, permanently deform, buckle, or fracture.
  5. Load the beam in approved increments and record deflection at one location.
  6. Stop at the instructor limit or after failure.
  7. Compare the observed behavior with the prediction.
  8. Write one reason for any mismatch.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • Complete prediction before testing
  • Use consistent load position
  • Record load and deflection for every increment
  • Identify observed failure mode with evidence
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Wear eye protection
  • Use a shield for brittle samples if directed
  • Keep body parts away from the failure zone
  • Do not exceed approved loads
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Free-body diagram
  • Prediction statement
  • Load-deflection table
  • Failure photo or sketch
  • Prediction-versus-result analysis
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Why was the support region critical?
  2. When did behavior stop appearing elastic?
  3. What measurement uncertainty affected the graph?
  4. How would doubling thickness change stiffness?
Download PDF
Engineering Tip

Mark the beam before testing so you can connect deformation to a specific location.

Common Mistake

Predicting failure only from where the load is applied ignores the internal bending moment distribution.

Stretch Goal

Compare two beams with equal mass but different cross-section shapes.

Career Connection

Structural test engineers compare analytical predictions with physical tests before certifying flight hardware.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
