POE Unit 2 | Mission Brief 2.4

Impact-Protection Lander Structure

Design a lightweight protective structure that limits damage to a payload during a controlled drop.

Advanced2-3 class periodsEngineering Challenge
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

A small sensor package must survive a hard landing. Your team will design a structure that manages impact energy without simply surrounding the payload with excessive material.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Apply energy absorption concepts
  • Design controlled deformation
  • Use iterative drop testing
  • Evaluate protection versus mass
Materials

What You Need

  • Small payload or raw egg substitute
  • Paper, straws, foam, cardboard, tape, or approved materials
  • Mass scale
  • Drop test area
  • Engineering notebook
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Record payload dimensions, mass, and failure risks.
  2. Sketch two concepts using crumple zones, suspension, landing legs, or protective frames.
  3. Select one concept and define a maximum structure mass.
  4. Build the first prototype and photograph it before testing.
  5. Conduct a low-height drop, inspect, and revise.
  6. Increase to the official height only after passing the lower test.
  7. Record damage, orientation, and structural deformation.
  8. Calculate total system mass and summarize protection performance.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • Payload must remain contained
  • Structure must fit within the assigned envelope
  • Mass must remain below the unit limit
  • No loose material may scatter during testing
  • Complete at least two test-and-revision cycles
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Use only the designated drop zone
  • No throwing or launching
  • Wear eye protection if required
  • Retrieve the device only after the area is clear
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Two concept sketches
  • Prototype photos
  • Test log
  • Mass record
  • Damage assessment
  • Final redesign explanation
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which feature absorbed the most energy?
  2. Did the structure protect by staying rigid or deforming?
  3. How did landing orientation affect results?
  4. What would change for a higher drop speed?

Engineering Tip

Plan where the structure should deform; accidental deformation may transfer load directly to the payload.

Common Mistake

Adding soft material everywhere can increase mass without controlling the load path.

Stretch Goal

Improve survival height by 25% without increasing mass.

Career Connection

Crashworthiness engineers design landing gear, energy absorbers, seats, and protective structures for aircraft and spacecraft.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
