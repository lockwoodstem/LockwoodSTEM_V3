01 / Core concept

Moment = turning effect of a force

When you open a door by pushing near the handle, the force acts farther from the hinge and creates more turning effect than the same force applied near the hinge.

Force (F)The push or pull; measured in newtons (N).
Pivot / moment center (O)The point about which we calculate rotation.
Perpendicular distance (d⊥)The shortest distance from the pivot to the force's line of action; measured in meters (m).

The basic moment equation

|MO| = F × d⊥

For a force perpendicular to a horizontal lever, the moment arm equals the horizontal distance. 30 N × 0.40 m = 12 N·m.

To give the moment a sign, check which way the force tends to rotate the body around the selected point O.

Line of action matters

The lever arm is not always the distance along the object. It is the shortest perpendicular distance between the pivot and the infinite straight line of action of the force.

If the force's line of action passes through O, then d⊥ = 0 and its moment about O is zero, even if the force is large.

02 / Direction and units

Clockwise or counterclockwise?

We will use the conventional sign system: counterclockwise (CCW) is positive, and clockwise (CW) is negative.

Counterclockwise

Positive moment (+)

Push down to the left of a pivot, or push up to its right: the tendency is counterclockwise.

Clockwise

Negative moment (−)

Push down to the right of a pivot, or push up to its left: the tendency is clockwise.

Units: Moment is N·m (newton-meters). It is dimensionally equivalent to a joule, but moments are not energy and should be labeled N·m. To convert N·mm to N·m, divide by 1,000.
03 / Angled forces

Only the perpendicular part creates a moment

If a force is not perpendicular to the lever, use the perpendicular force component—or the perpendicular moment arm—to calculate its moment.

Use the angle between r and F

|M| = r F |sin θ|

Here r is the straight distance from the pivot to where the force acts, and θ is the angle between the position vector and the force vector.

Example: 40 N at 30° below a 0.50 m horizontal lever → |M| = (0.50)(40)(sin30°) = 10 N·m, clockwise, so M = −10 N·m.

Use force components instead

M = rₓ Fᵧ − rᵧ Fₓ

For a horizontal lever extending right from the pivot, rᵧ = 0, so M = rₓFᵧ. Horizontal force Fₓ makes no moment about the pivot in this case.

This directly builds on the sine/cosine components in Lesson 2.5.

05 / Worked calculations

From a lever to a bridge support reaction

Always begin with a free-body diagram, choose a pivot, and write the sign convention beside your equations.

Example A · Single moment

30 N downward at 0.40 m

  1. Choose the pivot at the left end of a horizontal lever.
  2. The downward force acts 0.40 m to the right.
  3. Moment magnitude: |M| = Fd = 30(0.40) = 12 N·m.
  4. Downward on the right tends clockwise → negative sign.
MO = −12 N·m (CW)

Interpretation: doubling the force or lever arm doubles the moment magnitude.

Example B · Unequal beam reactions

80 N at 1.0 m on a 4.0 m span

  1. FBD: 80 N downward at x = 1.0 m, reaction Aᵧ at x = 0, reaction Bᵧ at x = 4.0 m.
  2. Take moments about A, CCW positive: ΣMA = +Bᵧ(4.0) −80(1.0) = 0.
  3. Solve: Bᵧ = 80/4 = 20 N upward.
  4. Vertical equilibrium: ΣFᵧ = Aᵧ +20−80 = 0, so Aᵧ = 60 N upward.
  5. Horizontal: Aₓ = 0 because no horizontal force acts.
Aᵧ = 60 N ↑  ·  Bᵧ = 20 N ↑

Check the same answer about the other support

Taking moments about B: ΣMB = +80(3.0) −Aᵧ(4.0) = 240 −240 = 0 N·m.

Why choose a support as pivot? Any force whose line of action passes through that chosen pivot has a zero moment about it, eliminating its unknown magnitude from the equation.

General rule: Bᵧ = Pa/L  ·  Aᵧ = P(L − a)/L

For one downward point load P between a pin at A and roller at B, with span L and load distance a from A. These are equilibrium reactions, not a material strength or deflection calculation.

07 / Engineering application

Planning a safe, fair moments experiment

You can investigate turning effects with a ruler or small lever, a fulcrum and teacher-approved small masses. Your drawing and calculations are the main work; any physical test must be instructor-approved.

Predict before testing

  1. Place a ruler on a stable pivot, marked as O.
  2. Choose a small known downward force (or compute weight W = mg).
  3. Vary the distance from the pivot while holding the force constant.
  4. Predict the clockwise or counterclockwise moment at each position.
  5. Record F (N), d⊥ (m), and moment (N·m), including sign.

Safety: No standing on bridges, school fixtures or improvised loaded spans. Use only instructor-approved small weights and supports.

Identify your variables

TypeWhat to measure or control
IndependentDistance from pivot
DependentCalculated moment magnitude
ControlsSame force, lever orientation, pivot and measurement method

Prediction: Increasing the distance by 50% while force stays constant increases moment magnitude by 50% for a perpendicular force.

08 / Independent practice

Sketch and solve before revealing the answers

Use CCW positive, CW negative. Include moment units and show how you chose the pivot.

Check A

Basic clockwise moment

A 30 N force acts vertically downward 0.40 m to the right of pivot O. Calculate its signed moment.

Reveal worked answer

M = −(30)(0.40) = −12.0 N·m, clockwise.

Check B

Counterclockwise sign

A 15 N downward force acts 0.60 m left of pivot O. What is its moment?

Reveal worked answer

Downward to the left produces counterclockwise rotation. M = +(15)(0.60) = +9.0 N·m.

Check C

Angle changes moment

A 50 N force acts 0.40 m to the right of a pivot at 30° above the horizontal. Calculate its moment about the pivot.

Reveal worked answer

M = rF sinθ = (0.40)(50)(sin30°) = +10.0 N·m (CCW).

Check D

Off-center bridge reaction

A 4.0 m simply supported beam carries 80 N downward 1.0 m from A. Find Aᵧ and Bᵧ.

Reveal worked answer

ΣMA = 4Bᵧ−80(1) = 0 ⇒ Bᵧ = 20 N upward. ΣFᵧ = Aᵧ+20−80=0 ⇒ Aᵧ=60 N upward.

Check E

New beam geometry

A 5.0 m simply supported beam has a 120 N downward load 3.0 m from A. Find the reactions.

Reveal worked answer

ΣMA = 5Bᵧ−120(3) = 0 ⇒ Bᵧ = 72 N upward. Aᵧ = 120−72 = 48 N upward.

Check F

Why pivot choice matters

Why do statics problems often take moments about a support? What happens if a force's line of action passes through that point?

Reveal worked answer

A force whose line of action passes through the chosen pivot has zero perpendicular moment arm, so its moment about that point is zero. Choosing a pivot at a support can eliminate unknown reaction forces from the moment equation.

Vocabulary

Moment: turning effect of force about a point.

Moment arm: perpendicular distance to force's line of action.

Moment equilibrium: sum of signed moments equals zero.

Support reaction: force or moment supplied by a restraint.
