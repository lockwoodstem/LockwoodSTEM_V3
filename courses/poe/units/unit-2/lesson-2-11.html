Coming In

What You Already Know

You predicted how materials should behave. Today you collect your own evidence under controlled conditions.

Learning Targets

By the End of This Lesson

  • Run a repeatable structural material test.
  • Measure mass, dimensions, load, and deflection consistently.
  • Use test data to make a justified material recommendation.
Safety

Testing can release stored energy

Wear required PPE, use the teacher-approved fixture, keep faces and hands out of the failure path, add load only as directed, and stop immediately at the teacher's stop condition. Do not improvise destructive tests.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

These notes are intended to be complete enough to learn the core idea directly from the page.

Controlled comparison

A fair test changes one important factor at a time while keeping specimen geometry, span, loading location, and measurement method consistent whenever possible.

Repeatability

Multiple trials reveal variability. If repeated measurements disagree significantly, investigate setup, measurement, specimen differences, or procedure.

Deflection data

Deflection under a standard load is a practical way to compare stiffness when geometry and test setup are controlled.

Failure evidence

Failure load, failure location, and failure mode provide different information. Record all three rather than only the final number.

Recommendation from evidence

A recommendation should cite measured performance and explain the tradeoff between stiffness/strength, mass, and manufacturability.

Apply It to Your Wing

Turn the Concept into Engineering Evidence

  1. Measure and record specimen dimensions and mass.
  2. Run the approved loading procedure and record deflection at the required load levels.
  3. If directed, continue selected specimens to failure and document the failure mode.
  4. Compare performance-to-mass and write a material recommendation for the wing.
Lesson Deliverable

Materials test report

Turn in:

  • Complete data table with units.
  • Photos or sketches of the test setup and relevant failures.
  • At least one graph or calculated comparison when directed.
  • A justified material recommendation that cites measured evidence.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, CAD/design file, spreadsheet, photos, or another teacher-approved format as appropriate to the lesson.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why should specimen geometry and load position be controlled when comparing materials?

Changing geometry or load position changes structural behavior, making it difficult to isolate the effect of material choice.

2. What makes a material recommendation evidence-based?

It cites measured results and explains the relevant tradeoffs rather than relying on preference or appearance.
