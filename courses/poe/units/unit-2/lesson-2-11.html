Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Controlled comparison

Change one important factor at a time while keeping specimen geometry, span, load location, and measurement method consistent whenever possible.

Repeatability

Multiple trials reveal variability and help identify setup or measurement problems.

Deflection

Deflection under a standard load is a useful stiffness comparison when geometry and setup are controlled.

Failure evidence

Record failure load, failure location, and failure mode—not just one final number.

Recommendation

Use measured performance together with mass and manufacturability to justify the material choice.

Suggested data

Record specimen dimensions, mass, standard-load deflection, failure/stop load when approved, failure location, and observations. Use identical units and procedures across specimens.

Apply It to Your Bridge

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Measure specimen dimensions and mass.
  2. Run the approved loading sequence.
  3. Record deflection and observations.
  4. If directed, continue selected specimens to failure.
  5. Write a material recommendation supported by data.
Lesson Deliverable

Materials test report

  • Complete data table with units.
  • Photos/sketches of setup and failures.
  • Graph or calculated comparison when directed.
  • A justified material recommendation.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Engineering thinking · Lesson 2.11

Collect comparable material-test data

Keep specimen dimensions, loading arrangement and measurement method consistent. Record peak force, deformation and failure location alongside material and grain direction.

Check your understanding: What variable must stay fixed for a defensible comparison of two plywood shapes?

Bridge challenge connection: Record calculations, design choices, inspection measurements or test evidence in your team's design portfolio. Review the complete material, connection and fabrication requirements.
