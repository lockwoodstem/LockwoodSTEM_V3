Calculating Force Vectors

Engineering question: How can one angled force be expressed as horizontal and vertical components?

LearnPracticeApplySubmitUnit 2
Foundational instruction

Learn it before the bridge project

Use varied engineering examples to master the mechanics, then apply the technique to your final structural design later in the unit.

Learning Targets

  • Draw coordinate axes and force directions
  • Apply sine and cosine correctly
  • Use signs and units in vector components

Learn the Concept

  • With θ measured from +x: Fx = F cos θ, Fy = F sin θ
  • Direction controls positive and negative signs
  • Vector magnitude F = √(Fx² + Fy²)

During instruction: Sketch the forces, geometry, variables or joints discussed and record units for every quantity.

Practice Together

A 100 N cable force acts 30° above the horizontal. Calculate horizontal and vertical components and label their directions.

Show: a labeled diagram, a mathematical model when relevant, and a final statement interpreting the result.

Apply the Skill

Resolve forces of 80 N at 45° above +x and 50 N at 60° below +x; explain the sign of each component.

Check your understanding: Why does using an angle measured from vertical swap which component uses sine and cosine?

Student Evidence

Completed component diagrams and calculations. Use your engineering notebook or teacher-provided guided notes; include dimensions, units and a conclusion.

Looking ahead: These mechanics techniques will be revisited when students analyze the bridge design in Lessons 2.15–2.20.
