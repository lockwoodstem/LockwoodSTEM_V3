Iteration & Final Engineering Design Review

Engineering question: How should test evidence change the design, and how can a team defend its final engineering decisions?

Learning Targets

By the End

  • Compare predictions with measured performance.
  • Propose a targeted revision tied to a performance gap or failure.
  • Defend decisions using quantitative and qualitative evidence.
Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Claim–evidence–reasoning

A strong engineering claim states what the design achieved, cites measurements/calculations, and explains why design features produced the result.

Prediction vs. result

Differences between prediction and test results are useful. Explain what was right, what was wrong, and what changed your understanding.

Targeted iteration

A useful revision addresses a specific weakness without adding unnecessary mass or creating new problems.

Tradeoffs

Discuss strength, stiffness, mass, manufacturability, reliability, and complexity together.

Limitations

State what was not tested or remains uncertain. This defines the boundary of the evidence.

Revision example

The bridge passed 100 N but deflected 8 mm. Failure later began by buckling in the upper support-side compression member. A targeted redesign might deepen the first structural bay or add a short support-side compression brace rather than adding mass along the entire span.

Apply It to Your Bridge

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Summarize whether each design criterion was met.
  2. Compare predicted and actual stiffness, failure location, and maximum load.
  3. Identify the most important success and weakness.
  4. Propose one targeted revision and predict its effect on performance and mass.
  5. Prepare the final engineering review using evidence from throughout the unit.
Lesson Deliverable

Final bridge engineering design review

  • Final presentation or teacher-approved report.
  • Requirements and selected-design rationale.
  • Relevant calculations and material-test evidence.
  • As-built prototype documentation.
  • Load/deflection graph, qualification, maximum load, and efficiency.
  • Failure analysis, targeted revision, and remaining limitation.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, photos, CAD, spreadsheet, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. What makes a redesign recommendation stronger than “make it stronger”?

It identifies a measured weakness, proposes a specific change, and predicts its performance and mass effects.

2. Why include a limitation?

Engineering conclusions should state the boundaries of the evidence and what remains uncertain.
