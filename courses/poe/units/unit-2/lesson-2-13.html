Engineering Analysis of Bridge Concepts

Engineering question: What evidence should be used to judge a concept before assigning scores in a decision matrix?

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Analyze before scoring

A decision matrix is only as good as the evidence behind each number. Do not score first and invent reasons later.

Load-path quality

Check whether load can travel continuously from the centered load point through chords/girders, bracing, joints, and the support.

Stiffness strategy

Identify what feature is intended to limit deflection: section depth, box geometry, deeper truss geometry, truss depth, bracing, or another approach.

Mass estimate

Use dimensions, density, measured component masses, or comparison with test specimens to estimate which concept approaches the 150 g limit.

Failure prediction

Predict the first likely weak point and explain the likely mode: support separation, member bending, compression buckling, joint tearing, adhesive failure, or another mechanism.

Evidence statement example

Concept B should resist bending better than Concept A because its upper and lower chords are separated by a deeper truss, which can improve stiffness, but the additional members and joints will increase mass. The load-point and support joints remain critical because they transfer large forces into the truss.

Apply It to Your Bridge

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Complete one structural analysis for each concept.
  2. Trace the primary load path and likely tension/compression regions.
  3. Identify the stiffness strategy and predicted weak point.
  4. Estimate relative or numerical mass.
  5. Reference at least one result from an earlier lesson for each concept.
Lesson Deliverable

Bridge concept analysis sheets

  • One analysis page/section per concept.
  • Load path, stiffness strategy, material choice, mass estimate, and predicted failure.
  • At least one cited piece of prior unit evidence for each concept.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, photos, CAD, spreadsheet, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why analyze before scoring?

Decision-matrix scores should summarize evidence rather than replace engineering analysis.

2. What should a predicted failure point include?

A location, likely failure mode, and reason based on loads, geometry, joints, or material behavior.

Engineering thinking · Lesson 2.13

Estimate forces and buckling risks

Use support reactions, member load paths, slenderness and approximate deflection to compare options; identify uncertain assumptions that need physical testing.

Check your understanding: Which predicted compression member is most likely to buckle first?
