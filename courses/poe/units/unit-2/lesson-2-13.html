Opening Prompt

Why do engineers test materials to failure and interpret stress-strain behavior?

Think about loads, materials, geometry, safety factors, testing, and evidence—not just whether the structure looks finished.

Quick class share: What evidence would help an engineer answer today’s focus question?

Reveal the working connection

Testing to failure reveals elastic behavior, yield, ultimate strength, fracture, and how materials respond under increasing load.

POE Connection

Aerospace Structures Must Earn Trust

Unit 2 connects structural analysis, material selection, and testing to a payload support structure that must be defended with evidence.

Loads

What acts on it?

Tension, compression, shear, bending, torsion, and distributed loads affect how a structure behaves.

Materials + Shape

What resists it?

Material properties and cross-section geometry affect strength, stiffness, mass, and failure behavior.

Evidence

What proves it?

Calculations, observations, repeated tests, and load data help justify structural decisions.

Interactive Vote

Which Action Fits?

Vote before opening the answer reveal.

Which action best supports today’s structural engineering work?

1Assume the structure works because it looks strong

2Compare the design to criteria using evidence, calculations, or test results

3Choose the lightest material without checking load performance

4Skip documentation until the final prototype is complete

Reveal the strongest engineering choice

Best answer: 2

Structural choices need criteria and evidence. A design may look strong but still fail because of load path, material choice, geometry, or weak connections.

Carry-forward idea: In POE, strong structural decisions are testable and defensible.
Key Idea

Claim → Evidence → Reasoning

Engineers connect what they believe to proof that others can inspect.

1

Claim

What you believe about the structure, material, load path, or design decision.

→
2

Evidence

Measurements, calculations, observations, photos, repeated trials, or comparison data.

→
3

Reasoning

Why the evidence supports the claim and what the team should do next.

Evidence can include: force diagrams; dimensions; load data; mass; photos; calculations; material observations; revision notes.
Mini Challenge

What Evidence Would You Need?

Select the strongest evidence before opening the reveal.

Claim: “Our payload support structure is strong enough and efficient enough for the challenge.”

AA labeled sketch showing supports, load path, and likely high-stress areas

BA statement that the team likes the design

CLoad test data, mass data, or calculations connected to the criteria

DA photo that makes the prototype look complete

EA short explanation connecting the evidence to the next design decision

Reveal the best evidence pair

Best support: A + C

A shows the structural intent and load path. C shows measured or calculated performance. E is also useful because it explains what the evidence means for the next decision.

Scenario Response

What Should the Engineer Do Next?

Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.

A prototype holds the load once, but the second test shows bending near one connection.

AIgnore it because the structure held once

BRecord the bending, identify the likely cause, reinforce or revise the connection, and retest

CDecorate the structure so it looks more finished

DStart over without documenting what happened

Reveal the strongest next step

Best answer: B

Record the bending, identify the likely cause, revise the connection, and retest. The strongest next step turns a structural weakness into useful evidence.
