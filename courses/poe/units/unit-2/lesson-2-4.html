01 / Understand

What is a free-body diagram?

An FBD is a simplified drawing of one isolated body with all external forces and relevant external moments acting on that body.

Choose the body

What are we analyzing?

A box, lamp, beam or an entire structure can be our body. Redraw it alone as a simplified line, rectangle or point. Remove the table, cables, ground or supports from the isolated view.

We select the system boundary first: only interactions that cross this boundary create external loads on our FBD.

Show the forces

Replace the surroundings with arrows

Every interaction with something outside the body becomes a force or moment as appropriate. Include gravity, applied loads and reactions. Draw and label arrows with correct directions.

Important: Do not draw both members of a Newton's third-law pair on the same body's FBD. The table's upward force on a box belongs; the box's downward force on the table does not.

Example: 12 kg box on a level table

Weight: W = mg = (12 kg)(9.8 m/s²) = 117.6 N downward. The stationary crate receives an upward normal reaction N = 117.6 N. The table itself does not appear in the isolated crate diagram.

02 / Force vocabulary and supports

Which forces should you include?

Each arrow must have a known or assumed direction, a label and a point or line where it acts.

Weight WGravity pulling the body downward; W = mg.
Normal NContact force perpendicular to a surface.
Tension TForce exerted by a taut cable along its length.
Applied force P or FKnown push, pull or externally applied load.
Friction fForce parallel to contact resisting relative sliding.
Reactions Aₓ, Aᵧ, BᵧUnknown support forces created by constraints.

Recognize the three main support symbols

Pin and roller

An ideal pin can exert horizontal and vertical forces, but no reaction moment. An ideal roller on flat ground exerts one vertical normal reaction. A roller on a sloping surface reacts perpendicular to that surface.

Fixed connection and cable

A fixed support can exert horizontal force, vertical force and a resisting moment. A taut cable only pulls along the cable. We will calculate reaction moments later in Unit 2.

04 / Worked beam example

From physical setup to force diagram

A simply supported beam carries a 60 N downward point load at its midpoint, with pin A and roller B 4 m apart. Neglect the beam's self-weight.

1. Physical setup

Identify the beam as the body. The load, pin and roller interact with it from the outside.

2. Isolated beam FBD

Remove the physical support symbols and add their possible reactions. Here Aₓ = 0 N because there is no horizontal load.

3. Use equilibrium and symmetry

  1. Horizontal: ΣFₓ = Aₓ = 0.
  2. Vertical: ΣFᵧ = Aᵧ + Bᵧ − 60 = 0.
  3. The load is at the center and the arrangement is symmetric, so Aᵧ = Bᵧ.
  4. Therefore Aᵧ = Bᵧ = 30 N upward.
+30 N +30 N −60 N = 0 N

Do not split off-center loads equally. For an off-center point load, support reactions differ. We'll solve those cases using moments in Lesson 2.6.

06 / Transfer the skill

Draw other everyday FBDs

A good FBD works for more than beams: the same reasoning applies to lamps, signs, crates and components.

Hanging lamp

A lamp weighs 90 N and hangs from one vertical cable. If motionless, T = W = 90 N for this simplified example.

Why does the tension arrow point upward?

The taut cable pulls the lamp toward its attachment. The lamp's weight acts downward.

Crate pushed but not moving

A 12 kg crate remains stationary while a person pushes horizontally to the right with 30 N on a rough floor.

  • Weight W = 117.6 N downward.
  • Normal N = 117.6 N upward.
  • Applied push = 30 N right.
  • Static friction = 30 N left, assuming it can oppose the push.
Verify equilibrium

ΣFₓ = +30 −30 = 0; ΣFᵧ = +117.6 −117.6 = 0. Actual static friction is the amount needed to prevent sliding, up to its maximum; it is not always μₛN.

07 / Practice independently

Draw first, then open each answer

Use the Lesson 2.4 practice worksheet or engineering notebook. The answers are intentionally hidden so you can test your understanding.

Check A

Stationary 5 kg crate

Draw all external forces on a 5 kg crate at rest on a level table. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

Reveal explanation

Weight = 49 N downward; normal = 49 N upward. With no horizontal interactions, no horizontal forces are needed.

Check B

Center-loaded 100 N beam

Draw the FBD for a pin-and-roller beam with a centered downward 100 N load. Neglect self-weight. Find the reactions.

Reveal explanation

Aₓ = 0; Aᵧ = 50 N upward; Bᵧ = 50 N upward. Include 100 N downward at midpoint. The geometry and load are symmetric.

Check C

Box pushed left

A box stays still when pushed left by 25 N across a rough level floor. Name the force types and directions.

Reveal explanation

Applied force 25 N left; static friction 25 N right (if sufficient friction); weight down; normal up.

Check D

Identify the support

Which support can resist x and y translation yet allows rotation? Which support also prevents rotation?

Reveal explanation

An ideal pin resists horizontal and vertical translation but allows rotation. A fixed support additionally resists rotation via a moment reaction.

Check E

Off-center load

A beam has a downward point load close to A, far from B. Can the reactions simply be divided equally?

Reveal explanation

No. The symmetric 50/50 shortcut applies only to centered loads with symmetric supports. Moment equilibrium is needed for off-center reaction magnitudes.

Check F

Internal versus external

Two bars are joined by a pin. When drawing the FBD of the entire two-bar assembly, why is their internal pin force omitted?

Reveal explanation

Both bars are inside the system boundary, so their mutual forces are internal and cancel. If you isolate just one bar, the connecting pin force becomes external and must appear.

Key vocabulary

Boundary: the body/system selected. Reaction: force or moment from a restraint. Equilibrium: net force and net moment are zero. External load: force exerted from outside the system boundary.

Assignment submission

Exactly what to submit

Complete your POE 2.4 Free-Body Diagrams classwork and homework worksheet (or notebook equivalent if directed). Use the site to learn and check, but turn in your own annotated diagrams and explanations.

Required evidence

  • Center-loaded beam FBD with known force and appropriate support reactions.
  • Independent hanging-object and crate FBDs with labeled force types.
  • Additional assigned beam/sign/crate practice FBDs from the worksheet.
  • At least one force-equilibrium check with units.
  • A short explanation of what makes a force external to the selected body.

Quality check

  • Object isolated clearly (no support symbols inside the final FBD).
  • External forces identified without duplicates.
  • Support reactions match the constraint type.
  • Arrows and labels are readable, and values have units.
  • Answer includes the independent practice and homework.
