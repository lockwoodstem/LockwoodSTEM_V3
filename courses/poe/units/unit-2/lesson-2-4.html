Vectors & Force Components

Engineering question: How do angled members carry load, and how can one diagonal force be separated into horizontal and vertical effects?

Coming In

What You Already Know

Your FBD describes the external forces on the wing. Inside the wing, many useful structural members are diagonal, so their forces act at angles.

Learning Targets

By the End of This Lesson

  • Represent forces as vectors with magnitude and direction.
  • Resolve an angled force into perpendicular components.
  • Use component reasoning to explain why diagonal bracing can stabilize a structure.

Horizontal component

Fₓ = F cos(θ)

Vertical component

Fᵧ = F sin(θ)

These equations assume θ is measured from the positive horizontal direction. Always sketch the vector triangle before choosing a function.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Use these ideas as your lesson notes. Focus on explaining the behavior, not memorizing vocabulary by itself.

Forces are vectors

A force is not described by magnitude alone. Direction matters because a 20 N horizontal force and a 20 N vertical force affect a structure differently.

Components

An angled force can be replaced by horizontal and vertical components that produce the same combined effect.

Choosing sine and cosine

If angle θ is measured from the horizontal, the horizontal component is adjacent to the angle and the vertical component is opposite it.

Why diagonals matter

A diagonal brace can turn sideways movement into axial tension or compression. Triangulation works because the geometry resists shape change without requiring a flexible rectangle to carry the entire load through bending.

Worked example: 20 N brace force at 30°

If a brace carries 20 N at 30° above horizontal, Fₓ = 20 cos(30°) ≈ 17.3 N and Fᵧ = 20 sin(30°) = 10.0 N. The original diagonal force and the two components are equivalent descriptions of the same effect.

Apply It to Your Wing

Turn the Concept into Engineering Evidence

  1. Sketch a diagonal brace in a wing or truss frame.
  2. Choose an example force magnitude and angle supplied by your teacher or lesson prompt.
  3. Resolve the force into horizontal and vertical components.
  4. Explain in words how the brace redirects load through the structure.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. A 20 N force is 30° above horizontal. Which component uses cosine?

The horizontal component, because it is adjacent to the 30° angle: Fₓ = F cos(θ).

2. Why are triangles structurally useful?

A triangle resists shape change and allows members to carry more of the load as axial tension or compression instead of relying only on bending.
