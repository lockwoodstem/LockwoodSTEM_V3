POE Unit 2 • Mechanics Foundations • Lesson 2.7

Introduction to Truss Systems

Engineering question: Why can a frame of slender members support large loads?

LearnPracticeApplySubmitUnit 2
Foundational instruction

Learn it before the bridge project

Use varied engineering examples to master the mechanics, then apply the technique to your final structural design later in the unit.

Learning Targets

  • Identify top and bottom chords, diagonals, verticals and nodes
  • Explain triangulation
  • Recognize axial force assumptions in ideal planar trusses

Learn the Concept

  • An ideal pin-jointed truss transmits primarily axial tension or compression
  • A triangular arrangement resists geometric distortion better than an unbraced quadrilateral
  • Loads and reactions are ideally applied at joints for a simple truss analysis

During instruction: Sketch the forces, geometry, variables or joints discussed and record units for every quantity.

Practice Together

Compare a rectangular four-member frame with the same frame after adding a diagonal. Predict their behavior under a sideways push.

Show: a labeled diagram, a mathematical model when relevant, and a final statement interpreting the result.

Apply the Skill

Label a simple Pratt and Warren truss; trace a load path and identify a joint where forces must balance.

Check your understanding: Why does a model with rigid glue joints not behave exactly like an ideal pin-jointed truss?

Student Evidence

Labeled truss vocabulary diagram and load-path explanation. Use your engineering notebook or teacher-provided guided notes; include dimensions, units and a conclusion.

Looking ahead: These mechanics techniques will be revisited when students analyze the bridge design in Lessons 2.15–2.20.
