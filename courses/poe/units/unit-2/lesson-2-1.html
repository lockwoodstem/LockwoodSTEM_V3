The Unit Challenge

Lightweight Bridge Structural Design Challenge

Design and manufacture a lightweight bridge that spans a fixed gap between two supports. The engineering problem is to carry a centered load efficiently through members and joints into the supports.

400 mmRequired clear span between supports.
80–120 mmPermitted bridge width.
100 NRequired centered design load.
≤ 10 mmMaximum midspan deflection at 100 N.
≤ 150 gMaximum finished structural mass.
Standard SupportsEvery team uses the same teacher-provided support locations.

Manufacturing Rules

Use approved teacher-supplied structural materials and adhesive. Laser-cut gussets, deck plates, and joint plates are allowed. Small 3D-printed connectors may be used when approved, but the complete bridge may not simply be 3D printed. Final testing uses progressive centered loading and may continue to failure only under teacher supervision.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Use these ideas as your lesson notes. Focus on explaining the behavior, not memorizing vocabulary by itself.

Structure vs. mechanism

A mechanism is designed to move; a structure is designed primarily to carry loads while maintaining its shape. A bridge still flexes, but excessive deformation can make it unusable long before it actually breaks.

Strength

Strength describes how much load a material or structure can resist before yielding, fracturing, buckling, separating at a joint, or otherwise failing.

Stiffness

Stiffness describes resistance to deformation. A bridge may be strong enough not to break but still fail the design if its midspan deflection is too large under the required load.

Structural efficiency

Structural engineers cannot solve every problem by adding more material. A useful structure carries the required load with as little mass as practical.

Why mass alone does not determine success

Imagine Bridge A has a mass of 70 g and carries 45 N before failure. Bridge B has a mass of 45 g and carries 40 N. Bridge A is stronger in absolute load capacity, but Bridge B may be more structurally efficient because it carries nearly as much load with much less mass. Later in the unit you will quantify this tradeoff.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why is “does not break” an incomplete success criterion?

Because the bridge must also meet the stiffness/deflection requirement and mass limit. A structure can remain intact yet deform too much or be too heavy.

2. Why can adding material be a poor structural-engineering solution?

Added material usually increases strength or stiffness, but it also increases mass. Efficient structures must balance performance with mass.
