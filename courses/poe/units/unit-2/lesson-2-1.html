The Unit Challenge

Aircraft Wing Structural Design Challenge

Design and manufacture the internal structure of a cantilever aircraft wing. Aerodynamic shape is not the goal; the engineering problem is to carry load efficiently from the wing into a standardized root fixture.

≥ 300 mmMinimum cantilever span from the root fixture.
≤ 120 mmMaximum wing chord.
20 NRequired design load applied 250 mm from the root.
≤ 20 mmMaximum allowed tip deflection at the 20 N design load.
≤ 75 gMaximum finished structural mass.
Standard RootEvery team uses the same teacher-provided mounting interface.

Manufacturing Rules

Use approved teacher-supplied structural materials and adhesive. Laser-cut ribs, plates, and gussets are allowed. Small 3D-printed fittings may be used when approved, but the complete wing structure may not simply be 3D printed. Final testing uses progressive loading and may continue to failure only under teacher supervision.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Use these ideas as your lesson notes. Focus on explaining the behavior, not memorizing vocabulary by itself.

Structure vs. mechanism

A mechanism is designed to move; a structure is designed primarily to carry loads while maintaining its shape. A wing still flexes, but excessive deformation can make it unusable long before it actually breaks.

Strength

Strength describes how much load a material or structure can resist before yielding, fracturing, buckling, separating at a joint, or otherwise failing.

Stiffness

Stiffness describes resistance to deformation. A wing may be strong enough not to break but still fail the design if its tip bends too far under the required load.

Structural efficiency

Aerospace engineers cannot solve every problem by adding more material. A useful structure carries the required load with as little mass as practical.

Why mass alone does not determine success

Imagine Wing A has a mass of 70 g and carries 45 N before failure. Wing B has a mass of 45 g and carries 40 N. Wing A is stronger in absolute load capacity, but Wing B may be more structurally efficient because it carries nearly as much load with much less mass. Later in the unit you will quantify this tradeoff.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why is “does not break” an incomplete success criterion?

Because the wing must also meet the stiffness/deflection requirement and mass limit. A structure can remain intact yet deform too much or be too heavy.

2. Why can adding material be a poor aerospace solution?

Added material usually increases strength or stiffness, but it also increases mass. Aerospace structures must balance performance with weight.
