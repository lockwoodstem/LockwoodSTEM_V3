Wing Concept Development

Engineering question: How can a team generate multiple genuinely different wing structures before committing to one solution?

Phase 3 · Engineer the Wing

Build evidence, not isolated assignments.

Learn the idea, use it on the wing challenge, and carry the result into the next lesson.

Coming In

What You Already Know

you now have structural principles and material-test evidence. Use them to deliberately generate solutions instead of building the first idea.

Learning Targets

By the End

  • Generate multiple structurally distinct concepts.
  • Show a continuous load path.
  • Communicate geometry, materials, joints, and root attachment clearly.
Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Concepts must differ structurally

Vary spar arrangement, bracing, section type, root connection, or architecture—not just cosmetic shape.

Think in subsystems

Consider root connection, primary spar, secondary spar/bracing, ribs, tip region, and joints.

Design from the load path

Add material because it has a structural job and trace how load returns to the root.

Sketch to communicate

Use clear views, labels, approximate dimensions, material notes, and connection details.

Three valid directions

A single deep spar with ribs, a dual-spar structure, and a truss/box structure are genuinely different because they carry load differently. Three tip shapes on the same spar are not.

Apply It to Your Wing

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Generate at least three substantially different wing concepts.
  2. Show root connection and primary load path for each.
  3. Label materials, spars, ribs, bracing, joints, and approximate dimensions.
  4. Note one predicted advantage and one risk for each.
Lesson Deliverable

Three wing concepts

  • Three clearly different annotated concepts.
  • Root connection and load path shown.
  • Materials, dimensions, and joint strategy labeled.
  • One predicted advantage and one risk for each.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Carry Forward

Why This Matters Next

Lesson 2.13 analyzes the concepts before any decision-matrix scoring.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. What makes concepts genuinely different?

Meaningful differences in structural architecture, geometry, load path, or connection strategy.

2. Why identify weaknesses now?

Early risk identification makes later analysis and selection more realistic.
