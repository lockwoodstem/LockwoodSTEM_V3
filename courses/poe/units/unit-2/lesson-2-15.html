Coming In

What You Already Know

Your team selected a concept. It is still only an idea until dimensions, materials, joints, manufacturing steps, and verification methods are defined.

Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

These notes are intended to be complete enough to learn the core idea directly from the page.

Detailed geometry

Final drawings or CAD should define overall span/chord and the dimensions/locations of spars, ribs, bracing, root connection, and critical joints.

Joint definition

Specify how loads cross each connection: adhesive surface, gusset, tab-and-slot geometry, fastener, printed fitting, or another approved method.

Bill of materials

A BOM identifies each material/component, quantity, size, and purpose. It also supports a mass estimate before you commit to fabrication.

Manufacturing sequence

Plan the order of cutting, dry fitting, alignment, bonding, curing, and inspection. Assembly order can affect dimensional accuracy.

Test plan

Define load locations, increments, measurements, stop conditions, PPE, exclusion zone, and required data before the test starts.

Build-ready test

A teammate who did not create the concept should be able to inspect the design package and answer: What do we cut? What size is it? What material is it? How are pieces connected? In what order do we assemble them? How will we know the wing is ready to test?

Apply It to Your Wing

Turn the Concept into Engineering Evidence

  1. Create the final dimensioned drawing or CAD model.
  2. Finalize materials and joint details.
  3. Prepare a BOM and predicted mass.
  4. Write the manufacturing sequence and inspection checks.
  5. Prepare the final load-test procedure and safety/stop rules.
  6. Submit for teacher design approval before fabrication.
Lesson Deliverable

Wing manufacturing design package

Turn in:

  • Dimensioned drawing or CAD of the selected design.
  • BOM/material list and predicted mass.
  • Joint/root-connection details.
  • Manufacturing sequence.
  • Load-test plan with safety and stop conditions.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, CAD/design file, spreadsheet, photos, or another teacher-approved format as appropriate to the lesson.

Carry Forward

Why This Matters Next

Lesson 2.16 is fabrication. The approved package becomes the baseline used to inspect the actual wing and document any deviations.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why should predicted mass be calculated before fabrication?

It allows the team to catch a likely violation of the 75 g mass limit while changes are still easy to make.

2. Why document the manufacturing sequence?

Assembly order affects alignment, access, curing, accuracy, and the reliability of joints.
