Bridge Design Research & Concepts

Engineering question: Which three bridge layouts are worth developing into detailed engineering designs under our manufacturing and performance constraints?

Bridge Capstone · Phase 1 of Design

Research → three concepts → choose one.

Students will research three structural approaches, draw each in side and top views, show how a 100 N load transfers to the supports and select the strongest candidate for analysis—not a pre-certified winner.

Materials: notebook and graph paper or Fusion, the Lesson 2.14 mass-budget analysis and credible research sources.

Project launch

Your challenge: generate three meaningfully different designs

Until now, students have studied mechanics and materials separately. This is the first lesson in which they apply that evidence to the real 400 mm bridge capstone.

1 · Research

Identify how Pratt, Howe and Warren-type webs differ, what carries the load and what connection challenges appear.

2 · Sketch

Produce three structurally distinct candidate bridges, with dimensions, two side frames, lateral bracing and centered load transfer.

3 · Select

Screen against required limits, compare alternatives and recommend the best concept for calculations in Lesson 2.16.

What counts as a different concept? A different web triangulation, panel arrangement, truss rise or joining/load-transfer system that changes how the structure behaves. Merely changing color, adding decorative fins or renaming the same truss is not a distinct structural concept.

01 / Nonnegotiable design rules

Buildable does not mean unrestricted

Your bridge design must account for both structural performance and the exact fabrication constraints. These values come from the Unit 2 challenge specification.

400 mm

Clear span between supports

65 mm MAX

Overall width including fittings

100 N

Centered qualification load

10 mm MAX

Midspan deflection at 100 N

150 g MAX

Finished mass, everything included

Approved materials

Wood-based main members only

Permitted primary structure

Balsa, basswood, craft sticks or plywood may form the primary structure. A complete laser-cut open-web plywood side truss is allowed, but not a solid plywood side plate. Cross bracing and loading connection must be designed.

Do not use: acrylic, steel/carbon fiber/aluminum/polyethylene/Delrin primary members from the materials lab, or fully 3D-printed bridge bodies.

Connections and manufacturing

Allowed: PVA or CA glue, interlocking wood joints, approved plywood gussets, approved PLA/PETG fittings and M3 nuts/bolts (washers allowed). No screws, hot glue, epoxy, wire or other bolt sizes.

Plywood local gussets ≤40 × 25 mm and ≤800 mm², with overlap ≤20 mm onto each member and at most one layer per side. Printed fittings must meet separate 50 mm/15 g each, 40 g total and specified slicer restrictions.

02 / Bridge research

Three ways to organize a truss web

Every example below is a schematic side elevation for comparison, not a complete build plan. Joint and diagonal orientation changes the force distribution and connection requirements.

Reference pattern A

Pratt-type truss

Diagonals generally slope downward toward midspan in the typical configuration. Under common downward load cases, these diagonals often carry tension while verticals carry compression.

Ask: Are short compression members and many glued joints an advantage for our wood-and-gusset build?

Reference pattern B

Warren-type truss

Repeating triangles can reduce the number of vertical web members and create a simple repeated pattern. Diagonal force directions vary by location and loading.

Ask: Does the central 100 N load land at a suitable joint or need a specially designed crossmember?

Reference pattern C

Howe-type truss

Diagonal orientation is opposite to Pratt. Under common gravity loading, the diagonals are often in compression, while the verticals tend toward tension.

Ask: How will slender diagonals resist buckling, and can the joints transfer those compressive forces?

Be precise: Force directions depend on the exact geometry, restraints and loading. These are common patterns, not universal predictions for every member. In Lesson 2.16 you will test your selected load paths using free-body diagrams and analysis.

03 / Think beyond a side view

How do two truss sides become a complete bridge?

A single 2D truss sketch cannot explain the entire test setup. Students must plan two parallel side trusses, transverse cross-bracing, a centered load-transfer member or platform and actual joining details.

Where does the 100 N go?

  1. The loading nose presses onto a designated central load-transfer point.
  2. A transverse member or joint arrangement transfers that force into both truss sides.
  3. Each side transfers force through diagonals, chords and joints to the supports.
  4. The support interface resists reaction forces without rolling, sliding or local damage.

For a symmetric ideal bridge with a centered load, the total vertical reactions are approximately 50 N at each end. Equal load-sharing between side trusses depends on symmetry and connection stiffness.

What should your top-view sketch show?

  • Both side trusses, separated by enough space for bracing and the loading point
  • Overall maximum width 65 mm, including hardware protrusions
  • Cross-members preventing lateral movement or twisting
  • Location and design of the loading crossmember
  • Connection method for braces, fittings and gussets

Don't solve by adding a solid deck: the bridge still must follow the permitted open-web design and material rules.

09 / Self-check

Work the question before revealing the explanation

Use a sentence, diagram or quick calculation for each answer.

A · Support reactions

Under an ideal centered 100 N vertical load and symmetric supports, what total vertical reaction would you expect at each end?

Reveal an explanation

50 N upward at each end from force and moment equilibrium. This does not guarantee the two side trusses share loads equally; crossmember design matters.

B · Design differences

Two sketches have exactly the same chords and web, but one has a different logo. Are these separate concepts?

Reveal an explanation

No. The structural configuration, loading, cross-bracing or connection engineering needs to change meaningfully, not decoration.

C · Width

Two side trusses are 55 mm apart measured outside-to-outside, but hardware adds 7 mm to each side. Does the bridge satisfy the 65 mm maximum?

Reveal an explanation

No. Overall width is 55 + 7 + 7 = 69 mm, which exceeds 65 mm.

D · Carbon fiber

Why can't the carbon-fiber strip from the 2.13 lab automatically be used as a bridge chord?

Reveal an explanation

It is a laboratory comparison material. Primary members must be approved wood/plywood under this challenge, regardless of strength or stiffness.

E · Truss drawings

Why is a side-view Pratt truss sketch not enough to begin construction?

Reveal an explanation

It does not show the second side truss, lateral stability, overall width, load-introduction crossmember, or detailed joins. The full 3D load path must be conceived.

F · Force predictions

Can you assume every diagonal in a Warren truss is in tension under the class test?

Reveal an explanation

No. Members may carry tension or compression depending on exact geometry, load location and support constraints. Perform structural analysis before labeling forces as definitive.

G · Mass estimate

An initial wood frame mass is estimated at 118 g and connections/bracing add 38 g. Is it below the 150 g limit?

Reveal an explanation

Total = 156 g, which is 6 g over the limit. The design needs a change.

H · Connection choice

A concept uses hot glue and M4 bolts because they are convenient. Can it pass the manufacturing review?

Reveal an explanation

No. Hot glue and M4 bolts are not permitted. The bridge specifies approved adhesives or joints and M3 nuts/bolts if using mechanical fasteners.

Required student evidence

Exactly what to submit

Submit a POE 2.15 Bridge Research and Three-Concept Design Packet as a PDF, teacher-provided template or organized engineering-notebook pages. This is a concept review, not final manufacturing approval.

Your packet must include

  • Research notes from at least two credible sources, including source titles/links and one useful technical observation from each.
  • Three distinct structural concepts (Pratt, Warren, Howe or defensible alternatives), each with a labeled side elevation and top-view sketch.
  • Each side view marked with 400 mm clear span, truss rise, central load and support locations.
  • Each top view showing total width ≤65 mm, lateral bracing, two side frames and centered load transfer.
  • For each design, a proposed approved material and joint/fastener scheme, a preliminary mass estimate and two possible failure or fabrication risks.
  • A completed weighted decision matrix for all three designs, with one sentence explaining the rationale for every major score.
  • A four- to six-sentence recommendation naming the preferred concept and at least two evidence-backed trade-offs.

Before turning it in

  • Concepts differ structurally, not cosmetically.
  • Dimensioned drawing views are legible and use millimeters.
  • The load path to both bridge sides and supports is clearly indicated.
  • Construction plan uses approved materials and fastener sizes; plywood and printed fitting limits are acknowledged.
  • Mass estimates include bracing and connections, not only chords.
  • Speculative performance and mass estimates are labeled as assumptions, not tested results.
  • The preferred design is a candidate for Lesson 2.16 analysis, not a claimed guaranteed winner.

Due date: Follow the Daily Agenda and current teacher submission directions.
