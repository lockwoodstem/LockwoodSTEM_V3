Bridge Load Testing

Engineering question: Does the finished bridge carry the required 100 N with no more than 10 mm midspan deflection—and what do the real measurements reveal?

The official 100 N criterion applies to the whole bridge, not independently to each side frame. Meeting a mass or load-to-mass target does not override other dimensional, deflection or manufacturing restrictions. Check complete rules ↗.

02 / Understand the apparatus

Two separate measurements: force and midpoint deflection

Apparatus checklist

  • Teacher-signed 2.18 pre-test inspection and a labeled finished bridge revision.
  • Rigid fixed support fixture with the required clear span; no rocking or slip.
  • Mounted calibrated digital force gauge and a teacher-approved rated loading mechanism.
  • Independent fixed-datum midpoint displacement gauge, dial indicator or properly calibrated viewing setup.
  • Digital scale and calipers for actual mass, clear span and maximum outside width.
  • Guarding, eye protection and an exclusion area for failure debris.

Establish an accurate zero

  1. Teacher verifies fixture capacity, alignment and support conditions.
  2. Locate the intended central loading crossmember and ensure direct vertical loading without eccentricity.
  3. Set the force gauge zero according to its operating instructions and the approved preload procedure.
  4. Measure the bridge's unloaded midpoint reference with a stationary independent datum.
  5. Record all subsequent force and midpoint movement from the same baseline; do not confuse actuator travel with actual bridge deflection.

Do not improvise a press or push by hand in place of a rated mounting system. Gauge and fixture ratings must be suitable for the load being applied.

03 / Test safety

Teacher controls the force; observers stay clear

Before starting

  • Verify that the finished bridge is approved, has cured, and has no unreconciled defects.
  • Check rated capacity of force gauge, attachments, fixture and loading contact.
  • Set teacher-defined force steps, ramp/hold time and maximum allowed force.
  • Position the bridge securely and establish the fixed 0 mm reference.
  • Assign one operator, a load reader, a deflection reader and a recorder.

Stop immediately for

  • Unexpected crack, joint separation or progressive splintering.
  • Sideways twist, buckling, loss of support contact or sliding fixture.
  • Sudden uncontrolled deformation or force drop.
  • Force gauge or fixture operating outside its rated range.
  • Any unsafe condition or teacher stop command.

No students touch the loaded bridge. The instructor directs unloading and evaluates whether the test can safely continue.

Policy: No repair is permitted after the official test begins. If the bridge cannot be tested safely, record an incomplete or stopped test. Testing beyond 100 N for a greater demonstrated load or failure study is optional, instructor-approved and requires a suitably rated guarded rig.

04 / Repeatable procedure

Follow these six steps in order

1 · Record pre-test

Bridge ID/revision, team, actual mass, span, overall width, gauge ID and signed inspection.

2 · Position and zero

Set the supports to the real clear span, center the loading nose, zero force and record the fixed midpoint reference.

3 · Apply approved load

Instructor advances to the next authorized increment and allows the reading to stabilize as required.

4 · Log a paired reading

Record actual F (N), actual midspan δ (mm), reading order, visible damage and any measurement uncertainty.

5 · Document 100 N

If safely achieved, enter the actual simultaneous force and deflection values at 100 N; record whether deflection is at or below 10 mm.

6 · Stop, inspect and save

Teacher stops for completion, apparatus limit or structural damage. Record why, retain photos and export data without repairing the bridge.

Whole-bridge stiffness

kbridge ≈ ΔF / Δδ   [N/mm]

The calculator reports a through-origin approximation based on the first three-to-five positive paired loading readings at or below 100 N. This assumes correctly zeroed instruments and an approximately linear initial region. If that assumption is not reasonable, discuss the graph without using a single stiffness value.

Performance per mass

ηqualification = 100 N / finished mass (g)
ηobserved = highest verified observed F (N) / mass (g)

These are supported load per gram calculations. Neither is a measured ultimate failure efficiency unless failure was actually observed and its force verified. A heavier or noncompliant bridge can have high N/g and still fail qualification.

08 / Structural behavior

Classify visible changes and damage

What can fail?

  • Compression diagonal or top chord bows or buckles.
  • Plywood gusset peels or adhesive separates, or M3 bearing zone splits.
  • Whole bridge twists due to weak lateral bracing.
  • Local center loading surface or end support crushes.
  • Bridge remains upright but exceeds the 10 mm displacement limit at 100 N.

Document for 2.20

Mark the actual defect on a photograph or sketch; record the first observed load if known; compare its location to predicted compression forces and weak joints from 2.16; and propose two evidence-driven revisions.

Do not repair during or after the official test. Lesson 2.20 is a redesign analysis, not permission to invalidate the tested result.

09 / Independent check

Test your reasoning

1 · Exact qualification reading

You recorded 95 N at 8 mm and 105 N at 11 mm, but nothing at 100 N. Does the software certify a pass?

Check answer

No. An interpolated estimate might be informative, but qualification requires observed data at the test load. Record the missing measurement; do not call it a pass.

2 · Actual bridge mass

The Fusion mass estimate was 140 g but the actual completed bridge weighs 153 g. Which value counts?

Check answer

153 g, the measured finished mass. It exceeds the 150 g maximum regardless of the CAD estimate.

3 · Qualification deflection

A bridge carries 100 N with a measured 9.2 mm midpoint deflection. What else must be checked?

Check answer

It meets the numeric deflection criterion. Also verify true supported span, total width, mass, approved construction and actual teacher-controlled test evidence.

4 · Demonstrated vs. ultimate

Testing stops without failure at exactly 100 N. Is the bridge's ultimate strength 100 N?

Check answer

No. 100 N is the highest demonstrated force if it was the highest measured. Failure force remains unknown.

5 · Ratio calculation

A tested bridge weighing 125 g supports 100 N. Calculate qualification load per mass.

Check answer

100 N / 125 g = 0.800 N/g. This does not compensate for any failed dimensional or deflection requirement.

6 · Actual stiffness

Measured force changes by 60 N while deflection changes by 4 mm in a linear region. What is the approximate slope?

Check answer

ΔF/Δδ = 15 N/mm, a whole-bridge stiffness under the actual support and load configuration, not the wood's Young modulus.

7 · Safety

A joint begins opening and the gauge is close to its rated force. Should students continue until the bridge breaks?

Check answer

No. The teacher stops and documents the safe maximum observed force; deliberate failure testing requires separate approved capacity, guarding and direction.

8 · After the run

The bridge's central gusset peels at 80 N during official testing. May the team re-glue and retest immediately?

Check answer

No. No repairs are allowed once the official test begins. Record the failure and plan an evidence-based redesign in 2.20.

Assignment deliverable

Exactly what students must submit for 2.19

Bridge Load Test & Structural Performance Report

  • Team ID, revision, test date, approved test setup and instrument information.
  • Actual bridge mass, clear span and overall width from physical measurements.
  • An annotated diagram or photograph of center loading and the independent deflection reference.
  • All genuine paired force–deflection data, including exact 100 N reading if achieved.
  • Labeled graph with the 100 N and 10 mm thresholds.
  • Clearly documented numeric qualification result and any unverified requirements.
  • Highest observed load, test stop reason, photos and the earliest observed damage.
  • N/g efficiency calculation where actual force and mass are known.
  • Comparison with 2.16 predicted compression/joint risks and two recommendations for 2.20.
  • CSV/summary export or printed four-page packet plus at least three labeled photos/stills.

Scientific integrity check

  • No fabricated or preset force–deflection data.
  • At 100 N, deflection is a genuine simultaneous reading—not an interpolated estimate.
  • Highest observed load is not automatically ultimate failure strength.
  • Instrument and fixture limits are respected; stopped tests are reported honestly.
  • No repairs once official testing starts.
  • Units are N, mm, g and N/g; clear span and total width are checked.
  • Actual physical results are separated from 2.16 predictions.
  • Teacher confirms qualification and manufacturing compliance.
