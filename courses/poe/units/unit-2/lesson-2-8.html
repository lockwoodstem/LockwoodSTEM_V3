Calculating Truss Forces

01 / Fundamentals

How does the method of joints work?

Every joint of an ideal truss is in equilibrium. If you isolate one joint, all connected members exert forces along their own axes, and those forces must balance the applied loads and support reactions at that joint. Read the reference method ↗

Whole-truss FBDStart with supports and external forces. Reactions usually come from ΣM = 0, ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣFᵧ = 0.
Joint FBDIsolate a single pin as a dot. Draw connected member forces along member directions plus any external loads at that joint.
At most two unknownsA 2D joint gives two independent force equations: ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣFᵧ = 0. Start with a joint containing no more than two unknowns.

Assume unknown members are in tension

Initially draw an unknown member force away from the isolated joint. After solving, a positive force means tension (a pull). A negative force means compression (a push); the real force acts in the direction opposite the assumed arrow.

Rule: +F = tension under the outward-arrow convention; −F = compression.

Ideal-model assumptions

Members are straight, weightless two-force members; ideal joints are frictionless pins; loads act at joints; and the structure is in static equilibrium. Actual manufactured bridges have connection stiffness, self-weight, imperfections and bending effects.

Critical: A calculated axial force is not a load limit or proof that a bridge will withstand that force. Real designs also require member strength, buckling, connection and stability checks.

Whole structure: ΣM = 0Individual joint: ΣFₓ = 0Individual joint: ΣFᵧ = 0
02 / Your repeatable process

A five-step method students can follow

1 · Draw the trussMark all loads, supports, geometry and joint labels.
2 · Find reactionsUse whole-truss moment and force equilibrium.
3 · Choose a jointStart where at most two member forces are unknown.
4 · Resolve & solveDraw outward assumed-tension arrows; use x/y components.

5 · Transfer member forces and check

When a member force is found, transfer its correct direction to the joint at the other end. For tension, the member pulls both joints toward itself along its axis (away from each isolated joint); for compression, it pushes outward on each joint. Check force equilibrium at a remaining joint to catch sign errors.

Geometry shortcut: For a right triangle with rise h, horizontal run r and diagonal length d, sin θ = h/d and cos θ = r/d. Units cancel in these ratios.

04 / Fully worked example

Analyze an entire triangular truss

A triangular truss spans 4.0 m with supports A (pin) and B (roller). Apex C is centered 1.5 m above the base. An external 120 N downward point load acts at C. Neglect the truss's self-weight.

Step 1 · Reactions

Find Aᵧ and Bᵧ

Use the whole truss, with CCW moments positive and coordinates measured from A.

ΣMA = Bᵧ(4) −120(2) = 0

Bᵧ = 60 N upward.

ΣFᵧ = Aᵧ +60−120 = 0

Aᵧ = 60 N upward; Aₓ = 0 because no horizontal external load acts.

Step 2 · Geometry

Resolve member directions

Each diagonal has a horizontal run of 2.0 m and vertical rise of 1.5 m.

Diagonal length d = √(2² + 1.5²) = 2.5 m.

sin θ = 1.5/2.5 = 0.6
cos θ = 2.0/2.5 = 0.8

θ = arctan(1.5/2) ≈ 36.87° above the horizontal. These two ratios will simplify both joint equations.

Step 3 · Joint C

Calculate AC and BC (diagonals)

Start with both unknown member forces assumed in tension: at joint C, draw arrows along CA and CB pointing down-left and down-right. Symmetry gives FAC = FBC = F.

ΣFᵧ = −0.6F−0.6F−120 = 0

Solve: −1.2F = 120 ⇒ F = −100 N.

A negative force under the assumed-tension convention means each member is actually in compression, magnitude 100 N. The diagram shows the corrected actual forces pushing upward on C.

FAC = FBC = −100 N (C)
Step 4 · Joint A

Calculate AB (bottom tie)

The 60 N upward reaction is known. AC is now known to be 100 N in compression, so at joint A it acts down-left.

ΣFₓ = FAB −100(0.8) = 0

FAB = +80 N, so member AB is in tension.

ΣFᵧ = 60−100(0.6) = 0

The vertical force check at A is satisfied. The positive 80 N value is consistent with the bottom member pulling joint A to the right.

Step 5 · Final check and results

QuantitySigned (tension-positive)Physical interpretation
Aᵧ+60 NUpward external reaction at A
Bᵧ+60 NUpward external reaction at B
AC−100 NCompression
BC−100 NCompression
AB+80 NTension

Joint B check: ΣFᵧ = +60−100(0.6) = 0; ΣFₓ = −80+100(0.8) = 0. All joint equilibrium conditions are satisfied. The signed member forces are not the same as the positive magnitudes you would use for an eventual strength calculation.

Interactive truss FBD

1 · Whole-truss reactions

Begin with the applied load and the supports. The load is centered, so each vertical reaction equals half the total downward load.

ΣFᵧ = Aᵧ + Bᵧ − P = 0

Assumptions: ideal symmetric pin-jointed triangle, load applied downward only at C, weightless members and no other loads. Display shows internal force demand, not allowable capacity or a failure prediction.

Prediction A: Double the load

Keep h = 1.50 m. Change P from 60 to 120 N. What happens to all calculated reactions and member forces?

Check

Every calculated force doubles. This ideal static analysis is linear in P while geometry is unchanged.

Prediction B: Make the triangle taller

Keep P constant and increase h from 1.00 m to 3.00 m. How do the diagonal compression and bottom-tie tension magnitudes change?

Check

Both decrease. The diagonals become steeper, so each supplies more upward force per unit axial force; the bottom tie also needs less tension to balance the horizontal components. External support reactions remain P/2 each.

06 / Quick second example

100 N load on a 45° triangular truss

Use a span of 4 m, a rise of 2 m and a centered downward 100 N apex load. The two diagonals make 45° angles with the horizontal.

Apply the same method

  1. External reactions: Aᵧ = Bᵧ = 100/2 = 50 N up.
  2. Joint C: −2F sin45° −100 = 0 ⇒ F = −70.71 N.
  3. Thus each diagonal has 70.71 N compression.
  4. Joint A: FAB −70.71 cos45° = 0 ⇒ AB = +50.00 N tension.

Why 45° is useful

sin45° = cos45° ≈ 0.7071. The equal diagonal component fractions simplify the calculations.

70.71(0.7071) ≈ 50.00 N

Remember: Steeper geometry can reduce required axial forces in this particular model, but that does not by itself decide which truss design is strongest or most material-efficient.

07 / Preview a special case

What is a zero-force member?

An unloaded truss joint with three connected members, two collinear, provides a useful shortcut: the third, noncollinear member has zero axial force for that specific load case.

Why member JC is zero

At joint J, member AJ and JB lie on the same horizontal line. There is no external load or support reaction applied at J.

ΣFᵧ = FJC = 0

Because JC is the only vertical force on the isolated joint, its axial force must equal 0 N.

Not a reason to remove JC from a real bridge. It may be important for bracing, buckling resistance or a different future load case.

08 / Your engineering task

Analyze, annotate and interpret a simple truss

Complete the assigned 2.8 worksheet or notebook pages. The default practice case is the 4.0 m span, 1.5 m rise triangle shown above; your teacher may assign a different force or geometry.

Part A · Draw and organize

  1. Draw the entire truss with joint letters, dimensions, applied load and supports.
  2. Sketch its whole-structure FBD and find all support reactions.
  3. Draw separate FBDs of joints C and A, with assumed-tension arrows on unknown members.
  4. Mark horizontal/vertical component ratios for each diagonal.

Part B · Calculate and explain

  1. Solve at C using ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣFᵧ = 0 (symmetry may simplify).
  2. Carry the now-known AC force to joint A and solve AB.
  3. Check equilibrium at B.
  4. Report every force magnitude in N and mark each member T or C.
  5. Explain why a negative result under your assumed sign convention indicates compression.
09 / Independent checks

Try each problem before viewing its solution

Use paper for free-body diagrams, equations and final results. Write force signs and whether members are in tension or compression.

Check A · Symmetric joint

100 N load and 45° diagonals

Joint C has a 100 N downward load and two identical 45° members. Assume tension for both unknowns. Determine their axial force and classify them.

Reveal worked reasoning

Symmetry gives F1 = F2 = F. From ΣFᵧ = −2F sin45°−100=0, F = −70.71 N. Both members are in compression; magnitudes are 70.71 N each.

Check B · Joint selection

Where should you start?

A truss joint has three completely unknown member forces and no other information. Can you solve all three forces using the two independent equations at this single planar joint?

Reveal worked reasoning

No. A planar joint supplies ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣFᵧ = 0: two independent scalar equations. Start at another joint with at most two unknown member forces, or solve known reactions first.

Check C · 3–4–5 geometry

Resolve a 50 N diagonal

A member force of 50 N acts upward and right, along a diagonal with horizontal run 4 and rise 3. What are its signed x and y components?

Reveal worked reasoning

Diagonal length is 5, so Fₓ = +50(4/5) = +40 N, Fᵧ = +50(3/5) = +30 N. This resolves a force, not a complete truss solution.

Check D · Compression convention

What does −85 N mean?

You drew a member force arrow away from an isolated joint and calculated −85 N. Is your answer automatically wrong? What force does the member actually exert on that joint?

Reveal worked reasoning

The negative sign means the assumed outward tension direction was incorrect. The member has 85 N compression and pushes on the joint opposite the outward assumed arrow.

Check E · Change the height

100 N apex load, 4 m span, 1 m rise

For a symmetric triangle with halfspan 2 m, determine the magnitude of compression in each diagonal and the bottom tie tension.

Reveal worked reasoning

sinθ = 1/√5 = 0.44721, cosθ = 2/√5 = 0.89443. Each diagonal magnitude = 100/[2(0.44721)] = 111.80 N compression. Tie = 111.80(0.89443) = 100.00 N tension. Reactions are 50 N each.

Check F · Zero-force member

A three-member joint with no external load

At joint J, JA and JB are collinear and JC is noncollinear. No external load or support reaction is applied at J. Determine the member force JC.

Reveal worked reasoning

Member JC = 0 N for this idealized loading case. The perpendicular component of the other two collinear forces is zero; equilibrium forces the noncollinear member force to be zero.

Key vocabulary

Axial force: force along a member's length. Tension: member pulled outward. Compression: member pushed inward. Joint equilibrium: balance of all x and y forces acting on one isolated joint.

Student submission

Exactly what to submit

Complete the POE 2.8 Calculating Truss Forces classwork and homework worksheet (or an assigned notebook equivalent), including the full method-of-joints analysis. Use the instructional videos and revealed checks as study aids, not a substitute for showing your own calculations.

Required evidence

  • A labeled whole-truss sketch with pin/roller, joint letters, load and dimensions.
  • Support reaction calculations using whole-truss equilibrium and correct units.
  • At least two individual joint FBDs with member force arrows and known external forces.
  • Correct sine/cosine components and ΣFₓ = 0 / ΣFᵧ = 0 equations.
  • Numerical member force results with each member marked T (tension) or C (compression).
  • At least one independent joint equilibrium check plus the assigned homework.

Before you submit

  • Every force calculation is labeled in newtons (N).
  • Your outward-arrow convention is stated, and negative results interpreted as compression.
  • All triangles and support reactions match the dimensions in your sketch.
  • Answers are checked at a different joint whenever possible.
  • Diagrams and calculations are readable and organized.
