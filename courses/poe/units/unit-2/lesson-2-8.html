Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Stiffness vs. strength

Stiffness is resistance to deformation; strength is resistance to failure. They are related but not interchangeable.

Density

Density controls mass for a given volume. Low density is useful only when the material still provides enough performance.

Toughness and brittleness

Tough materials absorb energy before fracture. Brittle materials may fail suddenly with little warning.

Manufacturability

A material can look excellent on a property chart but still be poor for the project if it cannot be cut, joined, or formed reliably.

Joint compatibility

Adhesives, gussets, tabs, fasteners, and printed fittings impose different requirements on the materials they connect.

Selection is a tradeoff

A light wood strip may be easy to cut and glue but vulnerable to splitting. Thin plywood may be heavier but stable and useful for gussets, deck plates, or cross members. A printed fitting may provide precise geometry but is inefficient if used as the entire bridge.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. Why is density alone not enough to choose a bridge material?

The material must also provide enough stiffness, strength, reliability, and manufacturability.

2. Why does joint compatibility matter?

Loads must cross connections, so unreliable joining can create the weakest point in the load path.
