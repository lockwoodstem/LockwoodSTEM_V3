Calculating Truss Forces

Engineering question: How can equilibrium reveal the force inside each truss member?

LearnPracticeApplySubmitUnit 2
Foundational instruction

Learn it before the bridge project

Use varied engineering examples to master the mechanics, then apply the technique to your final structural design later in the unit.

Learning Targets

  • Find external support reactions first
  • Draw a free-body diagram of an individual joint
  • Use ΣFx and ΣFy to solve member forces

Learn the Concept

  • Method of joints: start at a joint with no more than two unknown member forces
  • Assume each unknown member is in tension (force points away from the joint); negative result means compression
  • Solve joint-by-joint using force components

During instruction: Sketch the forces, geometry, variables or joints discussed and record units for every quantity.

Practice Together

At a joint, a 100 N downward load is supported by two identical diagonal members at 45° to the horizontal. Assuming symmetry, solve the axial magnitude in each member.

Show: a labeled diagram, a mathematical model when relevant, and a final statement interpreting the result.

Apply the Skill

Analyze a supplied small symmetrical truss: calculate reactions, then resolve forces at a joint with at most two unknowns.

Check your understanding: Why is a negative member force not automatically an incorrect answer?

Student Evidence

Worked method-of-joints calculations. Use your engineering notebook or teacher-provided guided notes; include dimensions, units and a conclusion.

Looking ahead: These mechanics techniques will be revisited when students analyze the bridge design in Lessons 2.15–2.20.
