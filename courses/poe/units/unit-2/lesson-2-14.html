POE Unit 2 • Lesson 2.14

Design Evaluation & Concept Selection

Engineering question: How can a team select a bridge concept using weighted engineering evidence instead of preference?

Phase 3 · Engineer the Bridge

Build evidence, not isolated assignments.

Learn the idea, use it on the bridge challenge, and carry the result into the next lesson.

Coming In

What You Already Know

you now have evidence for three concepts. Today that evidence becomes a transparent design decision.

Learning Targets

By the End

  • Use a weighted decision matrix correctly.
  • Justify scores with evidence rather than opinion.
  • Select a concept and identify changes required before fabrication.
Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Mission-based criteria

Use criteria tied to success: stiffness, structural efficiency, load-path quality, predicted mass, manufacturability, and reliability.

Weights communicate priority

Weights should total 100%. More important criteria receive more influence.

Scores need rationale

A score without evidence is weak. Tie ratings to analysis, measurements, testing, or explicit engineering judgment.

Matrix limits

A matrix supports judgment; it does not make unsafe or infeasible concepts acceptable.

Suggested weighting

Predicted stiffness 25%, structural efficiency 25%, load-path quality 20%, predicted mass 15%, manufacturability 10%, reliability 5%. You may adjust these only if your teacher approves the rationale.

Apply It to Your Bridge

Create Engineering Evidence

  1. Confirm criteria and weights.
  2. Score all concepts using one consistent scale.
  3. Write rationale for important scores.
  4. Calculate weighted totals and select the concept.
  5. List at least two refinements needed before detailed design.
Lesson Deliverable

Decision matrix + design selection

  • Completed weighted decision matrix.
  • Evidence-based score rationales.
  • Selected concept identified clearly.
  • Two or more required refinements before detailed design.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, digital file, photos, CAD, spreadsheet, or teacher-approved equivalent.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. What does a higher weight mean?

That criterion has greater influence on the total score.

2. Why define the scoring scale first?

A common scale improves consistency and reduces preference-driven scoring.
