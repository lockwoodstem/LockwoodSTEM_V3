Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

These notes are intended to be complete enough to learn the core idea directly from the page.

Criteria should match the mission

Use criteria that affect success: predicted stiffness, structural efficiency, load-path quality, predicted mass, manufacturability, and reliability.

Weights communicate priority

Weights should total 100%. A higher weight means performance in that criterion has a larger influence on the decision.

Scores need rationale

A number without a reason is weak evidence. Each score should be traceable to analysis, measurements, test results, or clearly stated engineering judgment.

The matrix informs—not replaces—engineering judgment

If a matrix result conflicts with a major safety or feasibility problem, fix the matrix assumptions or reject the unsafe concept.

Scoring rule

If using a 1–5 score, define what the ends of the scale mean before scoring. For example, 1 could mean “unlikely to meet the requirement based on evidence,” while 5 means “strong evidence suggests it will meet or exceed the requirement.” Apply the same scale to every concept.

Apply It to Your Wing

Turn the Concept into Engineering Evidence

  1. Choose or confirm criteria and weights.
  2. Score all three concepts using the same scale.
  3. Write evidence-based rationale for every important score.
  4. Calculate weighted totals and select the concept.
  5. List at least two revisions needed before the design is build-ready.
Lesson Deliverable

Decision matrix + design selection

Turn in:

  • Completed weighted decision matrix.
  • Score rationales tied to prior evidence.
  • Selected concept identified clearly.
  • Two or more required refinements before detailed design.

Format: Engineering notebook, class template, CAD/design file, spreadsheet, photos, or another teacher-approved format as appropriate to the lesson.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. What does a higher criterion weight mean?

Performance in that criterion has a larger influence on the total selection score.

2. Why should teams define the scoring scale before evaluating concepts?

A common scale makes scores more consistent and reduces the chance of changing standards to favor a preferred concept.
