Principles of Engineering • Unit 2 • Lesson 2.20

Bridge Design & Testing Presentation

Engineering question: How can we convince an audience—using real design evidence and honest measurements—that we understand why our bridge performed the way it did?

Assignment BriefSlide-by-Slide GuideVisual Examples100-Point RubricWhat to Submit
Final Bridge Capstone Presentation

Tell the engineering story of your team's bridge.

Create and give a 6–8 minute presentation to the class, followed by 2–3 minutes of questions. Every team member must present a meaningful section.

Product: one team slide deck and a live in-class presentation—not a newly fabricated redesign.

01 / Presentation assignment

Your final deliverable is a presentation to the class

Summarize the entire design–build–test process using evidence from Lessons 2.15–2.19. Your audience should understand what you designed, why you designed it, what you actually made, how it was tested, and what the results mean.

Presentation format

One team deck prepared in Google Slides, PowerPoint or equivalent. Use approximately 8–10 focused content slides (the outline below suggests ten). Embed real photos, Fusion screenshots and at least one readable test graph.

Time and participation

6–8 minutes speaking plus 2–3 minutes Q&A. Every team member speaks and should be prepared to answer an engineering question. Assign slide owners, rehearse handoffs and check that the class can read your graph.

Grading principle

A bridge that failed can earn full presentation credit if its measurements, engineering reasoning and lessons learned are accurate and well communicated. Missing or invented results must never be presented as successes.

Suggested preparation schedule: 1 period to assemble the deck and organize evidence, 1 period to refine figures and rehearse, and class presentation time as scheduled. Finished slides are submitted before speaking as directed by the teacher.

02 / What to include

Five things the audience must learn

Bridge design decisions

Compare the three concepts, identify the final truss design, show the Fusion assembly, and name the engineering trade-offs that drove the choice.

Predictions before testing

Include at least one real free-body/member-force calculation or risk assessment from 2.16. Label theoretical assumptions and predicted weaknesses.

Fabrication decisions

Show actual photos and approved materials/joints. Report final measured mass and one fabrication issue, modification or quality check.

Real experimental results

Present the actual 2.19 force–deflection graph, 100 N / 10 mm target, maximum observed load, actual test condition and numerical qualification evidence.

Engineering conclusion

Explain what matched or differed from analysis, identify the likely failure/deflection mechanism and propose two justified design improvements—without rebuilding the bridge.

Sources and credit

Credit externally sourced research visuals/information and identify your own CAD, measurements and team photographs. The class should be able to distinguish actual evidence from illustrative examples.

If your bridge never reached 100 N: Show the highest observed force and the actual reason the run stopped. State that the at-100-N requirement is unverified rather than inventing a value. If your bridge did not fail, do not claim an ultimate failure load.

04 / Examples of strong engineering visuals

Show the audience the evidence, not paragraphs about it

Illustration only: The graph above and its geometry are placeholders, not data from your bridge. You must replace them with the actual 2.19 graph, properly labeled axes (N and mm), and actual masses and force readings. In particular, show the 100 N / 10 mm requirement even if you did not achieve it.

Effective design slide

Use one large Fusion assembly screenshot with colored arrows or callouts for the center load, compression or tension forces, support reactions, and lateral bracing. Put dimensions on the image, not inside a dense paragraph.

Presentation sentence: “We selected this layout because ___, but our analysis predicted ___ could be vulnerable.”

Effective results slide

Use the real force–deflection graph alongside a short qualification table. Discuss the result verbally. Add a damage photo where helpful and identify what was observed versus predicted.

Presentation sentence: “At ___ N, the midpoint moved ___ mm; the requirement was ___, so our conclusion is ___.”

05 / Rehearsal and class Q&A

Prepare to explain the engineering rather than read slides

How to Avoid Death by PowerPoint — David JP Phillips, TEDx

Watch for: one main idea per slide, simple visuals and contrast that guides attention. Your engineering evidence still needs legible axes, quantities, and units.

Watch on YouTube ↗

Practical rehearsal checklist

  • Time the entire talk aloud; target 6–8 minutes total, not 6–8 minutes per student.
  • Everyone has a meaningful speaking part and knows when to hand off.
  • Slides use readable labels and contrast visible from the back of the classroom.
  • Units appear on every graph axis and every engineering result.
  • Each statement of “pass,” “failure,” “max load,” or “predicted” is supported by its correct evidence.
  • Each team member can explain one design decision, one real measurement, and one likely failure mechanism.
  • Practice responding to a question without reading the slide word for word.

Questions classmates may ask

1. Why did you choose a Pratt/Warren/Howe layout?

Answer guidance

Reference the 2.15 decision matrix and a structural trade-off such as compression-member buckling risk, achievable joints or expected mass.

2. How did your 2.16 calculations compare with the test?

Answer guidance

State a predicted force or risk, cite actual observed behavior, and explain which assumptions of the model were too simple.

3. Did your bridge actually meet every requirement?

Answer guidance

Show measured span, width, mass, and a real 100 N / deflection pair if reached; distinguish an inconclusive test from a failed numerical result.

4. What load did the bridge really withstand?

Answer guidance

Report the highest demonstrated measured force. Do not call it ultimate strength unless failure was observed and its load established.

5. What would you change without adding too much mass?

Answer guidance

Identify the most consequential failure/deflection mechanism, a feasible change using permitted material/joints, and its expected drawback.

6. What measurement would you improve next time?

Answer guidance

Mention an uncertainty in fixture alignment, force-gauge calibration, fixed midspan reference, load step resolution, or mass measurement.

07 / Exact deliverables

What to submit and what to do in class

Submit before your scheduled presentation

  • One team Google Slides link or editable PowerPoint presentation (as assigned).
  • A PDF export of the same slides so the presentation can be graded even if links or media fail.
  • Actual team CAD screenshots, dimensioned bridge photo(s), fabrication images and labeled load–deflection graph integrated into the deck.
  • At least one engineering analysis visual from 2.16 and a correct, clearly labeled results/qualification table from 2.19.
  • Two proposed redesign recommendations grounded in observed evidence—not a new physical build.
  • Source credits on visuals or a final references area/slide.

Present to your classmates

  • Speak for approximately 6–8 minutes per team.
  • Every member explains a meaningful portion of the engineering process.
  • Lead the audience through the bridge challenge, design, analysis, manufacture, test and results.
  • Show the real graph and state which qualification criteria were met, failed or unverified.
  • Answer classmates' questions for approximately 2–3 minutes.
  • Be prepared to explain limitations honestly, including incomplete or unsuccessful tests.

Due date and presentation order: Check the Daily Agenda and teacher announcement.

No second build or retest is required for Lesson 2.20. Proposed improvements are presented as drawings, annotations, or engineering explanations. Your original bridge test data remain unchanged.
