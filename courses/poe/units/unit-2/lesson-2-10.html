01 / Understand what is being calculated

What can a truss simulation tell us?

A truss solver models axial-only members connected at idealized joints. It combines node coordinates, member properties, restraints and external loads to calculate the forces and motion needed for equilibrium.

Outputs

What a simple 2D truss model estimates

  • Support reactions: forces exerted by restraints, in N.
  • Axial member forces: tension (+) or compression (−), in N.
  • Nodal displacements: how far joints move in x/y, in mm, if E and member cross-section area A are provided.
  • Total member length: useful as a material-use estimate, not finished mass.
Model limitations

What the solver doesn't automatically prove

Axial force is not failure capacity. Even with small simulated displacement, a real member may buckle, crack or fail at a connection. A 2D side-frame model omits the deck, lateral bracing, joints, manufacturing tolerance and three-dimensional effects.

Do not treat the simulated result as a safety certification or a complete check of the later Unit 2 bridge challenge.

Axial stiffness = EA / L  ·  Average axial stress = F / A

E is elastic modulus (N/mm²); A is cross-sectional area (mm²); L is member length (mm). Truss force and deformation depend on geometry, loading, and stiffness under the chosen assumptions.

Six-step simulation workflow

1. Create jointsEnter coordinates from the table; avoid accidental duplicate, unconnected nodes.
2. Add membersConnect the seven pairs as pin-jointed, axial truss elements.
3. Add restraintsPin A; roller C. Missing restraints can make the model unstable.
4. Apply force and EA100 N down at B, and the same E/A for all members.
5. Solve and examineRecord support reactions, member forces and vertical displacement at B.
6. Compare fairlyDuplicate the model, change D/E height only and solve again.
Model debugging: An unstable/singular result often means a missing restraint, unconnected joint or mechanism. Unexpectedly large displacements often indicate incorrect units or insufficient stiffness. Check N vs. kN, mm vs. m, and whether the tool uses N/mm² or another modulus unit.

Investigation 1 · Vary geometry

With P = 100 N, E = 3,000 N/mm² and area = 25 mm², compare a rise of 90 mm to 140 mm. Which design bends less? Which uses more ideal material volume?

Reveal answer

The 140 mm-rise truss predicts smaller center deflection and lower maximum compression under this model, but uses more total ideal member length and volume for the same cross-section. This is a trade-off, not proof of greater real-world capacity.

Investigation 2 · Vary modulus

Keep the geometry and load fixed and double the shared E value. Predict what happens to displacement and member axial forces.

Reveal answer

In this statically determinate, linear-elastic truss, doubling E halves displacement while axial member-force results remain unchanged when geometry and load are fixed.

06 / Validate before you trust

Check the simulation using statics

A good engineering report compares software results to at least one independent hand calculation.

Whole-structure reaction check

ΣMA = Cᵧ(400) −P(200) = 0
Aᵧ = Cᵧ = P / 2

For P = 100 N, expect 50 N up at each end and zero horizontal reaction. If the software disagrees, check the support and load definitions.

Six model-quality checks

  • Correct node coordinates and connected members
  • Pin at A; roller at C; no unintended extra constraints
  • Loads applied at a joint, downward
  • Units consistent: N, mm, N/mm² and mm²
  • Reactions and applied loads balance
  • Reported displacement is realistic relative to the span; large motion can invalidate small-deflection assumptions
07 / Independent engineering activity

Design, simulate and explain two alternatives

Prepare two versions of the five-joint truss with 400 mm span, 100 N at the center and identical supports and E/A. Change one geometry feature, such as upper-joint rise, and defend your choice using calculated evidence.

Step A · Prepare both models

  1. Sketch two labeled trusses and record node coordinates.
  2. Record all seven member connections, supports, applied load, E and cross-section area.
  3. Describe the one variable you will change and predict the effect.
  4. Solve both models with the on-page or approved external simulator.

Step B · Present evidence

  1. Save two readable model/result screenshots or document the full outputs in a labeled table.
  2. Compare center displacement, peak tension/compression and member length/volume.
  3. Show a hand-calculated support-reaction check.
  4. Write a 4–6 sentence recommendation citing two numerical differences and one limitation.
08 / Check your understanding

Answer first, then reveal the solution

Write a sentence or short calculation for each question in your notebook.

A · Reactions

A 400 mm truss carries a centered 120 N downward point load. What are its vertical reactions for ideal pin/roller supports?

Reveal answer

Each reaction is 60 N upward, obtained from moment equilibrium about either support and vertical-force balance.

B · Missing constraint

Why might a solver report a singular model if the right support does not prevent vertical motion?

Reveal answer

The structure may have a rigid-body degree of freedom or insufficient restraint, so the stiffness equations are not solvable.

C · Material stiffness

A truss remains geometrically unchanged, with the same load and cross-sections. What happens to its predicted displacement if E doubles?

Reveal answer

With linear-elastic behavior it becomes half as large. In this determinate model member axial forces are unchanged.

D · Units

The coordinates are entered in meters but E is entered in N/mm² without adjusting solver unit conventions. What is wrong?

Reveal answer

The model mixes unit systems and may predict severely incorrect stiffness/displacement. Convert all quantities to consistent units before solving.

E · Compression

The solver reports 80 N compression in a truss member. Does that tell you it cannot buckle?

Reveal answer

No. Buckling capacity depends on geometry, EI, end restraint and imperfections, so force demand alone cannot prove safety.

F · Fair test

A student changes both the load and material between Designs A and B. Can they attribute displacement changes only to truss geometry?

Reveal answer

No. Keep common loads, properties, member area, span and restraints; change a single design factor at a time.

G · Stiffness vs. material use

A taller truss bends less but has longer total members. What trade-off is involved?

Reveal answer

Lower predicted displacement can require greater member length and nominal volume; selection should balance multiple outcomes rather than one best number.

H · Model boundaries

A side-truss simulation has less than 10 mm center displacement. Does that prove the class bridge meets every design requirement?

Reveal answer

No. It excludes full 3D construction, width, mass, joints, buckling, deck loads and real-world testing. It is a preliminary analysis, not complete compliance evidence.

Student submission

Exactly what to submit

Complete the POE 2.10 Truss Design & Simulation comparison using the worksheet, notebook or approved PDF. The on-page simulation is acceptable as an educational model; the teacher may request external-tool confirmation.

Required evidence

  • Two labeled truss diagrams showing the changed geometry
  • Complete joint coordinate and member-connectivity data
  • Pin/roller support descriptions, 100 N applied load, and shared E/A values
  • Two simulation screenshots or a readable model-results table
  • Comparison of reactions, deflection, tensile/compressive forces and estimated material length/volume
  • At least one hand-calculated support reaction check
  • 4–6 sentence design recommendation supported by two quantitative comparisons, including a modeling limitation

Before turning it in

  • Units are consistent throughout.
  • Models have the intended connected joints and supports.
  • Both designs use the same loading and material properties.
  • Forces include correct tension/compression identification.
  • Simulation limits are explained; no unsupported claims about bridge safety.
  • All teacher-assigned additional questions are included.
