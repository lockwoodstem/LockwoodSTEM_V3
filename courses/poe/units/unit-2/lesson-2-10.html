Truss Design & Simulation

Engineering question: What can simulations tell us about a truss before it is built?

LearnPracticeApplySubmitUnit 2
Foundational instruction

Learn it before the bridge project

Use varied engineering examples to master the mechanics, then apply the technique to your final structural design later in the unit.

Learning Targets

  • Create a valid simplified truss model
  • Compare predicted member forces and deformation
  • Explain limits of idealized results

Learn the Concept

  • Use VEX/SSA or another approved truss-analysis tool
  • Define supports, nodes, geometry, member properties and applied joint loads deliberately
  • Simulations are models, not proof that a real structure is safe

During instruction: Sketch the forces, geometry, variables or joints discussed and record units for every quantity.

Practice Together

Build two simple truss configurations with the same span and loading. Predict which has larger deflection, then compare simulated outputs.

Show: a labeled diagram, a mathematical model when relevant, and a final statement interpreting the result.

Apply the Skill

Submit screenshots or a table of reactions, largest tension/compression member forces, and any deflection outputs.

Check your understanding: Why might a simulation disagree with a physical model that uses glue or flexible connections?

Student Evidence

Truss comparison and simulation evidence. Use your engineering notebook or teacher-provided guided notes; include dimensions, units and a conclusion.

Looking ahead: These mechanics techniques will be revisited when students analyze the bridge design in Lessons 2.15–2.20.
