Learn the Concept

What You Need to Know

Elastic deformation

Elastic deformation is recoverable when the load is removed.

Permanent deformation

Past the elastic range, a member can retain deformation even without fracturing.

Stress and strain

Stress describes force distributed over area; strain describes relative deformation.

Brittle vs. ductile

Brittle materials fracture with little permanent deformation; ductile materials can deform substantially first.

Instability

Slender compression members can buckle before the material itself crushes.

Testing

Nondestructive tests inspect without intentionally ruining a part; destructive tests reveal ultimate behavior by continuing to damage or failure.

Reading a test

If a bridge bends under 10 N and returns to its original position, behavior is mostly elastic. If it stays bent, slips at a joint, cracks, or buckles, permanent damage has occurred even if it can still carry load.

Self-Check

Can You Explain It?

1. If a member returns to shape after unloading, what occurred?

Primarily elastic deformation.

2. Why is buckling different from crushing?

Buckling is a geometry-driven instability that can happen before compressive material strength is reached.
