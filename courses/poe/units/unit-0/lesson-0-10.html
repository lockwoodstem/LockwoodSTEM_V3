CubeSat Lightweight Structure Competition

Focus question: Which engineering choices produced the lightest CubeSat structure that can still pass every qualification requirement?

CubeSat Project

Design for qualification first. Optimize mass everywhere you can justify it.

DefineDesignProve

The final winner is the lightest CubeSat structure that passes every required qualification gate.

Challenge Rules

CubeSat Lightweight Structure Challenge

Every team works inside the same architecture and material limits. Your engineering advantage comes from how efficiently you use them.

Competition Objective

Pass first. Then weigh in.

Structural qualification is pass/fail. Among the structures that pass every requirement, the lowest total assembled mass wins.

Fixed Architecture
  • Maximum assembled envelope: 4 in × 4 in × 4 in, including hardware.
  • Top and bottom: laser cut from the team’s single 4 in × 12 in × 1/8 in acrylic sheet.
  • Sides: remain open; no side panels.
  • Primary frame: 3D printed using the same teacher-selected filament material for every team.
  • Assembly: M3 screws for all primary structural connections; structural glue is not permitted.
Manufacturing Limits
  • Serviceability: the structure must be able to be disassembled and reassembled with normal hand tools.
  • Competition: the lightest structure that passes every qualification requirement wins.
Lesson Targets

What You Will Be Able to Do

Learning Target

I can complete a final qualification check, interpret competition data, and defend the engineering decisions that shaped my team’s structure.

Evidence

Final CubeSat + qualification record + official mass + short engineering design review

Success Looks Like

The structure passes every required check before weigh-in, and the team can connect its final mass and performance to specific design decisions.

Activity Flow

How Today Advances the CubeSat

1. Qualify the structure

Complete dimensional, construction, and standardized structural checks.

2. Official weigh-in

Only qualifying structures receive an official competition mass.

3. Defend the design

Connect final performance to geometry, material removal, print settings, and assembly decisions.

Qualification Sequence

Pass Every Gate Before the Scale

Qualification determines whether a structure is eligible. Mass ranks only the structures that qualify.

GateRequirementResult
1. EnvelopeEntire assembled structure fits within 4 in × 4 in × 4 in.PASS / FAIL
2. ConstructionLaser-cut 1/8 in acrylic top/base, open sides, 3D-printed frame, M3 structural assembly, no structural adhesive.PASS / FAIL
3. Structural LoadSupport a standardized 10 lb vertical load for 30 seconds using the teacher-provided load plate without fracture, joint separation, or visible permanent deformation.PASS / FAIL
4. Assembly IntegrityM3 joints remain secure and the structure remains serviceable after the load test.PASS / FAIL
5. Weigh-InQualifying structure is measured to the nearest 0.1 g.Official Mass
Winner

Lowest Qualified Mass

The lightest CubeSat structure that passes all four qualification gates wins.

Tie Breaker

Structural Efficiency

If official masses are within 1.0 g, lower measured deflection during the same load test wins. If still tied, greater usable interior volume wins.

Project Milestone

Today’s Required Deliverable

Final Competition Record & Design Review

  • Final assembled mass, including acrylic, printed frame, M3 hardware, inserts/nuts/washers, and all permanent components.
  • Pass/fail results for every qualification requirement.
  • Final print settings and estimated filament use.
  • One design decision that reduced mass.
  • One design decision that supported structural strength.
  • One change the team would make if a second version were allowed.

Document the Reasoning

Decision

Evidence

Reasoning

Why does that evidence make the decision appropriate for a lightweight structure that still has to qualify?
