POE Unit 0 • Lesson 0.12

Lunar Rock Payload Device: Build, Test, Improve, and Defend

Focus question: How can 30° tilt-test evidence guide an improvement to an open-top lunar payload device?

Test ProtocolYour WorkUnit 0 Overview
Build & Test

Build the approved design, test it in eight directions, improve one weakness, and defend the final result.

BuildTestImprove

A prototype is successful only when its performance is measured and explained.

Mission Requirements

Pass Criteria

10 RocksAll ten randomly placed simulated moon rocks must fit in the bay.
Completely Open TopNo cover, strap, net, flap, or obstruction may cross the opening.
30° in Any DirectionRetain all rocks in forward, backward, left, right, and four diagonal tilts.
Structural IntegrityThe device must remain intact and stable during testing.
Repeatable TestingUse the same loading and tilt procedure for comparable trials.
Documented RevisionMake and justify at least one evidence-based improvement.
Lesson Targets

What You Will Be Able to Do

Learning Target

I can safely build a prototype, collect repeatable tilt-test evidence, improve one weakness, and defend whether the design met the mission.

Evidence

A completed Lunar Rock Payload Test and Evaluation Record with initial data, revision evidence, final data, and an engineering conclusion.

Success Looks Like

The final device retains all 10 rocks at 30° in every required direction and the team supports its conclusion with recorded evidence.

Activity Flow

Today in Class

Start

  • Review the approved design and test procedure.
  • Confirm team roles, materials, and safety expectations.
  • Inspect the work area and complete the preflight readiness check.

Build / Analyze

  • Build the approved open-top payload device.
  • Document any changes from the original plan.
  • Randomly load all 10 rocks before each trial.
  • Run the complete eight-direction 30° tilt test.
  • Identify the weakest direction and one likely cause.
  • Make one justified design improvement and repeat the complete test.

Close

  • Compare initial and revised performance.
  • Complete the criteria-and-constraints evaluation and CER conclusion.
  • Return all rocks, clean the area, and submit team and individual evidence.
Testing

30° Tilt-Test Protocol

  1. Verify that the top is completely unobstructed and that the device is stable on the test platform.
  2. Randomly place all ten rocks into the payload bay without intentionally arranging or stacking them.
  3. Tilt the platform smoothly to 30°, hold briefly, and return it to level.
  4. Record the number of rocks retained, rocks lost, movement near the top edge, and any structural or stability issue.
  5. Repeat for all eight directions using the same procedure.
  6. After the first full test, revise one design feature and repeat all eight directions.

Engineering Defense

Claim, Evidence, and Reasoning

Claim

State whether the final payload device is ready for the lunar mission.

Evidence

Cite specific initial and revised tilt-test results, including the number of rocks retained in each direction.

Reasoning

Explain how the geometry or construction change caused the observed performance.
