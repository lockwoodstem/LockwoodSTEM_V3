Opening Prompt

How does Principles of Engineering connect mechanisms, energy, structures, controls, materials, data, and aerospace systems?

Think about systems, tools, safety habits, models, and evidence—not just finishing a task.

Quick class share: What evidence, tool, or system behavior would help answer today’s focus question?

Which Action Fits?

Which action best supports welcome to aerospace poe?

1Copy the first example without checking the system goal

2Use evidence and criteria to make a controlled decision

3Choose the easiest option because it is fastest

Carry-forward idea: In POE, strong choices are safe, testable, and connected to a system goal.
Key Idea

Claim → Evidence → Decision

Engineers connect what they believe to proof that others can inspect.

1

Claim

What you believe about the system, tool, model, or prototype.

→
2

Evidence

What proves, challenges, or clarifies the claim.

→
3

Decision

What you do next because of the evidence.

Evidence can include: sketches; measurements; screenshots; checklists; test data; observations; photos; reflections.
Mini Challenge

What Evidence Would You Need?

Select the strongest evidence before opening the reveal.

Claim: “My welcome to aerospace poe work is accurate and ready to move forward.”

AA labeled sketch, screenshot, diagram, or model view that shows what was created

BA statement that it looks good or seems finished

CRelevant measurements, checklist results, test data, or observations

DA file name that does not identify the lesson, unit, or task

EA short explanation connecting the evidence to the next decision

Reveal the best evidence pair

Best support: A + C

A shows what was created or observed. C shows whether it meets the requirement or supports the design decision. E is also useful because it explains what the evidence means.

Scenario Response

What Should the Engineer Do Next?

Choose the strongest next step and explain your reasoning.

A student notices the result does not match the expected system behavior or requirement.

AIgnore it and keep going because the task is almost finished

BRecord the issue, identify the likely cause, revise, and check again

CDelete the evidence and restart without notes

DChoose the faster option even if it cannot be justified

Reveal the strongest next step

Best answer: B

Record the issue, identify the likely cause, revise, and check again. The strongest next step turns the problem into useful engineering evidence instead of hiding it.
