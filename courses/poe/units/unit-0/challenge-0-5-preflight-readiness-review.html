POE Unit 0 | Mission Brief 0.5

Preflight Readiness Review

Create and conduct the most reliable preflight inspection for a classroom rover or mechanism.

Advanced1-2 class periodsCompetition
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

Two student teams are preparing identical systems for a mission demonstration. The winning team is not simply the fastest; it is the team whose checklist catches the most faults while remaining efficient.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Translate failure risks into inspection steps
  • Sequence checks efficiently
  • Use objective pass/fail criteria
  • Validate a checklist through fault insertion
Materials

What You Need

  • Classroom rover or mechanism
  • Fault cards or teacher-inserted issues
  • Checklist template
  • Timer
  • Engineering notebook
Procedure

Instructions

  1. List at least ten possible mission-preventing faults.
  2. Rank each fault by likelihood and consequence.
  3. Write a checklist with observable pass/fail criteria.
  4. Arrange checks from safe/no-power inspection through powered functional checks.
  5. Exchange systems with another team; your teacher inserts approved faults.
  6. Conduct the inspection while being timed.
  7. Record detected faults, missed faults, and unnecessary checks.
  8. Revise the checklist and repeat one validation round.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • At least 12 checklist items
  • Checklist must include identity, physical condition, power, movement, sensor/control, and final configuration
  • No item may use vague wording such as “looks good”
  • Score balances detection rate and completion time
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Only teacher-approved faults may be inserted
  • Do not defeat guards or create electrical hazards
  • Power must remain off during physical inspection unless instructed
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Risk-ranked fault list
  • Preflight checklist
  • Validation results
  • Revised checklist
  • Competition score summary
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which fault was easiest to miss?
  2. Which checklist item had the clearest pass/fail standard?
  3. What check could be automated?
  4. How did time pressure affect performance?
Download PDF
Engineering Tip

Write checklist items as actions with a condition: “Verify battery connector is fully seated and locked.”

Common Mistake

A long checklist is not automatically better. Repeated or low-value checks can hide critical items.

Stretch Goal

Convert the final checklist into a QR-linked digital form.

Career Connection

Aerospace operations teams use configuration-controlled checklists for aircraft, launch vehicles, and ground support equipment.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
