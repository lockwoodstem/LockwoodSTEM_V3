Lesson Goal: Build two VEX drive systems exactly as directed. First, build a gear reduction. Then, build a sprocket and chain reduction. The goal is to learn correct VEX construction habits before more open-ended mechanism challenges.

Build Rule: Do not force parts. If a shaft will not spin freely by hand, stop and fix the alignment before adding more parts.

Part A - Identify the Drive System Parts

TermMeaningSketch / Example
DriverThe gear or sprocket that receives input motion.
DrivenThe gear or sprocket that receives motion from the driver.
ShaftThe axle the gear or sprocket spins on.
Bearing flatSupports the shaft and reduces rubbing against the structure.
Shaft collarKeeps the shaft from sliding side to side.
Spacer / washerCreates clearance so parts do not rub.
ChainTransfers motion between sprockets separated by distance.

Part B - Guided Gear Reduction Build

Target System: Build a 12-tooth driver gear meshed with a 36-tooth driven gear. This creates a 3:1 gear reduction.

Input shaft [12T gear] ---- 4 VEX holes ---- Output shaft [36T gear]

#Build StepWhat to CheckDone
1Place two rails parallel to each other. Keep the holes lined up.Rails are square and do not twist.□
2Attach bearing flats at the input shaft location on both rails.Both bearings line up across the frame.□
3Slide the input shaft through both bearing flats.The shaft spins freely.□
4Add the 12T gear to the input shaft.Gear teeth face the center of the mechanism.□
5Add spacers if needed so the gear does not rub the rails.Gear has clearance on both sides.□
6Add shaft collars on the outside of the rails.Shaft cannot slide side to side.□
7Count 4 VEX holes from the input shaft and mark the output shaft location.The 12T and 36T gears will mesh.□
8Attach bearing flats at the output shaft location on both rails.Bearings are straight and aligned.□
9Slide the output shaft through the bearing flats.The shaft spins freely before the gear is added.□
10Add the 36T gear to the output shaft.The 36T gear meshes with the 12T gear.□
11Add spacers and collars to the output shaft.The gear does not rub and the shaft does not slide.□
12Turn the input shaft by hand for five full turns.Both gears rotate smoothly without skipping teeth.□

Part D - Guided Idler Gear Build

Why Add an Idler? An idler gear can change direction or move motion across distance. In a simple gear train, the idler does not change the overall driver-to-driven ratio.

Input [12T] ---- 4 holes ---- Idler [36T] ---- 6 holes ---- Output [36T]

#Build StepWhat to CheckDone
1Build or extend a rail frame with space for three shaft positions.The frame is rigid and straight.□
2Install the 12T driver gear on the first shaft.Driver gear spins freely.□
3Count 4 VEX holes and install the 36T idler gear on the second shaft.Driver and idler mesh smoothly.□
4Count 6 VEX holes from the idler and install the 36T output gear on the third shaft.Idler and output gear mesh smoothly.□
5Add collars and spacers to all shafts.No gears rub on the structure.□
6Turn the input shaft by hand.All gears move without binding.□
7Track the direction of each gear with arrows.Output direction is identified correctly.□

Part E - Guided Sprocket and Chain Drive Build

Target System: Build a 12-tooth driver sprocket connected by chain to a 24-tooth driven sprocket. This creates a 2:1 reduction. Unlike external gears, both sprockets rotate the same direction.

Input shaft [12T sprocket] ==== chain ==== Output shaft [24T sprocket]

#Build StepWhat to CheckDone
1Use a rigid frame with two shaft locations separated by enough space for a chain loop.Shafts are parallel.□
2Install bearing flats at the input shaft location.Bearings line up across the frame.□
3Add the input shaft with the 12T sprocket.Sprocket is centered and does not rub.□
4Install bearing flats at the output shaft location.Output shaft is parallel to the input shaft.□
5Add the output shaft with the 24T sprocket.Sprocket teeth line up with the 12T sprocket.□
6Add collars and spacers to both shafts.Shafts cannot slide sideways.□
7Wrap chain around both sprockets to estimate length.Chain makes a complete loop.□
8Add or remove links until the chain has slight slack.Chain is not tight and not sagging heavily.□
9Connect the chain and slowly turn the input shaft by hand.Chain stays on the sprockets.□
10Adjust sprocket alignment if the chain wants to climb off the teeth.Sprocket faces are in the same plane.□
11Rotate the input shaft five full turns.Output turns smoothly in the same direction.□

Chain Drive Check

CheckItemNotes

□The chain is not too tight.

□The output sprocket turns the same direction as the input.

Troubleshooting Guide

ProblemLikely CauseFirst Fix
Shaft will not rotateCollars too tight or bearings misalignedLoosen collars, square the frame, retest by hand
Gears skip teethShaft spacing is wrongRecount holes and adjust bearing locations
Gear rubs on railMissing spacer or gear too close to frameAdd spacer / washer
Shaft slides sidewaysMissing or loose shaft collarAdd or tighten collars
Chain falls offSprockets not alignedMove sprockets into the same plane
Chain is hard to turnChain too tightAdd a link or move shafts slightly closer
Chain sags heavilyChain too looseRemove a link or move shafts slightly apart
