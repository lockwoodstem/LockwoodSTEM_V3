3D Printing

Study Guide

Prepare to use classroom 3D printers safely and responsibly from file setup through cleanup.

Go to Practice Check Certification Home

1. Safety mindset

  • Use printers only after Engineering Safety and 3D Printing certification requirements are complete.
  • Do not reach into a printer while parts are moving or while the nozzle/bed may be hot.
  • Stop and ask for help if the printer makes unusual sounds, creates a filament blob, shifts layers, or shows an error.
  • Keep food, drinks, loose papers, and backpacks away from printer stations.
  • Never modify printer hardware, firmware, filament path, or settings without teacher approval.

2. Printer awareness

  • Bambu P1S: enclosed printer used for reliable supervised production prints.
  • Bambu A1 Mini: open-frame printer used for smaller supervised prints and quick prototypes.
  • Robo E3/E4: classroom printers used for PLA prototyping and student-ready print workflows.
  • Each printer may use a different software/profile workflow, so always confirm the correct printer before slicing or submitting.
  • Do not assume settings transfer perfectly between printer models.

3. Filament and materials

  • Use only teacher-approved filament for the assigned printer.
  • PLA is the default student material unless another material is specifically approved.
  • Keep spools clean, dry, untangled, and properly supported.
  • Do not cut, unload, or change filament unless you have been trained and given permission.
  • Report grinding, tangles, snapping filament, failed loading, or filament running out.

4. File preparation

  • Use approved STL, 3MF, or teacher-provided model files.
  • Check that the model is the correct size and units before printing.
  • Orient the part to reduce supports, improve bed contact, and strengthen the print for its purpose.
  • Use supports only when needed for overhangs or unsupported features.
  • Check estimated time and material before submitting; redesign if the print is too large or inefficient.

5. Slicing choices

  • Select the correct printer profile, nozzle/profile settings, filament profile, and build plate option.
  • Choose layer height based on speed versus detail.
  • Choose infill based on part function; more infill is not automatically better.
  • Use brims, rafts, or supports only when the model needs them.
  • Preview the sliced model before printing to catch support, orientation, and first-layer problems.

6. Starting and monitoring

  • Confirm the bed/plate is clear and properly installed before starting.
  • Confirm the correct filament is loaded and there is enough material for the job.
  • Watch the first layer whenever possible; first-layer problems often predict print failure.
  • Stop or ask for help if the print detaches, curls, blobs around the nozzle, or begins printing spaghetti.
  • Do not leave a print area messy or block access to printers.

7. Removing prints

  • Wait for the bed/plate and part to cool when required.
  • Use approved removal methods only; do not pry aggressively or damage build surfaces.
  • Watch for sharp edges, hot surfaces, and brittle supports.
  • Remove supports carefully and clean up support material.
  • Report bed damage, nozzle blobs, poor adhesion, or failed prints.

8. Cleanup and documentation

  • Remove scraps, purge lines, support material, and failed prints from the station.
  • Return tools and leave the printer area ready for the next student.
  • Record print settings, failures, changes, and results in your engineering notebook when required.
  • Use print failures as design data, not as a reason to keep reprinting without changes.
  • Communicate printer issues so the next user does not inherit a problem.

Hands-on performance checklist

To earn the badge, you must demonstrate an approved workflow from file preparation to cleanup. The teacher marks this portion complete only after the student shows safe, independent readiness.

How to complete this training

Follow the modules in order. At each Try it prompt, explain your choice to a partner or record it in your engineering notebook. You are preparing to make decisions independently—not memorize button locations.

  1. Identify hazards and parts of the printer.
  2. Prepare and export a correctly sized model from Fusion.
  3. Slice and inspect an efficient, printable part.
  4. Start, observe and evaluate the first layer with teacher supervision.
  5. Remove, inspect, document and clean up safely.
  6. Pass the online test and demonstrate the teacher-observed checkout.

Safety boundary: The certification badge does not authorize unattended printing, disassembly, nozzle changes or operation outside teacher-established classroom procedures.

Module 1 · Know your printer

Fused-filament fabrication (FFF/FDM) heats thermoplastic filament and lays down successive paths to form a part.

Hot end and nozzle
Heats and deposits filament; burn hazard even after motion stops.
Build plate
Provides the first-layer surface; may also be hot.
Extruder and filament path
Feeds material; jams and tangles require teacher assistance.
X/Y/Z motion
Positions the nozzle or bed; moving parts present pinch hazards.

Try it: On the printer assigned by your teacher, point out the nozzle, build plate, filament spool, emergency stop/cancel control and the moving axes without touching moving or hot areas.

Module 2 · Prepare a part in Fusion

  1. Inspect the design: closed solid, correct geometry, adequate wall thickness, no unintended bodies.
  2. Check the dimensions and units (millimeters).
  3. Export an approved mesh (STL or 3MF as your workflow requires).
  4. Import the model into the slicer and check the displayed dimensions again.

Try it: A 40 mm part appears as 40 inches in the slicer. What should you correct before printing? Never compensate for an unknown unit problem by guessing a scale factor.

Module 3 · Choose slicer settings for a purpose

  • Orientation: choose stable bed contact, accessible surfaces, appropriate strength direction and fewer unsupported features.
  • Layer height: thinner layers typically improve vertical detail but take longer.
  • Walls/perimeters: influence outer-surface durability and strength of thin features.
  • Infill: controls internal structure; higher infill adds time and mass and may not fix a weak geometry.
  • Supports: support difficult overhangs but add waste and removal effort.
  • Brim: may improve bed adhesion for narrow contact areas.

Try it: Compare two slice previews of the same bracket with different orientations. Predict which needs more support and which offers a larger first-layer contact area.

Important: Course projects may impose stricter settings. For example, the POE bridge challenge has separate limits for printed connector components; always follow the assignment specification in addition to printer safety rules.

Module 4 · Read the slice preview

  1. Confirm printer, nozzle, build plate and filament profiles.
  2. Review the first layer: is enough of the part touching the bed?
  3. Inspect unsupported overhangs and support placement.
  4. Look for gaps, very thin features and unintentional islands.
  5. Confirm estimated filament usage and print duration; request teacher approval before sending the job.

Try it: If a tiny peg is missing in the preview, should you increase infill or return to the model/settings to determine why that feature cannot be sliced?

Module 5 · First-layer decision point

A good first layer is attached to the plate, follows the expected path, and does not build up around the nozzle. A detached corner, dragging filament, or a growing blob is a reason to stop or request immediate teacher assistance.

  • Observe the first layer using the approved viewing method.
  • Never touch the print, nozzle, bed or moving mechanism while printing.
  • If you see unusual smoke, strong odors, heat damage or equipment faults, stop using the printer and alert the teacher; follow school evacuation/fire procedures where appropriate.

Try it: Explain what you would do if the print comes loose and the nozzle begins moving loose filament around the bed.

Module 6 · Diagnose, do not blindly reprint

ObservationPossible cause to investigate
Part releases from plateDirty plate, poor first-layer contact, unsuitable orientation
Stringing between featuresMaterial moisture, temperature or retraction settings
Missing lines / gapsFeed problem, partial clog or inappropriate settings
Layers shift sidewaysPart collision or motion-system issue
Weak or broken overhangUnsupported geometry, inadequate support or orientation

Try it: Choose one failed print, record the observable defect, suggest a plausible cause and state one change you would test with approval. Do not perform printer repair yourself.

Teacher-observed practical checkout · Required

After the online test, demonstrate these skills on the assigned printer with a teacher present. All steps must be demonstrated safely; a missed safety-critical step requires retraining and another check.

  1. Safety: identify hot and moving parts, site rules and when to stop/report a problem.
  2. Prepare: open a teacher-approved model; verify units, size and geometry.
  3. Slice: select the correct machine, nozzle, plate and filament; explain orientation, infill, support and wall choices.
  4. Inspect: show first-layer/toolpath preview, print-time estimate and filament estimate.
  5. Start: confirm the machine is ready and obtain approval before beginning.
  6. Monitor: identify acceptable first-layer behavior and explain when to cancel or request help.
  7. Finish: allow cooling, remove the part using approved tools, inspect it, clean the area and record a useful observation.

Teacher decision: pass only when the student can explain decisions and carry out the workflow safely, not simply repeat steps from a classmate. Teacher approval remains recorded through the existing dashboard.
