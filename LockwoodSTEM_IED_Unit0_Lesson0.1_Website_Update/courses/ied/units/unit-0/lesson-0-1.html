Opening Prompt

What do engineers do?

Think first: What actions, habits, or decisions make someone an engineer?

Quick class share: Raise your hand and share one short answer. Try to use an action word such as design, build, test, fix, invent, solve, measure, or improve.

Reveal the working definition

Engineers use knowledge, tools, and creativity to solve problems under real constraints.

1

Define

Clarify the need, user, criteria, and constraints.

2

Generate

Create multiple possible solutions before choosing one.

3

Test

Collect evidence from models, prototypes, data, and feedback.

4

Communicate

Document decisions so others can understand and improve the work.

Interactive Vote

Engineer actions: Which one fits?

Prompt: Which action sounds most like engineering work?

1
Copying exact instructions to make the same model as everyone else
2
Choosing the first idea because it seems easiest to build
3
Testing two versions and comparing data before deciding
4
Making a design look impressive without checking if it works

Vote first, then justify your choice.

Reveal the strongest engineering choice

Best answer: 3 — Testing two versions and comparing data before deciding.

It uses comparison, data, and a decision based on evidence. The other choices focus on copying, ease, or appearance rather than proof.

Carry-forward idea: In IED, an answer becomes stronger when it connects to evidence.

Aerospace Connection Map

Choose one system: rocket, drone, satellite, rover, or aircraft. Then identify one design choice an engineer would need to make.

Sketch
What features or shapes might matter?
Model
What could be represented in CAD or with a prototype?
Measure
What dimensions, forces, distances, or data might matter?
Test
What would prove the design works?

Share-out target: one system + one design decision + one kind of evidence.

Mindset Shift

Assignment vs. engineering problem

The goal is not just to finish. The goal is to make a design claim you can defend.

Typical assignment — completion mindset
  • Often has one expected answer
  • Can be completed and put away
  • Usually judged by correct or incorrect
  • Mistakes are avoided
Engineering problem — evidence mindset
  • Has multiple possible solutions
  • Must satisfy criteria and constraints
  • Uses testing to compare designs
  • Mistakes become evidence for improvement

Key Idea

Evidence-based engineering

Engineers connect what they claim to proof that others can inspect.

Claim

What you believe about the design.

→

Evidence

What proves or challenges the claim.

→

Decision

What you do next because of the evidence.

Evidence can include: sketches, measurements, test data, observations, photos, prototypes, and user feedback.

Mini Challenge

What evidence would you need?

Claim: “Our launch pad design supports the rocket safely and is ready for testing.”

Vote for the two strongest pieces of evidence.

A
A labeled sketch showing where the rocket contacts the support
B
A photo that makes the prototype look finished
C
A stability test showing the pad does not tip with the rocket placed on it
D
A teammate saying the design is probably strong enough
E
A list of the materials used to build the base and support
Reveal the best evidence pair

Best support: A + C.

A shows design intent and where support occurs. C provides direct performance evidence against the safety and stability claim.

Photos, material lists, and teammate opinions may support the story, but they do not prove the claim by themselves.

Scenario Response

What should the engineer do next?

A team builds a small rocket support. During the first test, it stands up, but the rocket leans to one side.

A
Keep it because the prototype did not fall over.
B
Add decorations so the design looks more complete.
C
Record the lean, identify where the support is uneven, revise, and retest.
D
Start over without writing down what happened.
Reveal the strongest next step

Best answer: C. Record the lean, identify where the support is uneven, revise, and retest.

Record

Write down what happened during the test.

Identify

Find the likely cause of the lean.

Revise + Retest

Change the design and collect new evidence.

Quick Check

Evidence or opinion?

Vote first, then open each reveal.

A. “Our design is the best because it looks strong.”

Opinion-heavy. The statement uses a vague judgment—“looks strong”—without test evidence.

B. “Version 2 traveled 12 ft farther than Version 1 in the official test.”

Evidence-based. It gives a measurable result that can be checked.

C. “We changed the wing fold because the first test curved left three times.”

Evidence-based reasoning. The design change is connected to repeated observations.

Takeaway: Claim + specific evidence = stronger engineering communication.
