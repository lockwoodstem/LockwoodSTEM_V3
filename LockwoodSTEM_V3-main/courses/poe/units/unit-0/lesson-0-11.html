POE Unit 0 • Lesson 0.11

Lunar Rock Payload Device: Plan and Design

Focus question: How can geometry and careful planning keep loose lunar samples secure in an open-top payload bay?

Mission RequirementsYour WorkUnit 0 Overview
Design Challenge

Design an open-top payload device that carries ten simulated moon rocks without losing any during a 30° tilt.

MeasureDesignPlan

Today you will prepare a build-ready design and test plan.

Mission Briefing

Lunar Rock Payload Challenge

A lunar surface vehicle needs a payload bay that remains accessible while protecting randomly loaded samples on uneven terrain.

Core requirement: The device must hold 10 randomly placed 3D-printed moon rocks, remain completely open on top, and retain all rocks when tilted 30° in any direction.
CapacityHold all 10 simulated moon rocks without intentional stacking.
Open TopNo lid, net, strap, flap, mesh, or partial cover may cross the top opening.
Tilt PerformanceRetain every rock at 30° forward, backward, left, right, and all four diagonals.
Random LoadingRocks are placed into the bay without arranging them to favor the design.
ConstructionUse only the approved prototyping materials and stay within teacher-set size limits.
DocumentationSubmit a complete plan and receive teacher approval before building.
Lesson Targets

What You Will Be Able to Do

Learning Target

I can turn mission requirements into a buildable payload design and a repeatable 30° tilt-test plan.

Evidence

A complete Lunar Rock Payload Design Plan with concepts, selection evidence, dimensions, materials, build steps, and test procedures.

Success Looks Like

Another team could build and test the selected concept using only the submitted plan.

Activity Flow

Today in Class

Start

  • Review the lunar mission and identify the user need.
  • Inspect and measure the ten simulated moon rocks.
  • Separate the mission information into criteria, constraints, and required evidence.

Build / Analyze

  • Generate at least two open-top payload concepts.
  • Consider wall height, sloped walls, interior baffles, recessed floors, corners, and center-of-mass placement.
  • Compare concepts with a decision matrix.
  • Create a labeled, dimensioned final sketch.
  • Develop a materials list, numbered build sequence, and eight-direction tilt-test procedure.

Close

  • Complete the teacher design-readiness review.
  • Submit the approved design plan.
  • Confirm team roles and identify the most likely failure point.
Prototype Kit

Approved Materials

Your teacher may adjust quantities to create equal team kits.

Structure

  • Corrugated cardboard
  • Chipboard or cardstock
  • Foam board or craft foam
  • Craft sticks and coffee stirrers
  • Drinking straws and index cards

Connections

  • Masking or painter’s tape
  • Rubber bands
  • Binder clips and paper clips
  • Brass fasteners
  • String or thin cord
  • Teacher-approved hot glue

Tools

  • Ruler and protractor
  • Pencil and fine-tip marker
  • Scissors
  • Graph paper
  • Digital calipers, optional
  • Cutting mat and teacher-controlled cutting tools

Open-Top Rule

Materials may shape the walls or interior of the payload bay, but nothing may span, cover, close, or restrict the top opening.

Required Deliverable

Lunar Rock Payload Design Plan

  • Mission need statement
  • Criteria and constraints
  • Moon-rock measurements and observations
  • Two labeled concept sketches
  • Decision matrix
  • Selected concept and justification
  • Dimensioned final sketch
  • Approved materials list
  • Numbered build sequence
  • Eight-direction 30° tilt-test procedure
  • Predicted failure points
  • Team roles
  • Teacher approval
Readiness Review

Approval Checklist

The concept holds all 10 rocks.
The top is completely open.
The design fits within the size limit.
The sketch includes usable dimensions.
The materials and steps are realistic.
The test covers all eight directions.
Success and failure are clearly defined.
Every team member has a role.
Exit Ticket

Mission Debrief

1

Which geometric feature should do the most to keep the rocks contained?

2

Which tilt direction is most likely to cause failure, and why?

3

What must your team verify before construction begins?
