POE Unit 4 | Mission Brief 4.4

Landing-Zone Probability Model

Use repeated test data to estimate the probability that a payload lands inside a target zone.

Advanced2 class periodsAerospace Mission
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

A relief drone drops small emergency packages. The team must determine whether the current deployment method is reliable enough for a defined landing zone.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Use descriptive statistics
  • Model target success probability
  • Visualize two-dimensional test data
  • Communicate risk and limitations
Materials

What You Need

  • Approved drop or launch simulation
  • Target grid
  • At least 20 trial coordinates
  • Spreadsheet
  • Engineering notebook
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Define the target-zone boundaries and coordinate origin.
  2. Collect or analyze at least 20 landing coordinates.
  3. Plot every point on a scaled scatter plot.
  4. Calculate mean x and y position and spread in each direction.
  5. Count successful landings and calculate observed success rate.
  6. Identify directional bias and possible environmental causes.
  7. Recommend an aim-point adjustment or process change.
  8. Predict the expected number of successes in 50 future drops.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • At least 20 trials
  • Target success defined before data collection
  • All points shown, including misses
  • Include observed probability and directional bias
  • Recommendation must acknowledge sample-size limits
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Use a safe simulation or approved low-energy system
  • Maintain a clear test zone
  • Do not alter data after seeing target results
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Coordinate data
  • Scatter plot
  • Success-rate calculation
  • Bias analysis
  • Aim-point recommendation
  • Future-performance prediction
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Did the mean landing point fall inside the target?
  2. Can a high average accuracy still produce poor reliability?
  3. How would wind change the distribution?
  4. What sample size would increase confidence?
Download PDF
Engineering Tip

Keep both coordinate directions; reducing the result to distance alone can hide a directional bias.

Common Mistake

Assuming the observed percentage is an exact future probability ignores limited sample size.

Stretch Goal

Create an elliptical landing zone that contains approximately 80% of the observed points.

Career Connection

Guidance, navigation, and test engineers quantify dispersion and probability for payload delivery, landing, and release systems.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
