Wing-Spar Material Trade Study

Compare candidate materials for a model wing spar and recommend the best option for the mission.

Intermediate1-2 class periodsAerospace Mission
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

A small unmanned aircraft needs a wing spar that is stiff, light, affordable, and manufacturable with available tools. No single material is best in every category.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Compare material properties
  • Create weighted decision criteria
  • Conduct simple bending tests
  • Defend a materials recommendation
Materials

What You Need

  • Samples of wood, plastic, aluminum, composite substitute, or approved materials
  • Mass scale
  • Ruler
  • Test fixture
  • Small loads
  • Decision matrix
Procedure

Instructions

  1. Define mission priorities for mass, stiffness, cost, availability, and manufacturability.
  2. Measure sample dimensions and mass.
  3. Perform a consistent bending test on each material using the same span and load.
  4. Record deflection and any permanent damage.
  5. Normalize or rank each material for the five criteria.
  6. Create a weighted decision matrix.
  7. Recommend one material and identify the biggest risk in that choice.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • At least four candidate materials
  • Same span, support spacing, and test load for every sample
  • Decision matrix must include at least five criteria
  • Recommendation must cite both test data and mission priorities
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Stay below teacher-approved load limits
  • Wear eye protection during bending tests
  • Do not test brittle materials near faces
  • Handle sharp metal edges carefully
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Material property table
  • Bending-test data
  • Weighted decision matrix
  • Mission recommendation
  • Risk statement
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which criterion most strongly affected the result?
  2. Did the stiffest material have the best overall score?
  3. How would production quantity change the decision?
  4. What missing property would you test next?
Download PDF
Engineering Tip

Compare materials using equivalent geometry whenever possible; geometry can dominate stiffness.

Common Mistake

A decision matrix is not objective if weights are chosen after seeing the test results.

Stretch Goal

Repeat the trade study for a different mission that prioritizes cost or impact resistance.

Career Connection

Materials and structures engineers select alloys, polymers, composites, and sandwich structures based on mission-specific tradeoffs.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
