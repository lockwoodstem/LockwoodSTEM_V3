POE Unit 2 | Mission Brief 2.1

Lightweight Rover Chassis Truss

Design and test a lightweight truss chassis that supports a specified payload.

Intermediate2-3 class periodsEngineering Challenge
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Engineering Design Brief

Work independently or with an approved partner.

Read the complete brief before gathering materials or beginning construction.

Background

Engineering Context

A planetary rover chassis must protect instruments while minimizing launch mass. Your team will create a small truss structure and compare strength-to-mass performance.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Identify tension and compression members
  • Use triangulation effectively
  • Measure structural performance
  • Calculate strength-to-mass ratio
Materials

What You Need

  • Craft sticks, paper strips, or approved structural material
  • Adhesive
  • Mass scale
  • Test weights
  • Ruler
  • Engineering notebook
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Define the required chassis span and payload location.
  2. Sketch two truss patterns and label expected tension/compression members.
  3. Estimate material quantity and mass for each concept.
  4. Select and build one chassis using consistent joints.
  5. Measure final mass and inspect alignment.
  6. Load the chassis in approved increments while observing deformation.
  7. Record maximum successful load and failure location.
  8. Calculate load-to-mass ratio and recommend one redesign.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • Span the required distance without intermediate support
  • Support the assigned base payload for 30 seconds
  • Use a clearly identifiable truss pattern
  • Record mass before testing
  • No repair during an official load trial
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Use only approved adhesives and cutting tools
  • Wear eye protection during destructive testing if required
  • Keep hands and faces away from the load path
  • Do not exceed instructor load limits
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Two concept sketches
  • Member-force predictions
  • Structure mass
  • Load-test table
  • Failure analysis
  • Redesign recommendation
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which members appeared to be in compression?
  2. Where did the first visible deformation occur?
  3. How did joint quality affect performance?
  4. What change would improve strength without adding much mass?
Download PDF
Engineering Tip

Compression members often fail by buckling before the material itself crushes.

Common Mistake

Adding material everywhere increases mass but may not strengthen the critical load path.

Stretch Goal

Rebuild one member or joint and demonstrate a 15% improvement in load-to-mass ratio.

Career Connection

Aerospace structures engineers design trusses, frames, and support structures to carry loads with minimal mass.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
