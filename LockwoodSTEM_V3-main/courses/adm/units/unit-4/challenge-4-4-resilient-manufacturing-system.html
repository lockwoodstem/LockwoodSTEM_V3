ADM Unit 4 | Mission Brief 4.4

Resilient Manufacturing System

Redesign a production process so critical orders continue after one machine or supplier failure.

Advanced2-3 class periodsEngineering Challenge
Back to Challenge HubDownload Challenge Brief (PDF)
Production Specifications

Orion Aerospace Systems

Department: Business Continuity
Goal: Continue priority production after a critical failure.

Background

Engineering Context

A single machine currently performs a critical operation for an aerospace component. A recent outage stopped the entire line. Your team must create a resilience plan using alternate routing, inventory, or recovery strategies.

Objectives

What You Will Practice

  • Map dependencies in a production system
  • Analyze single points of failure
  • Develop contingency and recovery strategies
  • Evaluate resilience tradeoffs
Equipment

What You Need

  • Current process map
  • Machine and supplier capacity data
  • Failure scenario cards
  • Risk matrix
  • Spreadsheet or planning board
Procedure

Student Instructions

  1. Create a current-state process map from raw material to shipment.
  2. Mark every machine, supplier, data system, and inventory dependency.
  3. Identify at least three single points of failure.
  4. Draw one failure scenario and calculate lost production if no action is taken.
  5. Develop three resilience options, such as alternate machine, safety stock, cross-training, or backup supplier.
  6. Estimate cost, recovery time, and production impact for each option.
  7. Use a weighted decision matrix to select a strategy.
  8. Create a response playbook with detection, containment, alternate operation, and recovery steps.
  9. Test the playbook using a second surprise failure scenario.
Requirements

Engineering Requirements

  • Address at least three failure points
  • Maintain priority production in the selected scenario
  • Include detection and communication steps
  • Quantify recovery time and production impact
Constraints

Constraints & Safety

  • Use provided capacity and cost data
  • Do not assume unlimited backup capacity
  • Safety and quality requirements cannot be waived
  • Document any unmet demand
Deliverables

What You Submit

  • Dependency/process map
  • Failure-impact calculations
  • Three resilience concepts
  • Decision matrix
  • Response playbook
  • Reflection
Reflection

Reflection Questions

  1. Which dependency was most critical?
  2. Why is maximum inventory not always the best solution?
  3. How did recovery time affect your choice?
  4. What information should be monitored in real time?

Engineering Tip

Resilience usually combines prevention, redundancy, flexible capacity, and a practiced response plan.

Common Mistake

A backup resource is not useful if it has never been qualified or cannot meet the required capacity.

Stretch Goal

Include a simultaneous cyber outage and supplier delay in the tabletop test.

Career Connection

Supply chain and business continuity engineers prepare factories for equipment, supplier, weather, and cyber disruptions.

Before You Submit
  • Notebook evidence included
  • Requirements checked
  • Testing documented
  • Reflection completed
