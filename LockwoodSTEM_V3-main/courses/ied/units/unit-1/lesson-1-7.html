Measurement, Precision & Tolerances

Focus question: How accurate does a measurement need to be, and how much manufacturing variation can a design allow?

What You Are Learning

Learning Targets

  • Select an appropriate measurement tool for the feature being measured.
  • Record measurements using consistent units and realistic precision.
  • Explain nominal size, limits, tolerance, clearance, and interference qualitatively.
  • Identify rocket interfaces where dimensional variation matters.

Class Flow

Work Through the Lesson

Day 1 — Measurement & Precision
  • Compare rulers and calipers for different measurement tasks.
  • Practice reading and recording dimensions with consistent precision.
  • Measure assigned rocket components and record units with every value.
  • Repeat selected measurements to check consistency.
Day 2 — Tolerance & Fit
  • Use measured examples to distinguish nominal size from allowable variation.
  • Analyze clearance and interference at component interfaces.
  • Identify dimensions that can vary more and dimensions that are function-critical.
  • Add tolerance/fit notes to the rocket measurement record.
Core Ideas

What to Pay Attention To

Precision

Record only the level of detail the measurement tool can reasonably support.

Tolerance

The allowable range of variation around a target dimension.

Fit

The dimensional relationship between mating parts can create clearance, transition, or interference behavior.

Engineering Check

Stop and verify before moving on

More decimal places do not automatically mean a better measurement. The recorded precision should match the tool and the engineering need.

Your Task

Measure and Evaluate Rocket Components

Build a measurement record that will support later documentation and redesign decisions. Focus on dimensions that affect assembly, alignment, or fit.

  1. Choose the correct tool before measuring.
  2. Record each value with units and consistent precision.
  3. Repeat critical measurements to check consistency.
  4. Identify at least two mating interfaces and describe the required fit.
  5. Flag dimensions that appear especially important to function or assembly.

Use evidence: Keep sketches, measurements, observations, and revisions visible. Unit 1 is building one connected engineering documentation package.

What You Will Submit

Rocket Measurement & Tolerance Record

  • Measured dimensions for assigned components.
  • Correct units and appropriate precision.
  • At least two interface/fit observations.
  • Identification of critical dimensions with a brief justification.

Before submitting: Check that your work is labeled, readable, complete, and consistent with the technical conventions used in this lesson.
