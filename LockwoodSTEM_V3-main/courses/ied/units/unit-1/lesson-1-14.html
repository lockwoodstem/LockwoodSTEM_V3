Tolerances

Focus question: Why do engineers allow some variation in measurements, but not too much?

Mission Focus

Use allowable variation to decide whether measured parts are acceptable for assembly.

Mission Briefing

Today’s Plan

Use this page with the lesson presentation and your engineering notebook.

Learning Targets

  • Distinguish nominal size from an acceptable size range.
  • Calculate upper and lower limits from a stated tolerance.
  • Classify measured samples and explain a possible functional consequence.

Success Looks Like

The inspection decision follows calculated limits rather than visual judgment.

Opening Prompt

Why can two parts have slightly different measurements and still both be acceptable?

Think first, then be ready to share a short response that connects the question to engineering communication.

Reveal: why this matters

Manufacturing cannot produce every feature at one exact value. A tolerance defines the range that still allows the part to perform its intended function.

Core Skill

What We Are Practicing

Key Idea 1

Nominal size is the target value for a feature.

Key Idea 2

Tolerance defines the allowed variation around the target.

Key Idea 3

A measurement is acceptable only when it falls within the calculated limits.

Your Task

Determine Whether Parts Meet Tolerance

Use stated nominal sizes and tolerances to evaluate sample measurements.

  1. Calculate the upper and lower acceptable limits for each assigned feature.
  2. Compare each sample with the limits and classify it as acceptable, undersized, or oversized.
  3. Explain one possible assembly consequence of an out-of-range sample.
  4. Recommend a reasonable tolerance for one additional feature and justify the choice.

Quality check: Every classification must be supported by shown calculations and units.

What You Will Submit

Tolerance and Inspection Analysis

Submit one completed analysis and one short engineering recommendation.

  • One table showing limit calculations and pass, undersized, or oversized decisions for the samples.
  • One paragraph explaining a functional consequence and justifying a tolerance for one additional feature.

Submission standard: Include units and plus/minus notation; a nominal value alone is not an inspection limit.
