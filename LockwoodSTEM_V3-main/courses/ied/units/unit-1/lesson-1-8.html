Joining Methods & Assembly Systems

Focus question: How do joining methods and component interfaces allow separate rocket parts to function as one assembly?

Mission Briefing

What You Are Learning

Use this webpage, your engineering notebook, and the listed lesson resources to complete the work in order.

Learning Targets

  • Identify mechanical fasteners, adhesives, friction/press fits, tabs, slots, and other joining methods.
  • Distinguish removable and permanent joints.
  • Explain why a joining method may be appropriate for a specific interface.
  • Organize rocket parts as components, subassemblies, and systems.

Success Looks Like

Analyze how parts connect, why a particular joining method was used, and how individual components form subassemblies and systems.

Class Flow

Work Through the Lesson

Investigation — Connections & Systems
  • Inspect each documented interface in the rocket assembly.
  • Identify the joining method and classify it as removable or permanent.
  • Record the function of the joint and what it must resist or allow.
  • Group related components into subassemblies and larger systems.
  • Create a simple system map showing important relationships.
Core Ideas

What to Pay Attention To

Joint choice

Engineers consider strength, serviceability, cost, alignment, manufacturing, and permanence when selecting a joint.

Component

A single part with a specific function within the product.

System

Multiple components or subassemblies that interact to accomplish a larger function.

Engineering Check

Stop and verify before moving on

Do not list a fastener without explaining what it connects and why that connection matters. The engineering information is in the relationship between parts.

Your Task

Map Rocket Interfaces and Systems

Use the disassembled rocket to connect the physical hardware to its engineering purpose. Your documentation should show how individual joints support the larger assembly.

  1. Identify each important joint or interface.
  2. Record what components are connected and the joining method used.
  3. Explain whether the joint must be removable and why.
  4. Group components into logical subassemblies/systems.
  5. Create a system map or annotated assembly sketch showing these relationships.

Use evidence: Keep sketches, measurements, observations, and revisions visible. Unit 1 is building one connected engineering documentation package.

What You Will Submit

Joining Method & System Map

  • Interface/joint analysis for assigned rocket connections.
  • Classification of joining methods as removable or permanent.
  • Rocket component → subassembly → system map.
  • At least one evidence-based explanation of why a joining method makes sense for its location.

Before submitting: Check that your work is labeled, readable, complete, and consistent with the technical conventions used in this lesson.
