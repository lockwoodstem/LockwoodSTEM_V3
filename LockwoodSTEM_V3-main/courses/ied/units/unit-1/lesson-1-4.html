Orthographic Projection & Multiview Sketching

Focus question: How do engineers translate a three-dimensional object into aligned front, top, and right-side views?

Class Flow

Work Through the Lesson

Day 1 — From 3D Object to 2D Views
  • Identify the front, top, and right viewing directions.
  • Practice mentally flattening a 3D object into each orthographic view.
  • Use projection/alignment to transfer widths, heights, and depths.
  • Analyze how the same feature appears differently in different views.
Day 2 — Complete Multiview Drawings
  • Lay out front, top, and right views in the class convention.
  • Add visible, hidden, and center lines as needed.
  • Use light projection lines to keep views aligned.
  • Complete an assigned multiview sketch and perform a three-view consistency check.
Core Ideas

What to Pay Attention To

Front view

Usually communicates the most characteristic shape and establishes width × height.

Top view

Communicates width × depth and must align horizontally with the front view.

Right-side view

Communicates depth × height and must align vertically with the front view.

Engineering Check

Stop and verify before moving on

A feature cannot move between views. If a hole is 20 mm from the left edge in the front view, the corresponding feature must remain aligned in every view that shares that dimension.

Your Task

Complete the Orthographic Projection & Multiview Worksheet

Use the isometric reference on each page to construct aligned Top, Front, and Right views. Treat the three views as one coordinated drawing and apply object, hidden, and center lines where appropriate.

  1. Choose the correct front orientation.
  2. Block in the overall view sizes with light construction lines.
  3. Project corresponding features into the top and right-side views.
  4. Apply object, hidden, and center lines only after the geometry is aligned.
  5. Compare all three views against the original object for consistency.

Use evidence: Keep sketches, measurements, observations, and revisions visible. Unit 1 is building one connected engineering documentation package.
