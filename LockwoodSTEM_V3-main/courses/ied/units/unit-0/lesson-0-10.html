IED Unit 0 • Lesson 0.10

Final Launch Pad Challenge & Design Review

Focus Question: How can engineers use performance evidence to defend a design?

View Lesson PlanWhat You SubmitBack to Unit 0
Mission Objective

Complete final qualification and stability competition testing, analyze performance, and communicate the engineering decisions behind the final launch pad.

Suggested pacing: 1 × 45-minute class period

Communicate
Mission Briefing

Today’s Plan

Every task advances the same Rocket Launch Pad Design Challenge.

Learning Targets

  • Demonstrate that the final launch pad satisfies the required constraints and qualification tests.
  • Collect final stability performance data.
  • Defend key design decisions and reflect on a future improvement using evidence.

Success Looks Like…

Your team completes final testing and can explain why the design performed as it did using specific evidence from the project.

Project connection: Your work today becomes evidence used in the next stage of the Engineering Design Process.
Final Qualification

Pass the required tests before entering the competition

DefineImagineSelectDevelopBuildTestImproveCommunicate

Dimensions

12 in × 12 in maximum footprint and 12 in maximum pad height.

Rocket Interface

Provided rocket installs, remains upright, and removes without damage.

Level Stability

60 seconds unsupported.

Required Tilt

10° in four directions for 5 seconds each.

Disturbance

Pass the standardized instructor-controlled disturbance test.

Competition

Maximum Tilt Challenge

Most Stable Launch Pad

After qualification, increase the test-platform angle in consistent increments until the design can no longer maintain the required stable condition. The design with the highest successful tilt angle wins the stability challenge.

Mass is not part of the competition.

Competition record

Record the highest successful tilt angle and the direction tested. If multiple teams achieve the same maximum angle, the smaller qualifying footprint is the tie breaker.

Design Review

Explain the engineering—not just the result

  1. What final design did your team build?
  2. What feature contributed most to stability?
  3. What evidence supports that conclusion?
  4. What did you change after initial testing?
  5. Did the change improve performance?
  6. If you built Version 2, what would you change next and why?
Engineering Design Process

You completed the full cycle

Define → Imagine → Select → Develop → Certify → Build → Test → Improve → Communicate

The point of Unit 0 is not only the launch pad. It is learning how engineers use a process, evidence, and documentation to move from a problem to a defensible solution.

Project Resources

Launch Pad Challenge Reference

Standard Team Materials

  • 1 × 12 in × 18 in foam-board sheet
  • 20 craft sticks
  • 10 straws
  • 48 in string
  • Hot glue as needed

Core Constraints

  • Use the provided rocket; do not modify it.
  • 12 in × 12 in maximum footprint.
  • 12 in maximum pad height, not including the rocket.
  • Rocket must be installable and removable.
  • Use only approved team-kit structural materials.
