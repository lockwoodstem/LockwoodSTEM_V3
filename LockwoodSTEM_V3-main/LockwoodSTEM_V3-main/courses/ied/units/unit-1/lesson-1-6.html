IED Unit 1 • Lesson 1.6 • 1–2 class periods

Rocket Reverse Engineering & Product Investigation

Focus question: How can engineers study an existing rocket assembly to understand what it does, how it is built, and where it might be improved?

Mission Briefing

What You Are Learning

Use this webpage, your engineering notebook, and the listed lesson resources to complete the work in order.

Learning Targets

  • Explain reverse engineering as the study of an existing product.
  • Document the as-received rocket before disassembly.
  • Identify visible components, likely functions, materials, interfaces, and joining methods.
  • Create predictions that can be checked during disassembly.

Class Flow

Work Through the Lesson

Part 1 — Before Disassembly
  • Photograph or sketch the assembled rocket from useful viewpoints.
  • Identify visible components and predict what each one does.
  • Record likely materials, joining methods, and subsystem relationships.
  • Write at least two questions or predictions you want to test during disassembly.
Part 2 — Initial Investigation
  • Begin careful disassembly only when directed.
  • Keep parts organized in sequence and avoid damaging reusable connections.
  • Compare actual connections/components with your predictions.
  • Record discoveries immediately in your notebook.
Core Ideas

What to Pay Attention To

As-received condition

Document the complete product before changing it so you always have a reference for reassembly.

Interface

An interface is where two components meet, attach, transfer force, or interact.

Evidence

Photos, measurements, sketches, observations, and part relationships are stronger than memory.

Engineering Check

Stop and verify before moving on

Do not take the rocket apart faster than you can document it. If you cannot explain where a part came from or how it was oriented, you moved too quickly.

Your Task

Create a Rocket Product Investigation Record

Treat the rocket like an unknown engineered product. Your first job is to understand and document it—not to redesign it yet.

  1. Record the assembled condition.
  2. Identify major visible components and possible subsystems.
  3. Predict functions, materials, and joining methods.
  4. Begin controlled disassembly and record what you discover.
  5. Update incorrect predictions using evidence.

Use evidence: Keep sketches, measurements, observations, and revisions visible. Unit 1 is building one connected engineering documentation package.

What You Will Submit

Rocket Investigation Record

  • As-received photos and/or sketches.
  • Component and subsystem observations.
  • At least two initial predictions plus what the investigation revealed.
  • Organized notes that can support later assembly documentation.

Before submitting: Check that your work is labeled, readable, complete, and consistent with the technical conventions used in this lesson.
